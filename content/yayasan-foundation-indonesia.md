A yayasan is Indonesia's foundation structure, and a large share of the schools, clinics and community projects foreigners engage with are built on one.

## What it is

A non-profit legal entity established for social, religious, humanitarian or educational purposes. It has founders, supervisors and managers rather than shareholders.

**It has no owners.** That is the defining feature and the source of every problem people create with it.

## The rule that matters

**A yayasan cannot distribute profit to its founders or managers.** Surplus must go back into the foundation's stated purpose.

People can be paid reasonable salaries for genuine work. What cannot happen is the foundation functioning as a business that quietly returns money to the people who set it up.

## Where foreigners use them legitimately

International and alternative schools. Clinics and health projects. Environmental and conservation work. Animal welfare. Community and education programmes.

Several of Bali's best-known institutions are yayasan.

## The trap

Using a yayasan to run what is really a commercial business, because it appears to avoid PT PMA capital requirements and foreign ownership restrictions.

It does not work, for the same reason a nominee arrangement does not work: the structure exists to achieve something the rules prohibit, and the documents that make your control explicit are the evidence of it.

**You do not own a yayasan.** If the relationship with the Indonesian founders or managers breaks down, there is no shareholding to fall back on.

## If you are involved with one

- Understand whether you are a founder, supervisor, manager or employee, and what each carries
- Confirm the stated purpose matches what actually happens
- Check whether any foreign staff hold the correct work authorisation
- Keep the finances genuinely separate from any related commercial business

## The property angle

A yayasan is an Indonesian legal entity and can hold certain land rights for its purposes. That makes it attractive to people looking for a workaround, which is precisely why it draws scrutiny.

Land held by a foundation is held **for the foundation's purpose**, not for the person who arranged it.

## Common questions

### What is a yayasan in Indonesia?
A non-profit foundation established for social, religious, humanitarian or educational purposes. It has no shareholders and cannot distribute profit.

### Can a foreigner set up a yayasan?
Foreigners can be involved, subject to conditions on roles and composition. Involvement is not ownership, because a yayasan has no owners.

### Can a yayasan make a profit?
It can generate surplus, but that surplus must serve the stated purpose and cannot be distributed to founders or managers.

### Can I use a yayasan to hold property?
It can hold certain rights for its purposes. Using one as a personal ownership workaround is the same problem as a nominee arrangement.

### Is a yayasan a good alternative to a PT PMA?
Only if your purpose is genuinely non-profit. For a commercial business it is the wrong structure and a known enforcement target.
