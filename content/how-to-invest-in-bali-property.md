Most people start by looking at villas. That is the wrong end. The sequence below is the whole job, in the order that protects you.

## Step 1: decide what you are actually buying

A Bali property is two assets. **Land** holds value and is genuinely scarce in well-zoned coastal locations. **The building** is a depreciating operating asset in a climate that destroys things, with a refurbishment cycle around every five years.

If a seller prices a ten-year-old villa as though the structure is new, they are pricing one asset as if it were the other.

## Step 2: pick the structure before the property

| Route | What you hold | Who it suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leasehold | A contract for a fixed term | Most buyers, smaller budgets, no company |
| Hak Pakai | A registered right of use in your own name | Anyone with a KITAS buying a home |
| HGB via PT PMA | A registered building right held by your company | Genuine businesses and developments |
| Nominee | Nothing enforceable | Nobody. It is prohibited |

This decision changes which properties you can even consider, so it comes first.

## Step 3: work out what the property can lawfully earn

Short-term letting is a licensed activity. On 22 July 2026 Bali closed the villa and homestay classifications to new foreign-owned company registration.

So before you value any rental income, establish whether a licence exists, whether it covers this building, and whether it transfers. If it does not, the income is not income. It is a hope.

## Step 4: verify, then pay

- Certificate verified at the land office, through a notary **you** appointed
- Seller is the registered owner, with spouse and heir consent where needed
- Zoning on the specific parcel permits your intended use
- Boundaries walked against the certificate
- Access rights registered, not customary
- PBG and SLF exist and describe the building as it stands

Only then does money move, traceably, to the registered owner, against written refund conditions.

## Step 5: model it honestly

Gross yield is not a return. Subtract vacancy, platform commission, management, cleaning, staff with BPJS and THR, utilities, repairs, insurance, tax and a sinking fund for the five-year refurbishment.

Then calculate break-even occupancy: annual fixed costs divided by what one booked night actually contributes. If break-even sits close to your projection, you have no margin for a bad year, and bad years happen.

## What the numbers look like

Transaction costs run around 7 to 8 percent. Construction runs USD 600 to 1,500 per square metre by specification. Furnishing a three-bedroom villa to rental standard is USD 25,000 to 45,000. Staff for that villa run IDR 84M to 100M a year once BPJS and THR are counted.

## The mistakes that cost the most

Buying the projection rather than the asset. Paying a deposit before verification. Using the seller's notary. Treating a nominee arrangement as normal because it is common. Assuming a licence transfers. Ignoring the remaining term on a leasehold.

## Common questions

### Can foreigners invest in Bali property?
Yes, through leasehold, Hak Pakai with residency, or HGB held by a PT PMA. Freehold in a foreign name is not available and no structure changes that.

### What is a realistic return on Bali property?
Net of vacancy, management, staff, maintenance, tax and a refurbishment provision, considerably less than the gross figures in sales material. Build the model yourself from twelve months of real operating statements.

### How much money do you need to start investing in Bali?
Entry varies enormously by area and title. Whatever the price, add roughly 7 to 8 percent for transaction costs, plus fit-out, plus a cash buffer for a slow first year.

### Is Bali property a good investment in 2026?
Well-zoned land in locations with real demand still works. Generic three-bedroom villas in oversupplied corridors, priced on optimistic projections, increasingly do not.

### What is the safest way to buy property in Bali?
The structure that is lawful for you, verified independently before payment, with every tranche tied to a check that has actually been completed.
