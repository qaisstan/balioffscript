Bali property investment works for a specific kind of buyer, and it disappoints almost everyone else. The difference is not luck or timing. It is whether you understand what you are actually buying, whether you run the numbers on net instead of gross, and whether you verify the paperwork before any money moves.

This is the whole picture in one place: what a foreigner can legally own, what it really returns, what it costs to buy and to hold, where to look, and how to do it in an order that protects you.

## The short answer

**Bali property is a good investment for you if all of these are true:**

- You can hold for **ten years or longer**. Bali is not a liquid market.
- The deal still works on **net** numbers, after every cost, at realistic occupancy.
- You understand that a foreigner **cannot own the land**, and you have priced the structure you can hold.
- The **title, zoning, permits and rental licence** are checked by your own notary before you pay.

**It is the wrong move if:** you need the money back within a few years, the numbers only work at 80 percent occupancy, someone is guaranteeing you a return, or the seller is pushing you to pay before checks are finished.

If you are still deciding whether to buy at all, the [honest buyer test](/ownership/should-you-buy-villa-bali/) and the [pros and cons, side by side](/ownership/pros-cons-buying-villa-bali/) are shorter reads.

## What foreigners can legally own

Everything else follows from this. **Hak Milik**, Indonesian freehold, is reserved for Indonesian citizens under the Basic Agrarian Law of 1960. No company, marriage or contract changes that. What you can hold instead:

| Route | What it is | Who it suits | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Leasehold** (Hak Sewa) | A notarised contract to use the land for a set term, usually 25 to 30 years | Most buyers, any visa, any budget | Not registered, not mortgageable, the value runs down every year |
| **Hak Pakai** | A registered right of use in your own name | Residents holding a KITAS or KITAP | Residency and a minimum property value are required |
| **HGB through a PT PMA** | A registered building right held by your own foreign-owned company | Businesses and developments | Capital rules, yearly compliance, and the activity must be open to foreign companies |
| **Nominee** | Land in an Indonesian person's name with side agreements | Nobody | Void in law, and criminal in Bali under Perda 4/2026 for both sides |

The full comparison, with the clauses that decide a lease's value, is in [leasehold vs freehold in Bali](/ownership/leasehold-vs-freehold-bali/). If you hold residency, read [Hak Pakai explained](/ownership/hak-pakai-explained/) first.

## Is Bali property a good investment in 2026?

It can be, and it frequently is not. The difference is arithmetic, not opinion.

**What makes it work:**

- **Long remaining term at a price the income supports.** Twenty-five or thirty years on a property that returns your capital within the term with a margin. Not exciting. It works.
- **Legal certainty.** Zoning that allows what you plan, permits issued for the building that exists, and a rental licence that covers this address.
- **Operation run as a business.** A good manager, a written agreement, a capital reserve, and quarterly comparisons against similar villas. The gap between a well-run and a badly run villa on the same street is bigger than the gap between two areas.
- **Honesty about the purpose.** A home you rent out when you are away is a different purchase from an income property you might visit. Most disappointment comes from buying one and using it as the other.

**What makes it fail:**

- A short lease at a prime-area price. The address does not extend the term.
- Off-plan bought on a projection, with permits still "in process".
- A green or yellow zoned plot with the licence "to be arranged".
- Any deal where the numbers only work at peak-season occupancy.
- A nominee arrangement of any kind.

**The market right now.** Demand is real but no longer grows on its own. Statistics Indonesia counted **3,203,156 foreign arrivals in Bali from January to June 2026**, 2.42 percent fewer than the first half of 2025, with Australia still the largest source at 802,837 visits. At the same time, supply of the standard pool villa grew fast, especially in Canggu. The years when any villa anywhere filled itself are over. Differentiated, well-run, properly licensed properties still perform. Generic ones compete on price. See [the Bali property market in 2026](/rental/bali-property-market-2026/) and [whether Canggu is oversupplied](/rental/is-canggu-oversupplied/).

My view as an adviser: the market is neither the opportunity it is sold as nor the trap it is sometimes described as. It is an ordinary market with unusual legal mechanics. Buyers who do the work do well.

## What returns are realistic

The short version, with the full arithmetic in [Bali villa rental yield and ROI](/rental/villa-rental-licensing-roi/):

| | Typical figure |
| --- | --- |
| Island-wide occupancy | around 64 to 66 percent |
| Island-wide average nightly rate | around USD 90 to 95 |
| Running costs | 40 to 50 percent of gross on a short-term rental |
| Advertised gross yield | usually 8 to 15 percent |
| Net yield, average stock | 4 to 6 percent |
| Net yield, well bought and well run | 7 to 9 percent |
| Net yield, the best villas in the best spots, run excellently | up to the low teens |

Then comes the number almost nobody shows you: **what the lease costs you each year.** A USD 300,000 villa on a 25-year lease uses up about USD 12,000 of its value every year, because at the end the land and building [go back to the owner](/ownership/land-rights-expiry-bali/). On that villa, a "7 percent net" is closer to 3 percent once the lease is counted. A strong, priced [extension clause](/ownership/leasehold-extension-clause/) changes this. A weak one does not.

Put any seller's numbers into the [ROI calculator](/calculator/) before you trust them. If a projection cannot survive a spreadsheet, it was built for selling, not for deciding.

## Capital growth: who actually gets it

Land in the developed corridors has gone up a lot over the last decade. Buyers of Pererenan or Seseh land five years ago did very well.

Two things to understand:

- **On a lease, you do not own the land, so you do not capture the land's growth.** You own a shrinking number of years. Your lease can still sell for more than you paid if the area rises faster than your term runs down, but that is a race against the clock, not ownership of appreciation.
- **The corridors that grew are now priced as if they will keep growing.** Today's buyer pays for the growth story rather than discovering it.

If capital growth is your main goal, that points to land in an earlier corridor under a structure that captures it, held long. See [land banking in Bali](/rental/land-banking-bali/) and [Bali land prices, area by area](/areas/bali-land-prices/).

## What it costs to buy and to hold

**On top of the price, when you buy:**

- Roughly **7 percent** on a titled transfer: about 5 percent BPHTB transfer tax, 1 to 2.5 percent notary and PPAT, plus due diligence and translation. A leasehold avoids the BPHTB element. See [the hidden costs of buying](/ownership/bali-hidden-costs-buying/).
- **Due diligence:** USD 1,500 to 8,000 depending on the deal. It is the line buyers cut and the one they regret.
- **Furniture and fit-out:** USD 25,000 to 50,000 for a three-bedroom to a standard that rents.

**Every year you hold it:** management, platform commission, the 10 percent regional accommodation tax, staff, utilities, maintenance, insurance, a capital reserve and income tax. On a rented villa that is 40 to 50 percent of gross before income tax. The full list is in [the cost of owning a villa in Bali](/rental/cost-of-owning-villa-bali/).

## How to invest in Bali property, step by step

**1. Decide what the property is for.** A home, an income property and a development each point to a different structure. Choosing the property before the structure is the most expensive common mistake.

**2. Choose the structure.** Hak Pakai if you hold residency and it is your home. A long, well-drafted leasehold for most other buyers. HGB through a PT PMA only if it is genuinely a business and the activity is still open to foreign companies: Bali closed villa and homestay classifications to **new** foreign-owned companies on 22 July 2026. See [the KBLI closure](/company/pt-pma-kbli-closure-bali/).

**3. Run the numbers before you view anything.** Realistic nightly rate for that street, times realistic occupancy, minus every cost, minus the yearly cost of the lease. Compare that against the price plus 7 percent plus fit-out.

**4. Pick the area for the strategy, not for the holiday you had.** A place that made a great two-week trip and a place that fills a booking calendar are often different places.

**5. Verify before you pay.** Your own notary, not the seller's. The certificate checked at the land office. The person signing matches the certificate, with spousal consent if married. Zoning checked for the exact plot. PBG and SLF matched to the building that exists. The extension clause read, and whether it is guaranteed or only a right of first refusal. One to three weeks for a clean title. Anyone pressuring you inside that window has told you what you need to know. See [the buying process, step by step](/ownership/buying-process-bali-step-by-step/) and [the due diligence timeline](/ownership/bali-due-diligence-timeline/).

**6. Plan the exit on the day you buy.** A USD 250,000 villa has hundreds of possible buyers. A USD 900,000 villa has a dozen, and they are not all looking this year. Above USD 750,000, twelve to twenty-four months to sell is normal, and on a lease the term keeps running while you wait. See [exit strategy](/rental/bali-property-exit-strategy/).

## The risks, and how to handle each

| Risk | What it looks like | How you handle it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zoning | On 21 July 2025, 48 buildings at Bingin, Uluwatu were demolished. They stood on state land in a green zone, and the Governor said none had permits | Check the zoning for the exact plot with the regency, not the agent. See [zoning colours](/building/bali-zoning-colours/) |
| Permits | A villa with no PBG or SLF, or permits for a different building | Match PBG and SLF to what is standing. See [PBG and SLF](/building/pbg-slf-building-permits/) |
| Rental licence | Since the 31 March 2026 deadline under Permenpar No. 6/2025, listings without a verified business number with the right classification get pulled | Treat the licence as part of the price. No licence, no rental income in your numbers |
| Nominee | Land "owned" through an Indonesian name | Walk away. See [why nominee deals fail](/ownership/nominee-structure-bali/) |
| Off-plan | Payments to a developer before permits exist | Pay against milestones, with permits in hand. See [off-plan risk](/building/off-plan-villa-bali-risk/) |
| Oversupply | Many near-identical villas on one street | Buy something different, run it well, price it honestly |
| Currency | Your rupiah return is not your return in AUD, GBP or EUR | See [currency risk](/tax/currency-risk-bali-property/) |
| Term decay | The lease runs down every year | Price it in, and buy the longest term you can |
| Liquidity | Slow resale at the top of the market | Hold long, and buy at a price that survives a slow, discounted exit |

## Taxes, in short

Transfer tax (BPHTB) of about 5 percent on titled transfers. A 10 percent regional accommodation tax on stays. Rental income tax, which for a resident individual is a 10 percent final tax. A final tax when you sell. ## Common questions

### Is Bali property a good investment in 2026?
For buyers who hold ten years or longer, buy on net rather than gross yields, and verify title, zoning, permits and licence before paying, yes. For buyers relying on a guaranteed return or a quick resale, no.

### Can foreigners invest in Bali property?
Yes, through a leasehold, Hak Pakai with residency, or HGB held by a foreign-owned company (PT PMA). Foreigners cannot hold Hak Milik, Indonesian freehold, under any structure, and nominee arrangements are void and now criminal in Bali.

### What return can you expect from a Bali villa?
Net of every cost, 4 to 6 percent on average stock and 7 to 9 percent on a well-bought, well-run villa, with the best properties reaching the low teens. On a leasehold, subtract the yearly cost of the term as well. Advertised figures of 12 to 18 percent are gross.

### How much money do you need to invest in Bali property?
Under USD 100,000 buys land, a short lease or a small off-plan unit. A finished villa with a long term in a good area usually starts in the USD 250,000 to 500,000 bracket. Add about 7 percent in transaction costs and USD 25,000 to 50,000 for fit-out.

### What is the best area to invest in Bali?
Pererenan and Cemagi for income at a mid-range budget, Berawa or Seminyak for reliability and resale, the Bukit for the highest nightly rates, and Pecatu, Ungasan or Seseh for land and building.

### Is buying a villa in Bali worth it?
It is worth it for buyers who want a usable asset in a place they like, with a decent income, who verify properly and run it seriously. It is not worth it as a hands-off investment expected to return 18 percent.

### Does Bali property go up in value?
Land in the developed corridors has risen a lot. On a leasehold you do not own the land, so you do not capture that growth directly: you own a shrinking number of years. Capital growth is mainly a landowner's return.

### What is the biggest risk when investing in Bali?
Paying for something that cannot legally do what you bought it for: a weak lease, a zone where nightly rental is not allowed, or a missing licence or permit. All of these can be checked before you pay.

### How do I start investing in Bali property?
Decide what the property is for, choose the structure that fits, run the numbers on net, pick the area for the strategy, have your own notary verify everything, and plan the exit before you buy.

### Is Bali property passive income?
Rarely. Even with a manager, a rental villa needs decisions, reviews and capital spending. See [is Bali property really passive income](/rental/passive-income-bali-property/).
