Solar in Bali looks like an obvious win — equatorial sun, expensive electricity, a villa with a large roof — and the economics are better than most people expect and worse than the installers' projections.

## Why the case is strong here

**Irradiance.** Bali sits close to the equator with consistent sun and relatively short seasonal variation. Generation is predictable in a way it is not at higher latitudes.

**Electricity is expensive relative to local costs**, and a villa with multiple aircon units, a pool pump and a water pump uses a lot of it. Pool pumps in particular run during daylight hours, which aligns almost perfectly with solar generation.

**Load matches generation.** Daytime aircon, pool filtration and water pumping all happen when the sun is up. This is the single best argument for solar on a rental villa and it is why the payback is better here than on a house that is empty all day.

## Why it is not as good as the pitch

**Export is limited.** PLN's arrangements for rooftop solar and net metering have changed, and the ability to export surplus to the grid for credit is considerably more constrained than it was.

That matters enormously. A system sized to cover your peak load generates surplus at midday, and if you cannot export it for meaningful credit, that generation is simply wasted unless you store it.

**Batteries change the economics badly.** Storage makes surplus useful and roughly doubles the system cost while adding a component with a finite life. On most villa projects the arithmetic does not support batteries unless you are off-grid or supply is genuinely unreliable.

**Rain and cloud.** The wet season from roughly November to March reduces output meaningfully. Annual generation is not the dry season figure extrapolated across twelve months, and some projections quietly do exactly that.

**Humidity, salt and dust.** Panels degrade and soil faster in this climate, particularly near the coast. Cleaning is a recurring maintenance item rather than optional, and dirty panels lose output quickly.

**Roof suitability.** Traditional Balinese roofs, thatch, complex pitches and heavy shading from mature planting all reduce usable area. The roof that looks large frequently is not.

## How to size it properly

**Size to your daytime base load**, not to your total consumption and not to the roof area.

The right system covers what you actually use while the sun is shining — aircon, pool pump, water pump, lighting, kitchen — and does not generate large surpluses you cannot use or export.

Get twelve months of **actual PLN bills** and look at consumption, not just cost. Size against that. An installer sizing against your roof rather than your load is selling panels, not savings.

## Realistic payback

For a well-sized grid-connected system on a villa with genuine daytime load, payback in the range of five to eight years is a defensible expectation, with panels lasting considerably longer than that.

Installer projections of three to four years generally assume full export credit, dry-season generation across the year, no soiling losses and no maintenance. Discount them.

With batteries, payback extends substantially and frequently does not work at all on a grid-connected property.

## Where it genuinely makes sense

**Villas with high daytime load** — multiple aircon units, a large pool, water pumping.

**Properties in areas with unreliable supply**, where solar plus a modest battery is partly a resilience purchase rather than purely a financial one.

**Off-grid or poorly serviced land**, where the comparison is against a generator rather than against PLN, and solar wins easily.

**New builds**, where the roof can be designed for it and the cabling runs are installed during construction rather than retrofitted.

## The guest angle

It is worth saying that a solar installation has some marketing value with the segment of guests who care, and Bali attracts a fair number of them.

It is not worth overstating. Guests book on photographs, location, pool and reviews. Solar is a nice line in the listing, not a rate driver.

## What to check before committing

Twelve months of actual consumption data.

The **current PLN position on rooftop solar and export**, confirmed directly rather than from an installer.

Usable roof area after shading, pitch and structure.

Installer track record on completed Bali systems, with references you found yourself.

Warranty terms on panels, inverter and installation, and who honours them.

Maintenance and cleaning schedule and cost.

## Common questions

### Is solar worth it for a Bali villa?
For a villa with genuine daytime load from aircon, a pool pump and water pumping, payback in five to eight years is realistic on a well-sized grid-connected system.

### Can I sell solar power back to the grid in Indonesia?
Export arrangements have been constrained, which significantly affects the economics of a system sized above your daytime load. Confirm the current PLN position directly.

### Do I need batteries for solar in Bali?
On a grid-connected property with good supply, usually not — batteries roughly double the cost and extend payback substantially. They make sense off-grid or where supply is unreliable.

### How much does rain affect solar in Bali?
The wet season from roughly November to March reduces output meaningfully, so annual generation is well below a dry season figure extrapolated across the year.

### How should I size a solar system for a villa?
Against twelve months of actual consumption data, covering your daytime base load. An installer sizing against roof area rather than load is selling panels rather than savings.
