Balinese food is distinct from the rest of Indonesian cuisine: more complex spice pastes, more pork, and a ceremonial tradition that produces dishes you will not find in Java or Sumatra.

## The dishes to look for

**Babi guling.** Suckling pig, spit-roasted with a turmeric and spice paste, served with crackling, blood sausage, lawar and rice. The signature Balinese dish and, for many visitors, the best thing they eat here. Traditionally a lunchtime food; the famous places sell out by early afternoon.

**Bebek betutu.** Duck rubbed with spice paste, wrapped in banana leaf and slow-cooked for hours until it falls apart. Often needs ordering in advance.

**Ayam betutu**, the chicken version, more widely available.

**Sate lilit.** Minced fish or chicken with coconut, lime leaf and spice, wrapped around a lemongrass stick and grilled. Balinese satay, and better than the skewered version.

**Lawar.** A mix of vegetables, coconut, minced meat and heavy spice. The red version traditionally contains fresh blood. Ask if that matters to you.

**Nasi campur.** Not a dish so much as a format: rice with small portions of half a dozen things, chosen by the cook or by you at the counter. The cheapest good meal in Indonesia, and the one you should eat most often.

**Tipat cantok**, rice cakes with vegetables and peanut sauce. **Jukut ares**, banana stem soup. **Rujak**, fruit with chilli and palm sugar.

## The Indonesian staples

**Nasi goreng** and **mie goreng**, fried rice and noodles, everywhere and usually good. **Gado-gado**, vegetables with peanut sauce. **Soto ayam**, chicken soup. **Martabak**, savoury stuffed pancake or its sweet chocolate-and-peanut cousin, sold from street carts at night.

## Where to eat

**Warungs.** Small family-run places, often with a counter of dishes you point at. A full meal is 25,000 to 50,000 rupiah. The best food in Bali at the best price is here, and the rule is simple: eat where it is busy, particularly at lunchtime, when turnover is highest.

**Night markets.** Gianyar's night market is the best known, Sanur and Denpasar have good ones, and Canggu's is aimed at visitors but works.

**Restaurants.** Seminyak and Ubud have the depth: serious Indonesian restaurants, Japanese, Italian, and a handful of tasting-menu rooms that hold their own internationally.

**Beach clubs.** Fine, expensive, and not where the cooking is.

## What things cost

- Warung meal: 25,000 to 50,000 rupiah.
- Cafe brunch in Canggu: 120,000 to 250,000.
- Mid-range restaurant dinner: 200,000 to 400,000 per person.
- Fine dining with wine: 700,000 to 1,500,000 per person, and the wine is most of it.

Imported alcohol carries heavy duty. Local beer and arak-based cocktails are cheap; imported wine and spirits are not.

## Food safety, briefly

Busy warungs cooking to order are safer than quiet buffets. Drink bottled or filtered water. Commercial tube ice is fine. See [Bali belly](/travel/bali-belly-health/) for the full version.

## Coffee

Indonesia grows excellent coffee, and Bali's specialty scene is genuinely good. **Kopi tubruk**, coarse grounds with hot water poured over, is the traditional local version and worth trying once.

**Luwak coffee** is the one sold to tourists at plantation stops. Much of the production involves caged civets, and the taste does not justify the price or the practice. Skip it.

## Dietary requirements

Bali is the easiest place in Indonesia to be vegetarian or vegan: Ubud and Canggu are full of dedicated places. Note that many Indonesian dishes use shrimp paste, **terasi**, and chicken stock, so "no meat" needs to be specific.

Halal food is widely available; pork is central to Balinese Hindu cooking, so check when it matters.

## Common questions

### What is the national dish of Bali?
Babi guling, spit-roasted suckling pig with spice paste, is the signature Balinese dish. Nasi campur is the everyday staple.

### Is street food safe in Bali?
At busy places cooking to order, generally yes. Turnover matters far more than whether it is a street stall or a restaurant.

### How much does a meal cost in Bali?
25,000 to 50,000 rupiah at a warung, 120,000 to 250,000 for a cafe brunch, 200,000 to 400,000 for a mid-range dinner.

### Is Bali good for vegetarians?
Very. Ubud and Canggu have a large concentration of vegetarian and vegan restaurants, and most Indonesian menus have meat-free options.

### Should I try luwak coffee in Bali?
Most tourist luwak production involves caged civets and the coffee is not worth the price. Try Indonesian specialty coffee instead.
