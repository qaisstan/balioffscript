This is the most consequential immigration question a mixed-nationality family here faces, and it has a hard deadline attached.

## Limited dual citizenship

Indonesia does not generally permit dual nationality for adults. It makes an exception for **children of mixed marriages**, who may hold both nationalities until a set age.

At that point the child must **choose one**. The choice is not automatic and it is not indefinitely postponable.

## Why families miss it

The deadline arrives years after the paperwork was done, when the family has stopped thinking about it. Parents assume the status simply continues, or that a decision can be made later.

Missing the window can mean losing the Indonesian nationality by default, with consequences for land ownership, residence and the child's future options here.

**If you have a child of a mixed marriage, find out the exact deadline that applies to them and diarise it.** This is not something to discover late.

## Registration comes first

The dual status generally depends on the child being properly registered, which means birth registration, reporting where the birth occurred abroad, and the supporting documents legalised and translated.

A child who was never registered may not have the status the parents assume they have.

## If the child is not an Indonesian citizen

A foreign child of an Indonesian parent can hold a dependent permit sponsored by that parent. It grants residence, not the right to work, and it follows the sponsor's status.

## The property consequence

An Indonesian citizen child can hold **Hak Milik**, freehold. A foreign child cannot, and inheriting a title they cannot lawfully hold triggers a disposal requirement within a set period.

So the citizenship choice is also a property decision, and it interacts with whatever structure the family holds land through. Where a marriage has no prenuptial or postnuptial agreement separating assets, that compounds.

Take advice on immigration and property together, because separately they miss the interaction.

## Common questions

### Can a child have Indonesian and foreign citizenship?
Children of mixed marriages may hold both until a set age, at which point they must choose one.

### What happens if we miss the choice deadline?
The Indonesian nationality can be lost by default, affecting land ownership, residence and future options here.

### Can my foreign child live in Indonesia with me?
Yes, on a dependent permit sponsored by the Indonesian or permit-holding parent. It does not grant the right to work.

### Can my child inherit our Bali property?
An Indonesian citizen child can hold freehold. A foreign child inheriting a title they cannot hold faces a disposal requirement.

### What should we do first?
Confirm the child is properly registered, then find and diarise the exact deadline for the citizenship choice.
