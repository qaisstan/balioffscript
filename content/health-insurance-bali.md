Bali has decent private hospitals and a hard ceiling on what they can handle. Understanding where that ceiling sits is what determines the cover you need.

## What is available locally

**Private hospitals in the south** — Denpasar, Kuta, Nusa Dua — handle routine and moderately serious care competently. Consultations, imaging, minor surgery, maternity, and most of what a family needs day to day.

**BIMC and Siloam** are the names most foreign residents use, with international-standard facilities and English-speaking staff.

**Outside the south**, facilities thin rapidly. The east, north, highlands and islands have limited capacity, and serious cases travel.

**BPJS Kesehatan**, the national health insurance, covers residents and is mandatory for employees. It provides genuine coverage within the public system and is not what most foreign residents rely on for serious care.

## Where the ceiling is

**Major trauma, complex cardiac, serious neurological cases, advanced oncology and anything requiring specialist intensive care** generally get evacuated — to Singapore, or to Australia.

That is not a criticism of Bali's hospitals. It is the reality of an island with a finite specialist base, and the medical staff will tell you the same thing.

**Medical evacuation to Singapore costs tens of thousands of dollars and can exceed that substantially.** An air ambulance with a medical team is an expensive aircraft.

This is the single most important number in this article. It is why evacuation cover is not optional.

## What to look for in a policy

**Medical evacuation and repatriation**, with a stated limit that is realistic. A policy with a low evacuation sub-limit is a policy that covers part of the flight.

**Inpatient and outpatient cover** at the facilities you would actually use.

**Direct billing arrangements** with local hospitals, so you are not paying tens of thousands up front and claiming afterwards.

**Pre-existing condition treatment**, which varies enormously and is where most claims fail.

**Maternity**, with its waiting period, if relevant.

**Renewal terms**, particularly whether cover continues after a claim and whether premiums reprice individually.

## The exclusions that catch people

**Motorcycle accidents.** This is the big one in Bali.

Many travel and health policies exclude motorcycle accidents entirely, or exclude engine sizes above a threshold, or require that you held a **valid licence for that vehicle in the country where you were riding**.

Bali's road injury rate is high and foreign visitors are disproportionately represented. A foreigner riding a scooter without a motorcycle endorsement on their licence, who has a serious accident, may find the claim declined — and then faces the evacuation bill personally.

**Get the licence.** An international driving permit with a motorcycle endorsement obtained before you travel, or an Indonesian SIM C if you hold a KITAS. Then read your policy's motorcycle clause specifically.

**Adventure activities.** Surfing, diving, climbing. Diving in particular frequently requires a specific extension, and Bali is a diving destination.

**Alcohol.** Most policies exclude incidents where alcohol was a factor.

**Pre-existing conditions**, undeclared.

**Dengue and tropical illness**, generally covered, but check.

## Travel insurance versus expatriate health cover

**Travel insurance** is designed for trips. It covers emergencies away from home, typically with duration limits and without routine care. Adequate for a holiday, inadequate for living here.

**International health insurance** for expatriates covers you where you live, includes routine care, and is designed for long-term residence. More expensive and the right product if Bali is home.

The gap between the two catches people who moved here on a long holiday mindset and kept renewing travel policies.

## For a property owner specifically

If you take paying guests, **public liability** is a separate and important cover. Someone injured at your pool is an exposure on your property policy, not your health policy.

Check your villa policy covers commercial letting. A property insured as a private residence while taking guests is the most common reason Bali property claims fail.

## Practical points

**Keep a card or document** showing your policy and emergency number accessible, not filed at home.

**Know which hospital you would go to**, before you need to.

**Tell someone.** If you live alone, somebody should know your policy details and who to call.

**Dental and optical** are generally inexpensive here and often worth paying for directly rather than insuring.

## Common questions

### Is healthcare good in Bali?
Private hospitals in the south handle routine and moderately serious care competently. Major trauma, complex cardiac, serious neurological and advanced oncology cases are generally evacuated.

### Do I need medical evacuation insurance in Bali?
Yes. Evacuation to Singapore costs tens of thousands of dollars and can exceed that substantially, and serious cases do leave the island.

### Does my insurance cover scooter accidents in Bali?
Frequently not, unless you held a valid licence for that vehicle in the country where you were riding. Many policies exclude motorcycles entirely or above an engine size threshold.

### Is travel insurance enough for living in Bali?
No. Travel policies are designed for trips with duration limits and no routine care. International health insurance for expatriates is the right product for residence.

### Does BPJS cover foreigners in Indonesia?
BPJS Kesehatan covers residents and is mandatory for employees, providing genuine coverage within the public system. Most foreign residents carry private cover for serious care.
