Buying a home in Bali and letting it while you are away is the most common plan foreign buyers arrive with. It works, and two things about it are consistently underestimated.

## The licensing question

**Letting nightly to paying guests is commercial accommodation**, regardless of the fact that you live there the rest of the year.

That requires zoning that permits it, an operating entity with the correct KBLI classification, PBG and SLF, and operational licensing — noting Bali closed new foreign-owned villa and homestay registration to PMA companies on **22 July 2026**.

Plenty of owner-occupiers let nightly without all of that. The practical consequence is rarely enforcement; it is that the property becomes **unsaleable to any buyer doing proper due diligence**, and a tax liability accumulates.

**Long-term letting to a single tenant sits differently** and does not engage the accommodation framework in the same way, which for some owner-occupiers resolves the question entirely.

Confirm your specific position rather than assuming either direction.

## The arithmetic problem

**The weeks you use are the weeks worth the most.**

If you take six weeks in July and August, that is peak — rates forty to seventy percent above base, and a period when a decent property fills at almost any price.

Six peak weeks in a four-bedroom is a very large number, and it is income you did not earn. An owner-occupier who models a full-year yield and then takes the best six weeks has overstated their return substantially.

**Model it honestly.** Take your realistic annual net, subtract the weeks you will actually use at their seasonal rate, and look at what remains. Then decide whether the use is worth it — which it very often is, and it should be a conscious trade rather than an accident.

**If you can be flexible, take shoulder season.** May, June, September and early October are excellent in Bali and cost you far less income than August does.

## The practical friction

**Personal belongings.** A home has your things in it. A let property cannot, beyond a locked owner's storeroom. Decide what that room holds and accept that everything else is exposed to guests.

**Wear.** Guests treat a property differently from an owner. Your furniture ages faster, your finishes mark, and things break.

**Standards.** A home cleaned to your standard is not a villa cleaned to a guest's. Housekeeping has to run to a commercial schedule with linen sets, turnover cleans and mould management, not a weekly tidy.

**Coordination.** Your dates block the calendar, and blocking peak weeks at short notice loses bookings that were already made or would have been.

**Photography.** A lived-in home photographs differently from a styled villa. Depersonalise for the shoot.

## How to make it work

**Decide which it primarily is.** A home that earns, or an investment you use. The answer changes what you buy, where, how you furnish it and how you price it.

**Fix your dates early**, a year ahead, and block them. Late blocking costs bookings.

**Use a manager**, even for limited letting. Remote coordination of guests around your own occupancy is more work than it sounds.

**Lock a storeroom** and treat everything outside it as a rental property.

**Furnish for guests**, not for yourself. Durable, replaceable, uncluttered, photographing well. You can keep the things you love in the storeroom.

**Budget replacement** at ten to fifteen percent of fit-out value annually, because guest use ages a property faster than your own does.

## The alternative that suits many people

**Long-term letting while you are away**, if your absence is a coherent block rather than scattered.

Lower gross at forty to sixty percent of nightly rates, far lower cost and workload, no seasonality, no guest management, and a different licensing position.

For an owner who is away for eight or nine months and wants the property occupied and earning with minimal attention, this is frequently the better answer than a nightly operation managed around their own visits.

## Common questions

### Can I rent out my Bali home when I am not there?
Yes, and nightly letting to paying guests is commercial accommodation requiring appropriate zoning, classification and licensing regardless of your own occupancy.

### Does using the villa myself reduce my return?
Substantially, if you take peak weeks. Six weeks in July and August is a very large amount of forgone income, and it should be modelled rather than ignored.

### How should I furnish a home I also let?
For guests — durable, uncluttered and photographing well — with a locked owner's storeroom for personal belongings, which are otherwise exposed.

### Is long-term letting better for an owner-occupier?
If your absence is a coherent block, frequently yes. Lower gross but far lower workload, no seasonality, and a different licensing position.

### When should I block my own dates?
A year ahead. Late blocking costs bookings that were already made or would have been, particularly in peak season.
