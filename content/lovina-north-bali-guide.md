North Bali faces the Java Sea rather than the Indian Ocean, which changes everything: no swell, no rips, calm flat water and black volcanic sand. It is three to four hours from the airport, which is why almost nobody goes.

## Lovina

A string of villages along the coast west of Singaraja, with a quiet beachfront, a low-key restaurant strip and prices that belong to a different decade.

**The dolphin boats** are the famous activity: outriggers leaving at dawn to find pods offshore. It is genuinely lovely when the fleet is small and uncomfortable when twenty boats chase one pod. If you go, choose an operator that keeps distance, and go on a weekday.

**The beach** is black sand, calm and shallow, with no surf. Fine for swimming, not a postcard.

**Singaraja**, the old Dutch colonial capital, with a harbour, a library of lontar manuscripts and an atmosphere entirely unlike the south.

## Around the north

**Banjar hot springs**, stone pools of warm sulphurous water in a garden, busy at weekends and pleasant early.

**Brahmavihara-Arama**, a Buddhist monastery above Banjar with a miniature Borobudur and views over the coast.

**Gitgit, Sekumpul and Aling-Aling waterfalls**, in the hills behind. Sekumpul is generally rated the most spectacular in Bali and the descent is genuinely steep.

**Air Sanih**, natural spring-fed pools by the sea east of Singaraja.

**The north coast temples**, including Pura Beji at Sangsit, carved in a style that exists nowhere else on the island.

## Pemuteran and Menjangan

Further west, and the reason many people come north at all.

**Menjangan Island**, part of West Bali National Park, has **the best snorkelling and diving in Bali**: a coral wall in clear, calm water, protected as a marine park. Boats leave from Labuan Lalang.

**Pemuteran** itself has an accessible house reef, a long-running coral restoration project using Biorock structures, and a quiet, low-key village atmosphere with good small hotels.

**West Bali National Park**, with birdwatching including the critically endangered Bali starling, and trails through dry monsoon forest that look nothing like the rest of the island.

## Getting there

- From the airport: three to four hours.
- From Ubud: two and a half to three hours, over the mountains through Bedugul.
- From Canggu: three to three and a half hours.
- From Amed, along the east and north coast: two and a half hours, and a beautiful drive.

The classic route is a loop: Ubud, Munduk, Lovina or Pemuteran, then back down the west coast or across to Amed.

## Where to stay

Lovina for budget and dolphin boats. **Pemuteran** for diving, quiet and the best small hotels on this coast. A handful of serious resorts sit near Menjangan, priced accordingly.

## Practicalities

**Weather:** the north is drier and hotter than the south, particularly in the far northwest. Wet season is shorter here.

**Food:** simple, cheap, and limited outside hotel restaurants.

**Getting around:** a scooter or a driver. Distances between sights are significant.

**Money:** ATMs in Lovina and Singaraja, few elsewhere. Carry cash.

## Who it suits

Divers and snorkellers heading for Menjangan, anyone doing a full island loop, travellers who want quiet and low prices, and families who want calm water without resort prices. It does not suit a short trip: the drive is too long to justify fewer than two nights.

## Common questions

### Is Lovina worth visiting?
As part of a longer loop, yes: calm water, low prices and the waterfalls and hot springs behind it. As a standalone destination from the south, the drive is hard to justify.

### Are the Lovina dolphin tours ethical?
It depends entirely on the operator and the crowd. Boats that keep distance and go on quiet days are fine; a fleet chasing one pod is not.

### Where is the best snorkelling in Bali?
Menjangan Island in the northwest, inside West Bali National Park, with Pemuteran's house reef a close second.

### How long does it take to drive to Lovina from Ubud?
Two and a half to three hours over the mountains via Bedugul, with waterfall stops available on the way.

### Is north Bali good for swimming?
Yes. It faces the Java Sea, so there is no surf and no rip currents, and the water is calm and shallow.
