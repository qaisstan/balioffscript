Every answer you find online is somebody describing their own life and calling it a benchmark. A single person in a Canggu guesthouse and a family of four in Umalas are not living in the same country, financially.

So here is the range, and then what actually moves it.

## What Bali costs per month in 2026

| Lifestyle | Monthly, USD |
| --- | --- |
| Budget: room, scooter, local food | 600 to 900 |
| Mid-range: apartment or small villa | 1,100 to 1,800 |
| Comfortable single expat | 1,800 to 2,500 |
| Couple sharing | 1,600 to 2,500 |
| Luxury | 2,500 and up |
| Family with international school | 4,000 to 6,500 |

Most remote workers land between 900 and 1,500 a month.

Against home, Bali runs roughly 50 to 65 percent cheaper than Western Europe, and 60 to 70 percent cheaper than Australia or the United States for a comparable standard of living.

That gap is real. It is also narrower than it was, and considerably narrower in Canggu and Seminyak than anywhere else on the island.

## The four things that decide your number

**Where you live.** Canggu and Seminyak carry a premium that has nothing to do with quality. The same house in Sanur or Ubud costs meaningfully less. Moving twenty minutes changes your rent more than any other single decision.

**How you rent.** Yearly contracts, paid up front, are dramatically cheaper per month than rolling monthly deals. That upfront lump is the biggest gap between the budget people plan and the one they live. A one-bedroom villa in Canggu starts around IDR 17.5 million a month, and Sanur is consistently cheaper for the same space.

**Whether you have children.** School fees are the largest line in any family budget here by a wide margin, and they are the reason the family row in that table sits so far above the others.

**How much you live like a tourist.** Beach clubs, daily cafes and imported groceries will double a budget quietly. Local markets and warungs will halve it. Most people land somewhere in between and are surprised by which side.

## Where the money actually goes

**Rent** is the largest fixed cost for anyone without children. Expect to pay a full year up front for the better rates.

**Electricity** surprises people. Air conditioning across several bedrooms, a pool pump and a water heater add up quickly, and it is almost never included in a rental quote.

**Food** splits into two economies. A warung meal and a Canggu brunch differ by a factor of ten, and your monthly total depends almost entirely on which one you default to.

**Transport.** A scooter is genuinely cheap. A car with a driver is not, and rainy season makes you want one.

**Visas.** Not free, and they recur. Budget for renewals and agent fees rather than treating them as a one-off.

**Healthcare.** Routine care is affordable. Serious care is not, and the gap is enormous.

## What people underestimate

**International school fees.** Budget USD 8,000 to 28,000 per child per year all-in, once registration, transport, uniform and lunch are added to tuition.

**Health insurance with evacuation cover.** Medical evacuation to Singapore runs USD 55,000 to 100,000. Without cover for that specific line, one bad day rewrites your finances.

**Flights home.** Two or three a year, for a family, is a serious annual number that no cost-of-living article includes.

**Rainy season.** Roughly November to March. Damp, mould, repairs, and more money spent indoors than you planned.

**The setup cost.** Furnishing, deposits, a scooter, initial visa work. The first two months always cost more than the steady state.

## What the number does not include

Buying property gives you no right to live here. Residence is a separate system with its own cost. The self-funded route requires a deposit of roughly USD 130,000 held in an Indonesian bank, and there are several other routes depending on what you will actually be doing.

Cross 183 days in a calendar year and you become an Indonesian tax resident, whatever your visa says. That has consequences for foreign income and pensions, and it is worth understanding before you cross the line rather than after.

## The honest summary

Bali is cheaper than home for the same standard of living. It is not cheap in the way it was ten years ago, and Canggu in particular now prices close to a mid-sized Western city for anything aimed at foreigners.

The single best thing you can do is come for a long stay, including a rainy season, and track what you actually spend. That number is worth more than every article on this subject, including this one.

## Common questions

### How much do you need per month to live in Bali?
A single person lives comfortably on USD 1,800 to 2,500 a month, covering a private villa or apartment, eating out regularly, a scooter and a social life. A budget lifestyle is possible from USD 600 to 900. A family with children in international school should plan for USD 4,000 to 6,500.

### Is Bali still cheap in 2026?
Cheaper than Western Europe by roughly 50 to 65 percent and cheaper than Australia or the United States by 60 to 70 percent, for a comparable lifestyle. But prices have risen sharply since 2022, particularly in Canggu and Seminyak, and Bali is no longer the ultra-low-cost destination it was a decade ago.

### Can you live in Bali on 1000 dollars a month?
Yes, but not the version most people picture. That budget means a room rather than a villa, local food for most meals, a scooter, and limited air conditioning. Plenty of people live well on it. It is not the life shown in the videos.

### How much is rent in Bali per month?
A one-bedroom villa starts around IDR 17.5 million a month in Canggu and IDR 15.6 million in Seminyak. Two-bedroom villas run roughly IDR 15 to 45 million depending on area and condition. Annual contracts are cheaper per month but are usually paid in full up front.

### Do you pay tax living in Bali?
If you spend more than 183 days in Indonesia in a year you are an Indonesian tax resident, regardless of your visa type or where your income is paid. That applies to remote workers earning entirely foreign income.
