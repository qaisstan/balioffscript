Guests book a Bali villa on photographs, and a large share of what makes those photographs work is green. Landscaping is also one of the most consistently omitted lines in a build budget.

## What it costs

**A basic garden** on a small plot — ground preparation, turf, hedging, a few specimen plants and irrigation — is a modest four-figure sum.

**A properly designed garden** on a three to five are villa plot, with mature planting, hardscaping, lighting and irrigation, commonly runs **USD 8,000 to USD 25,000** depending on scale and specification.

**Mature specimen trees** are where the money goes. A large frangipani, a mature palm or an established tree transplanted in costs considerably more than a young one and is the difference between a garden that photographs now and one that photographs in four years.

**Hardscaping** — paths, stone, decking, water features — is priced like small-scale construction and adds up quickly.

**Lighting**, which is underrated and transforms evening photographs, is inexpensive relative to its effect.

**Irrigation**, which is not optional on anything beyond a small garden, because hand watering does not survive a manager's schedule.

## Why it is not in the build quote

Because it is not building. Contractors quote the structure; landscaping is a separate trade and a separate quote, and nobody mentions it until the building is finished and the site is bare earth.

Assume it is excluded unless the contract says otherwise, and budget it alongside furniture as part of getting the property usable rather than as an afterthought.

## Why it pays

**It is in every photograph.** The pool shot, which is your cover image, includes the garden behind and around it. A bare plot photographs as a building site regardless of how good the villa is.

**It creates privacy**, which in an increasingly dense Canggu is a genuine differentiator. Planting between you and the neighbour is cheaper and better looking than a wall.

**It moderates temperature**, meaningfully. Shade and planting reduce the load on your aircon.

**Guests expect it.** Bali sells as lush. A villa with a hard-landscaped courtyard and no green does not read as Bali and the reviews say so.

Per dollar spent, landscaping moves the nightly rate more than most of what goes into a building.

## What works here

**Local and tropical species**, which grow fast, need less water and belong in the setting. Frangipani, heliconia, bird of paradise, bamboo, palms, ferns and ground cover.

**Fast-growing screening** for privacy, which in this climate establishes quickly.

**Structure.** A garden of only soft planting reads as unmanaged. Stone, paths, level changes and defined areas give it form.

**Lighting.** Uplighting on trees, path lighting, and light in the pool area. This is the cheapest single improvement to evening photographs available.

**Low-maintenance where possible**, because everything grows fast here and a high-maintenance garden becomes a neglected one.

## What to avoid

**Lawn as the main feature.** It needs constant water, constant mowing and it looks poor in the dry season.

**Anything that drops heavily into the pool.** Certain trees will cost your pool service hours every week and produce a pool that is never quite clean.

**Planting against the building**, which bridges termite barriers, traps moisture against walls and causes damp.

**Roots near the pool, the septic system or the drainage.** Bamboo in particular is aggressive and will find and damage things.

**Over-planting.** A garden crammed at installation looks good for a year and becomes an overgrown maintenance problem in three.

## Maintenance

A garden in Bali grows continuously. There is no dormant season.

**Budget a regular gardener** — two or three visits a week on a typical villa, or a daily presence on a larger one. This is an ordinary operating cost.

**Irrigation** on a timer, checked regularly, because a failed irrigation system in the dry season kills a garden faster than anything.

**Periodic renewal.** Planting gets leggy, hedges outgrow their position, and a garden needs refreshing every few years to keep photographing well.

## If you are buying an existing villa

A tired garden is one of the highest-return improvements available.

Ahead of a reletting push or a sale, cleaning up the planting, adding structure and mature specimens, installing lighting and then **rephotographing the property** commonly returns more than any interior work at a fraction of the cost.

It is also the improvement most owners never make, because the garden is not broken — it is just not selling the property.

## Common questions

### How much does landscaping cost for a Bali villa?
A properly designed garden on a three to five are plot commonly runs USD 8,000 to USD 25,000 including mature planting, hardscaping, lighting and irrigation.

### Is landscaping included in a Bali build quote?
Almost never. It is a separate trade and a separate quote, and it should be budgeted alongside furniture as part of making the property usable.

### Does the garden affect villa rental income?
Substantially. It appears in every photograph including the cover image, creates privacy, moderates temperature, and per dollar moves the nightly rate more than most building spend.

### What plants work best in a Bali villa garden?
Local and tropical species that grow fast and need less water — frangipani, heliconia, bird of paradise, palms, ferns and ground cover — with hardscaping for structure.

### What should I avoid planting near a Bali villa?
Anything that drops heavily into the pool, planting against the building which bridges termite barriers and traps moisture, and aggressive roots near the pool, septic system or drainage.
