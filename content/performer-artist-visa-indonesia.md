Bali has an enormous events, music and wellness economy, a large share of it staffed by foreigners, and a meaningful share of that running on the wrong permits.

## The activity that counts

**Performing for an audience** is work. A DJ playing a set, a band playing a venue, a dancer performing, an artist exhibiting commercially.

**Teaching** is work. A visiting yoga teacher running a retreat, a workshop leader, a trainer delivering a course.

**Delivering a service at an event** is work. A wedding photographer, a videographer, a celebrant, a makeup artist, a planner running the day.

**Payment is not the test.** A DJ playing for free at a friend's opening is performing. An unpaid guest teacher at a retreat is teaching.

## What is required

A **sponsoring entity in Indonesia** — the venue, the promoter, the event organiser, the retreat operator — that is registered and in a position to sponsor.

**Documentation of the activity**: what you will do, when, where, for how long, and for whom.

**Appropriate authorisation** for the activity, which for short engagements is typically a visit-type arrangement with entertainment or performance permitted, and for longer or employment-like arrangements engages the **RPTKA** foreign worker framework.

Categories, index codes and requirements change, and short-engagement entertainment arrangements have their own administrative practice. Verify the current position rather than assuming.

## Why enforcement has tightened

Bali's events and wellness sectors are visible and commercial, and unauthorised foreign workers in them have drawn attention.

There have been enforcement actions against foreign nationals performing and teaching without authorisation, and the general regulatory direction in Bali is one-way — **Perda Bali 4/2026** on nominee arrangements, the **22 July 2026** closure of new foreign-owned villa and homestay registration to PMA companies, and materially stricter tax administration under Coretax.

Building a working pattern on the assumption that enforcement stays light is planning against the trend.

## The consequences

Fines, deportation, and a **re-entry ban** of a defined period.

For someone whose income depends on working the Bali season annually, a re-entry ban ends that. For someone who also owns property here, it separates them from an asset they cannot manage remotely or easily sell from abroad.

The venue or organiser carries its own exposure, which is why the good ones insist on doing it properly.

## Where people get caught

**The recurring visitor.** Someone who comes for three months every year, plays or teaches, and leaves. Repeated entries with a visible public profile is the pattern immigration notices.

**Social media.** A performer publicising their Bali dates is publishing evidence of the activity. This is not hypothetical — it is how a number of cases have started.

**A venue that says it is fine.** The venue is not the one deported.

**"I'm on holiday and just playing a set."** The distinction between a genuinely spontaneous appearance and a booked, advertised, paid engagement is one immigration is capable of drawing.

## What to do instead

**Ask the venue or organiser what they are sponsoring**, under what index, and whether they have done it before. Established venues and promoters handle this routinely.

**Get it in writing** before you commit to dates or flights.

**For repeat annual work**, look at whether a longer-term arrangement is available — a work KITAS with RPTKA through a single sponsoring employer is more secure than a series of short engagements on visit permits.

**If you work remotely for a foreign employer** and perform only incidentally, the **E33G remote worker KITAS** covers the remote work but not the performing, and the distinction matters.

## For a villa owner hosting events

If you host weddings or retreats and foreign suppliers deliver at them — photographers, celebrants, teachers, DJs — their permit position is a question worth asking, because an enforcement action at your property is your problem too.

Use suppliers who can answer it. The professional ones can.

## Common questions

### Do you need a visa to DJ in Bali?
Yes. Performing for an audience is work regardless of payment, and it requires a sponsoring entity and appropriate authorisation rather than a tourist visa.

### Is teaching yoga in Bali considered work?
Yes. Teaching is work in immigration terms whether or not you are paid, which includes visiting teachers running retreats and workshops.

### Can I shoot a wedding in Bali on a tourist visa?
No. Delivering a service at an event is work. A sponsoring entity and appropriate authorisation are required.

### What happens if I perform without the right visa in Indonesia?
Fines, deportation and a re-entry ban, with the venue or organiser carrying its own exposure. Publicising dates on social media is how a number of cases have started.

### What should I ask a Bali venue before accepting a booking?
What visa they are sponsoring, under what index, and whether they have done it before. Get the answer in writing before committing to dates or flights.
