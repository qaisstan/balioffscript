The **SKCK** — *Surat Keterangan Catatan Kepolisian* — is the Indonesian police certificate confirming an absence of recorded criminal history. It appears in more processes than people expect and it is rarely arranged in advance.

## When it is needed

**Some immigration applications**, depending on the permit type and current requirements.

**Employment**, particularly with larger employers, in regulated sectors and in anything involving children.

**Some business licensing**, depending on the activity.

**Certain civil registration processes.**

**Volunteer placements**, especially those involving vulnerable people, where a clearance is both a requirement and a sign that the organisation is operating properly.

Requirements change and differ by process. Confirm what the specific application actually needs rather than obtaining one speculatively — though obtaining one early is generally better than discovering you need it.

## Getting one in Indonesia

Applied for through the police, at a level appropriate to the purpose — local, regency, provincial or national depending on what the receiving authority requires. A certificate issued at too low a level will be rejected.

You will generally need your **passport**, your **stay permit**, address documentation such as the **SKTT**, photographs to a specified standard, and fingerprinting.

It has a **validity period**, after which it expires and must be reissued. Obtaining one a year ahead of when you need it is not useful.

## The clearance from your home country

Separately, many Indonesian processes want a police certificate from your **country of nationality or previous residence**, not just an Indonesian one.

That is obtained from your home authority — a national police check in Australia, an ACRO certificate in the UK, an FBI identity history summary in the United States — and it then needs:

**Legalisation** by apostille, since Indonesia is a party to the Hague Convention, or consular legalisation where applicable.

**Sworn translation** by a *penerjemah tersumpah*.

This chain is the slow part. An FBI check with an apostille and a sworn translation is a matter of weeks to months, not days, and it is the step that most often delays an application.

**Start it early.** If you are planning a permit application, a company formation involving directorships, or employment, begin the home-country clearance before anything else.

## Order of operations

**Legalise first, translate second**, in most cases. Translating before legalising frequently means paying for the translation twice.

Confirm the sequence with the authority that will receive it, because practice varies and a rejected document costs weeks.

## Where this catches people

**The applicant who has everything else ready.** Company formed, lease signed, flights booked, and then a police clearance from a country they left eight years ago that takes two months to obtain.

**Validity expiring** between obtaining it and submitting, because another part of the application ran late.

**The wrong issuing level**, where a local certificate was obtained for a process requiring a provincial or national one.

**Name mismatches**, where the certificate spells a name differently from the passport. Indonesian systems match strictly and reject rather than query.

## Practical advice

**Establish what is actually required** for your specific application, in writing, before obtaining anything.

**Start the home-country clearance first**, because it is the longest chain.

**Check the validity period** against your realistic submission date.

**Match the name exactly** to your passport, including middle names and their order.

**Keep the legalised original and the sworn translation** together, somewhere reachable from another country. You will very likely need them again.

## Common questions

### What is an SKCK in Indonesia?
The police certificate confirming an absence of recorded criminal history, issued by the police at local, regency, provincial or national level depending on the purpose.

### When do I need an SKCK?
For some immigration applications, employment particularly in regulated sectors or involving children, some business licensing, and certain civil registration processes.

### Do I need a police check from my home country too?
Frequently yes, in addition to an Indonesian one, legalised by apostille and translated by a sworn translator. This chain takes weeks to months.

### How long is an SKCK valid?
It has a defined validity period after which it must be reissued, so obtaining one far in advance of when you need it is not useful.

### What order should I legalise and translate a police certificate?
Legalise first, then translate, in most cases. Confirm the sequence with the receiving authority, because doing it in the wrong order often means paying twice.
