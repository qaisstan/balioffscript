This is the first question almost every foreign buyer asks, and it contains a false premise. Once you see the premise, the rest of the decision becomes straightforward.

## The short answer

- **Freehold (Hak Milik) is only for Indonesian citizens.** Not for foreigners with residency, not through marriage, not through a company.
- **What a foreigner can hold:** a **leasehold** contract, **Hak Pakai** in your own name if you hold residency, or **HGB** through your own foreign-owned company (PT PMA).
- **Most foreign buyers hold leasehold.** It needs no visa, no company and no minimum value, and it is fine when it is drafted well.
- **Anything sold to a foreigner as "freehold"** is a lease described wrongly, a company holding HGB, or a nominee arrangement that gives you nothing enforceable.

## Freehold is not on the menu

**Hak Milik**, Indonesian freehold, may only be held by Indonesian citizens. This is not a policy that changes with the government of the day. It sits in the Basic Agrarian Law of 1960, which reserves the strongest land right in the country to citizens, and it has survived every reform since.

So leasehold versus freehold is not a choice you get to make. The dangerous version of "freehold for foreigners" is the **nominee arrangement**: the land goes into an Indonesian person's name with a stack of side agreements (a loan, a mortgage, a power of attorney, a statement of trust) meant to give you control in practice. Indonesian courts have consistently held that agreements designed to get around the foreign ownership rule are void, and a Denpasar court annulled one in Decision No. 274/Pdt.G/2020/PN Dps. Since **Perda Bali 4/2026**, nominee arrangements are also criminal in Bali, with up to five years and a fine of one billion rupiah, and the liability falls on **both** sides. You are relying entirely on the goodwill of the person whose name is on the certificate. See [why nominee deals fail](/ownership/nominee-structure-bali/).

## The real comparison: leasehold, Hak Pakai and HGB

| | Leasehold | Hak Pakai | HGB via PT PMA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Contract with the landowner | Registered right of use | Registered building right |
| In whose name | Yours, contractually | Yours, at the land office | Your company's |
| Residency needed | No | Yes, KITAS or KITAP | No, but you run a company |
| Minimum value | No | Yes, houses and apartments | Capital thresholds apply |
| Term | Whatever the contract says | Defined, extendable | Defined, extendable |
| Use | Anything the lease permits | Residence | Business, subject to classification |
| Ongoing cost | None | None | Annual compliance and filings |
| Financeable | Rarely | Occasionally | More possible, still hard |
| Appears on a certificate | No | Yes | Yes |
| On your death | Only if drafted to bind heirs | Registered, heirs face disposal rules | Passes as shares |

## How leasehold works in Bali

**Hak Sewa**, leasehold, is a right to use land for a set period in exchange for payment, created by a contract between you and the landowner and signed as a notarial deed. Most Bali leases are paid as one sum at the start for the whole term.

It is not a land right in the way Hak Milik, HGB and Hak Pakai are. Those are registered at the land office and appear on a certificate. Hak Sewa is not registered and appears on no certificate. Three things follow, and they are the whole subject:

**You have no registered interest.** Anyone inspecting the title sees the landowner and no trace of you. Your evidence is the deed in your possession.

**No bank will lend against it.** There is no registered interest to take security over. This is the main reason Bali is a cash market.

**Your protection is contractual, not proprietary.** The quality of the drafting is not a detail of the purchase. It is the purchase.

None of this makes leasehold unusable. Most foreign-held property in Bali is leasehold and most of it is fine. Any foreigner on any visa can sign one, and it is the fastest and cheapest lawful way into the market. See [why a Bali lease is not registered](/ownership/hak-sewa-not-registered/).

## What makes one lease good and another worthless

**1. The extension clause.** Indonesian practice separates two things that sound alike in English and are not alike at all:

- **Jaminan perpanjangan**: a guaranteed extension. The landowner is bound to extend, on terms already agreed.
- **Prioritas perpanjangan**: a priority to extend. You get first refusal, at whatever price is then demanded. This is worth very little, and it is what most leases actually contain.

What you want written in: a stated extension term, a fixed price or a formula tied to a published index, a defined window to exercise it, language binding the landowner's heirs and successors, and a named remedy if they refuse. On a thirty-year lease this clause is worth more than everything else in the contract combined. See [extension clauses explained](/ownership/leasehold-extension-clause/).

**2. Binding on heirs and successors.** If the landowner dies or sells the land, the new owner must honour your lease only if the deed says so in terms that survive. Without it, your counterparty can change to someone with no interest in your position.

**3. A right to assign or sublease.** Without it you cannot sell and you cannot rent, which removes your exit and your income in one clause. See [transferring a Bali lease](/ownership/transferring-bali-lease/).

**4. A right to build, if you plan to.** With an obligation on the landowner to sign every permit application within a set time and without further payment, plus a power of attorney so you are not waiting on them at each step.

**5. Full payment recorded.** The deed must record that the whole term is paid, or you leave room for a later claim of arrears.

**6. Named remedies.** What happens if the landowner obstructs, sells, refuses consent or disputes the boundary. Specific performance and a defined compensation figure, not a promise of good faith.

**7. Vacant possession and quiet enjoyment**, stated expressly.

## How long a Bali lease can run

Twenty-five to thirty years is standard, often with an agreed extension taking it to fifty or more.

Leases advertised at eighty years are almost never one eighty-year term. They are built as an initial period plus contractually agreed extensions, typically 30 plus 25 plus 25, because Indonesian notaries generally decline to sign a single term far beyond thirty years. That structure is workable and serious buyers use it. What you must check is whether the extensions are guaranteed or only promised, and at what price. An eighty-year headline built on two prioritas clauses is a thirty-year lease with marketing attached.

## What happens to the building at the end

Under Indonesian practice the building generally follows the land at the end of the lease, unless the contract says otherwise. You paid for the villa; at expiry it belongs to the landowner. See [what happens when land rights expire](/ownership/land-rights-expiry-bali/).

This is why a leasehold is a **wasting asset**. Each year removes a year of use and income, and the decline speeds up near the end, because the pool of buyers willing to take a short remainder shrinks fast. A lease with eight years left trades far below half the value of the same property with thirty. On a USD 300,000 villa with 25 years, the term alone costs about USD 12,000 a year. The full arithmetic is in [Bali villa rental yield and ROI](/rental/villa-rental-licensing-roi/).

## Hak Pakai: the registered option for residents

**Hak Pakai** is a registered right of use in your own name at the land office. It needs a KITAS or KITAP and a minimum property value that varies by region and property type. For a resident buying a home, it is the only route that puts a registered right in your own name. See [Hak Pakai explained](/ownership/hak-pakai-explained/) and [PT PMA vs Hak Pakai](/company/pt-pma-vs-hak-pakai/).

## HGB through a PT PMA: the company route

A foreign-owned company can hold **HGB**, a registered building right with a defined, extendable term. It suits a genuine business or development, with capital requirements and permanent yearly compliance. It is still not freehold. On **22 July 2026** Bali closed the villa and homestay classifications to **new** foreign-owned companies, which matters if the plan was short-let rental. See [HGB vs leasehold](/ownership/hgb-vs-leasehold-bali/) and [the KBLI closure](/company/pt-pma-kbli-closure-bali/).

## How to choose

**A home, and you hold a KITAS or KITAP.** Hak Pakai, the only route that puts a registered right in your own name.

**A home, no residency, or a smaller budget.** A well-drafted long leasehold. The drafting is the product, and the money you spend on an independent notary reviewing it is the best-spent money in the transaction.

**An income property.** Usually leasehold, priced so the income clears the yearly cost of the term, with the rental run through the lease route described in [the yield guide](/rental/villa-rental-licensing-roi/).

**A business or a development.** HGB through a PT PMA, provided the classification you need is open.

**Anything described as freehold for foreigners.** Walk. Not negotiate, not investigate further. The description tells you what kind of counterparty you are dealing with.

## Before you sign anything

- Verify the certificate at the land office, not from a copy handed to you.
- Confirm the landowner on the certificate is the person signing, and that a married landowner's spouse consents.
- Read the extension clause and establish whether it is jaminan or prioritas.
- Check that the lease binds heirs and successors and permits assignment.
- Confirm the zoning allows what you plan to do. See [zoning colours](/building/bali-zoning-colours/).
- Get a sworn translation, and have the Indonesian version reviewed by a notary **you** engaged, not one the agent introduced. See [notary and PPAT](/ownership/notary-ppat-bali/).

Every one of those is cheap now and expensive later. For the full order of steps, see [the buying process](/ownership/buying-process-bali-step-by-step/), and for the investment side, the [Bali property investment guide](/rental/how-to-invest-in-bali-property/).

## Common questions

### Can foreigners buy freehold in Bali?
No. Hak Milik is reserved for Indonesian citizens under the Basic Agrarian Law, and no structure changes that. Anything sold to a foreigner as freehold is something else.

### Is leasehold or freehold better in Bali?
The choice is not available to a foreigner. Compare leasehold against Hak Pakai and HGB through a company, which are the three lawful routes.

### What is a leasehold in Bali?
A notarised contract that gives you the right to use land, and usually the building on it, for a set term, typically 25 to 30 years, paid up front. It is not registered at the land office and appears on no certificate.

### Can a foreigner hold leasehold in Indonesia?
Yes. Any foreigner on any visa can sign a lease in their own name. It needs no residency, no company and no minimum value.

### Can a foreign investor get Hak Pakai?
Hak Pakai needs residency, a KITAS or KITAP, and a minimum property value. A foreign investor without residency usually holds leasehold, or HGB through a PT PMA if it is a genuine business.

### How long is leasehold in Bali?
Commonly 25 to 30 years, often with agreed extensions. Eighty-year leases are built as an initial term plus extensions, because notaries generally decline a single term far beyond thirty years.

### Can I get a mortgage on a Bali leasehold?
No. There is no registered interest for a bank to take security over, which is why almost all Bali purchases are paid in cash or with money borrowed abroad.

### Who owns the villa at the end of a Bali lease?
Generally the landowner, because the building follows the land unless the contract says otherwise. This is why the extension clause carries the value.

### Can I sell a Bali leasehold?
Only if the deed allows assignment, and only for the years remaining. Check whether the landowner's consent is needed, and on what terms, before you buy.

### Does a PT PMA give me freehold?
No. A PT PMA holds HGB, a registered building right with a defined term. It is not Hak Milik.

### Is a nominee arrangement legal in Bali?
No. Courts have consistently held these agreements void, and since Perda Bali 4/2026 they are criminal in Bali for both parties.

### What is the difference between jaminan and prioritas perpanjangan?
Jaminan is a guaranteed extension the landowner is bound to grant. Prioritas is only a right of first refusal at whatever price is then demanded, which is worth far less.
