Buyers compare these three on layout and price. The differences that actually matter are legal and structural, and they show up years later at the exit.

## Villa

A standalone building on its own plot, with its own pool in most cases, and its own land right underneath it.

**Title.** Leasehold over the land, Hak Pakai if you hold residency and the value threshold is met, or HGB held by a PT PMA. You control the land right directly.

**Control.** Total. You decide on renovation, management, pricing and when to sell. No committee, no service charge, no shared decisions.

**Income.** The highest gross and net of the three, and the widest range of outcomes. A well-run villa outperforms everything. A badly run one underperforms an apartment.

**Costs.** The highest. Pool maintenance, garden, staff, structural upkeep, insurance. Expect fifteen to twenty-five percent of gross revenue before management fees.

**Exit.** The deepest resale market in Bali by a large margin. Villas are what buyers come here looking for.

## Apartment

A unit within a building, usually with shared facilities and a body corporate running them.

**Title.** Strata title — *Hak Milik atas Satuan Rumah Susun* — over the unit, with the land held collectively. Foreigners may hold strata title over an apartment subject to a minimum value threshold, and in practice most foreign apartment ownership in Bali runs through leasehold or a company anyway.

**Control.** Limited. A building committee makes decisions about the common property, the service charge is set collectively, and your ability to renovate is constrained.

**Income.** Lower gross than a villa of equivalent value, but lower costs and much lower management burden. Occupancy is often higher because the price point serves a bigger market.

**Costs.** Service charge, which is predictable and unavoidable, plus a sinking fund for building works. Considerably simpler than running a villa.

**Exit.** The narrowest market of the three in Bali. Apartments are a small share of the market and the buyer pool is thin. This is the single biggest argument against them here.

## Townhouse

A unit in a row or complex of similar units, sharing walls and often a communal pool or garden, but with its own entrance and usually its own small outdoor space.

**Title.** Varies more than the others. Sometimes strata, sometimes a lease over a defined portion of a larger parcel, sometimes an arrangement that needs careful reading. This is where I see the most confused paperwork in Bali.

**Control.** Partial. Your unit is yours, the shared parts are not.

**Income.** Between the two. Lower rate than a villa, higher than an apartment, with a guest market that likes the space without the villa price.

**Costs.** Shared maintenance on common areas, own maintenance inside. Moderate.

**Exit.** Better than an apartment, worse than a villa. The buyer pool is mostly investors rather than owner-occupiers, which makes it more price-sensitive.

## The licensing difference

If you intend to rent short-term, the three are not equivalent.

A villa operated commercially needs the appropriate operational licensing, and Bali closed new foreign-owned villa and homestay registration on 22 July 2026. An apartment in a building already licensed as serviced accommodation sits under that building's licence, which can be simpler. A townhouse depends on how the complex is registered.

Before assuming you can rent any of them nightly, confirm the specific position for the specific property. "Everyone here does it" is not a licence.

## What I would actually recommend

**Villa** for almost everyone. Best income, best control, deepest exit market. The costs and the management burden are real and they are the price of the other three advantages.

**Townhouse** if the budget does not reach a villa in an area you want, and the title is clean. Read the structure documents carefully before anything else.

**Apartment** only if you have a specific reason — you want minimal management, you want a lock-up-and-leave, or the building itself is exceptional. As a pure investment in Bali, the thin resale market is a real cost that does not show up until you try to sell.

## Common questions

### Can foreigners buy an apartment in Bali?
Yes, through strata title subject to a minimum value threshold, or more commonly through leasehold or a company structure. The rules differ from those for landed property.

### Is a villa or apartment better investment in Bali?
A villa in most cases: higher net income, full control and a far deeper resale market. An apartment has lower costs and less management burden but a thin buyer pool at exit.

### What is the difference between a townhouse and a villa in Bali?
A villa is standalone on its own plot with its own land right. A townhouse shares walls and usually common areas, and the title structure varies considerably between complexes.

### Can I rent out an apartment short-term in Bali?
Only if the building carries the appropriate licensing. Confirm the specific position for the specific building rather than assuming, since operational licensing differs from building permits.

### Which property type holds value best in Bali?
Villas, by a large margin, because they have the deepest resale market. Apartments are the hardest to sell at a good price.
