Almost every Bali build overruns, and the reasons are predictable enough that a realistic programme is possible if you build them in from the start.

## The realistic sequence

**Land search and due diligence: one to three months.** Finding the parcel, then one to three weeks of verification on a clean title, longer where there is an undivided inheritance, an encumbrance or a records discrepancy.

**Design: two to four months.** Concept, developed design, technical drawings and structural calculations. Rushing this is how you get a building that costs more to construct and earns less.

**Permits: variable, and the most unpredictable step.** PBG processing depends on the regency and on how complete the submission is. An incomplete application is returned rather than processed, which is why the design stage matters.

**Construction: six to twelve months** for a straightforward villa once permits are in hand.

**Furniture and fit-out: four to eight weeks**, and it can run in parallel with the end of construction if you order early.

**SLF and licensing: weeks**, after completion and inspection.

**Listing, photography and ramp-up: six to twelve months** to reach sustainable occupancy, because platform ranking rewards booking history and review volume.

**Land purchase to first guest: eighteen to twenty-four months** is realistic. Land purchase to a property performing at its steady state is closer to two and a half to three years.

Model your returns on that, not on construction finishing.

## The pauses to build in

**Nyepi** stops everything island-wide for a day — no vehicles, no work, nobody leaving their property.

**Galungan and Kuningan**, the major celebrations ten days apart, pause work and staff have genuine obligations.

**Odalan**, temple anniversary ceremonies on a 210-day cycle, pause work locally and can close roads.

**Cremation ceremonies**, which involve significant community participation.

**The wet season**, roughly November to March, slows groundwork, pours and external finishing.

A programme that assumes continuous work is wrong before it starts, and blaming a contractor for a ceremony is not a productive conversation. Put the pauses in the schedule and everyone's expectations stay aligned.

## What actually causes overruns

**An incomplete specification.** A fixed price against vague drawings becomes a variations argument, and variations are where both budget and programme go.

**Variations instructed verbally** and priced afterwards. The most common cause of dispute on Bali builds. Require written instruction with an agreed price **and an agreed time impact** before the work is done.

**Remote management.** A build supervised closely finishes better, cheaper and faster than one run from abroad. Substantially so. Either be here for much of it or pay an architect or project manager five to ten percent for genuine site supervision.

**Material lead times**, particularly for anything imported or custom-made. Custom joinery and furniture take weeks and makers are busy ahead of the season.

**Utility connections.** Upgrading PLN capacity takes time and in some areas needs infrastructure work. Water, where a bore is required, can be expensive and uncertain. Start these early rather than at the end.

**Permit deviation.** Building something different from the approved drawings surfaces at SLF, and fixing it then is the most expensive version of every problem.

**Cash flow.** A contractor waiting on a payment stops work. Milestone payments should be inspected and released promptly.

## How to keep it on track

**Complete the specification before you start.** Every material, finish and fitting, with allowances stated where a choice is outstanding.

**Contract with a programme, a completion date and liquidated damages** at a stated daily or weekly rate. Without a number, late delivery costs the contractor nothing.

**Pay against verified milestones**, inspected by someone you appointed, and release promptly once verified.

**Hold five to ten percent retention** through a defects liability period.

**Order long-lead items early** — furniture, custom joinery, imported fittings, utility upgrades.

**Visit, or have someone visit weekly** and report with photographs.

**Add a genuine contingency** — ten to fifteen percent on cost, and two to three months on time. You will use both, and a programme with no float is a programme that fails on the first ordinary delay.

## Common questions

### How long does it take to build a villa in Bali?
Six to twelve months of construction once permits are in hand, and eighteen to twenty-four months from land purchase to first guest including design, permits and fit-out.

### Why do Bali builds overrun?
Incomplete specifications producing variations, verbal instructions priced afterwards, remote management without supervision, material lead times and utility connection delays.

### Do ceremonies stop construction in Bali?
Yes. Nyepi stops everything island-wide, and Galungan, Kuningan and local temple ceremonies pause work, sometimes for several days. Build them into the programme.

### How much contingency should a Bali build have?
Ten to fifteen percent on cost and two to three months on time. A programme with no float fails on the first ordinary delay.

### When will a new villa actually earn at its full rate?
Two and a half to three years from land purchase, because a new listing takes six to twelve months after completion to reach sustainable occupancy.
