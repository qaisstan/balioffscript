Volunteering in Indonesia is lawful, and doing it on a tourist visa is not. The distinction catches a lot of well-intentioned people.

## Why a tourist visa does not cover it

A visa on arrival permits tourism, visiting, meetings and conferences. It does not permit **work**, and Indonesian immigration practice treats productive activity as work **regardless of whether you are paid**.

Teaching at a school, building something, running a programme, providing professional services to an organisation — these are work. That an NGO is receiving the benefit rather than a company, and that you are receiving nothing, does not change the analysis.

This surprises people who assume payment is the test. It is not.

## What the volunteer route is

A **visit visa for approved voluntary activity**, sponsored by a recognised organisation in Indonesia.

It permits the volunteer work described in the application, with that organisation, for a defined period. It does not authorise other work, and it does not convert into general residency.

## What it requires

**A sponsoring organisation** that is properly registered and in a position to sponsor. Not every organisation running volunteer programmes is, and this is the step that most often fails.

**Documentation of the activity** — what you will do, for how long, under whose supervision.

**The usual personal documents**, financial evidence and often a police clearance, particularly where the work involves children.

Requirements and index codes change, so verify the current position rather than following older guidance.

## Where this goes wrong in Bali

Bali has a large volunteer and "voluntourism" sector, and a substantial part of it operates on tourist visas.

Three problems follow.

**The volunteer is exposed.** Working without authorisation carries consequences from fines through deportation to a re-entry ban. For someone who also owns property here, a re-entry ban is disproportionately damaging.

**The organisation is exposed.** Hosting unauthorised foreign workers is the organisation's problem too, and a complaint from a competitor or a former volunteer converts a dormant issue into an active one.

**Child protection.** Programmes placing unvetted short-term foreign volunteers with vulnerable children are a recognised problem internationally, and the absence of a proper permit process is usually a symptom of an absence of proper safeguarding generally.

If an organisation tells you a tourist visa is fine, that tells you something about the organisation.

## Professional skills volunteering

A separate category worth naming: retired or working professionals — architects, doctors, teachers, engineers — offering skills to a local organisation.

This is genuinely valuable and it is unambiguously work in immigration terms. The more professional the contribution, the more clearly it falls on the wrong side of the line for a tourist visa.

If you want to contribute skills, do it through an organisation that can sponsor the correct permit. The good ones can and will.

## For property owners

The relevant point is the same as everywhere else in Indonesian immigration: **your permit has to match what you are actually doing**.

Someone who bought a villa, lives here part of the year on tourist visas, manages the property themselves and volunteers at a local school twice a week is accumulating three separate exposures and has probably not thought of any of them as a problem.

None of it is dramatic. All of it surfaces at the wrong moment — a permit application, a dispute, a complaint.

## Common questions

### Can you volunteer in Indonesia on a tourist visa?
No. Indonesian immigration treats productive activity as work regardless of payment, so volunteering requires a visa covering the activity, sponsored by an approved organisation.

### Does unpaid work count as work in Indonesia?
Yes. Payment is not the test. Teaching, building, running programmes or providing professional services are work whether or not you receive anything.

### What do I need for a volunteer visa in Indonesia?
A properly registered sponsoring organisation, documentation of the activity and supervision, personal documents, financial evidence and often a police clearance.

### What if the organisation says a tourist visa is fine?
That is information about the organisation. The exposure falls on you as well as on them, and programmes without proper permit processes usually lack proper safeguarding too.

### What happens if I volunteer without authorisation?
Consequences range from fines through deportation to a re-entry ban, which is disproportionately damaging for anyone who also owns property in Indonesia.
