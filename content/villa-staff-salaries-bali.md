Staff are usually the single largest fixed cost of running a villa here, and they are owed whether or not a guest ever arrives.

## Monthly base salaries

| Role | Monthly, USD |
| --- | --- |
| Housekeeper | 120 to 300 |
| Gardener | 50 to 120 |
| Pool service | 30 to 70 |
| Security | 120 to 200 |
| Villa manager | 300 to 600 |
| Cook | 150 to 350 |

A typical three-bedroom villa runs two to three staff, roughly IDR 7M to 12M a month in base salaries combined.

## The costs on top of base salary

This is where owners get caught out, because these are legal obligations rather than optional extras.

**BPJS contributions.** Employer contributions to the national health and employment social security schemes add roughly 14 percent on top of salary. This is not discretionary for properly employed staff.

**THR.** Tunjangan Hari Raya is the mandatory religious holiday allowance: one full month of salary, paid annually before the holiday. Every employee with twelve months of service is entitled to it, and pro rata below that.

So a team costing IDR 7M a month in base salary is not costing IDR 84M a year. Add BPJS and THR and the real annual figure for a two-person team lands closer to **IDR 84M to 100M**.

## Regional minimum wage

Bali sets a provincial minimum wage annually, and regencies set their own within it. Badung, which covers Canggu, Seminyak and the airport side, runs the highest in the province. Paying below the applicable minimum is a labour law problem, not a negotiation.

## Employed or contracted?

Owners sometimes prefer to treat staff as contractors to avoid BPJS and THR. Whether that holds depends on the substance of the relationship, not the label on the paperwork: fixed hours, a single employer, direction and control all point to employment.

Getting this wrong is a labour dispute, and Indonesian labour law is protective of workers. Take local advice rather than assuming the arrangement you prefer is the one that applies.

## Termination is expensive

Indonesian law makes ending employment considerably harder and more expensive than most foreign owners expect. Severance is calculated on length of service and can run to many months of salary.

That has a practical consequence: hire carefully and hire slowly. A villa overstaffed in its first optimistic year is expensive to correct later.

## Whether staff should come with a manager

If a management company runs the villa, ask precisely who employs the staff. If the manager employs them, you have less control and less exposure. If you employ them and the manager directs them, you carry the obligations while someone else makes the decisions.

That second arrangement is common and is worth resisting, or at least pricing.

## Common questions

### How much do villa staff cost in Bali?
Base salaries run USD 120 to 300 a month for a housekeeper, USD 50 to 120 for a gardener, USD 30 to 70 for pool service, USD 120 to 200 for security and USD 300 to 600 for a villa manager.

### What is THR in Indonesia?
Tunjangan Hari Raya, the mandatory religious holiday allowance. It is one full month of salary paid annually, pro rata for employees with under a year of service. It is a legal entitlement, not a bonus at your discretion.

### What is BPJS and do I have to pay it?
BPJS is Indonesia's national health and employment social security system. Employer contributions add roughly 14 percent on top of salary for properly employed staff, and it is a legal obligation.

### How many staff does a Bali villa need?
A three-bedroom villa typically runs two to three: housekeeping, garden and pool, with security or a manager depending on how it operates. More staff is not automatically better service, and termination costs make overstaffing expensive to unwind.

### Can I pay Bali villa staff as contractors?
Only if the relationship genuinely is contracting. Fixed hours, a single employer and day-to-day direction point to employment regardless of what the paperwork says, and misclassification is a labour dispute waiting to happen.
