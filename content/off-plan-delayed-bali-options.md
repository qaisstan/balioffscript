Most off-plan projects in Bali finish late. A few months is normal. A year or more, with no clear new date, is a different situation, and the buyers who come out of it best are the ones who act early and calmly.

## Start with three lines in your contract

1. **The handover date.** The exact date, not "estimated".
2. **The grace period.** Many contracts give the developer extra months before any penalty applies.
3. **The penalty clause.** Often a percentage of the price per month of delay, usually with a cap. Check whether the cap has already been reached, because after that the developer has no money reason to hurry.

Then look for the clause most buyers miss: **your right to cancel and get your money back** after a long delay, sometimes called a long-stop date. That right is the strongest card you hold.

## Check what is really happening on site

Do not rely on progress photos from the developer. Someone you trust should visit and look at:

- whether work is happening every day, and how many workers are there;
- what stage the building is really at (structure, roof, windows, finishing, pool);
- whether the developer is building **a new phase** while yours stands still. That can mean money from new buyers is paying for old problems.

Talk to other buyers in the same project. A group of buyers is taken more seriously than one.

## Be careful what you sign

A common move is an addendum that offers you the rest of the penalty, or a new date, **in exchange for giving up your right to cancel**. Read it twice. If you sign away the right to cancel without a firm new deadline and continued penalties, the developer can take as long as it likes.

A fair addendum has a **clear new long-stop date**, penalties that keep running until handover, and your cancellation right kept if the new date is missed.

## Your options, from mildest to strongest

1. **Wait, with a better addendum.** Firm date, penalties continuing, right to cancel kept.
2. **Negotiate an exit.** A refund, part refund, or a swap into a finished unit from the same developer.
3. **Sell your contract.** Many contracts require the developer's written consent to transfer before completion. Ask for it in writing.
4. **Formal steps.** A formal notice from a lawyer, then mediation, arbitration or court. These take time and cost money, but a well-written notice often moves a developer who has been ignoring emails.

## Where to get help

- An **Indonesian notary or lawyer** for formal notices and anything in court.
- The **other buyers** in your project.
- An **independent review**: someone who reads your contract, visits the site, checks the developer's other projects and the real market value of your unit, and tells you which option makes sense in numbers. That is part of what I do for buyers in this situation.

## Common questions

### Is a delay normal for Bali off-plan projects?
A few months, yes. Weather, permits and materials slow most builds. A delay of a year or more with moving dates is a warning sign.

### Can I get my money back if the villa is not finished?
Only if your contract gives you that right, usually after a long-stop date, or if the developer agrees. Check the wording before you sign any new document.

### Should I sign an addendum with a new date?
Only if it keeps your right to cancel, sets a firm new date and keeps penalties running. Never give up the right to cancel in exchange for a small payment.

### Can I sell my off-plan villa before it is finished?
Often yes, but most contracts require the developer's written approval for a transfer before handover. Ask for it before you list it.
