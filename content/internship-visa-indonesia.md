Internships happen in Bali constantly — hospitality, design, development, NGOs — and a large share of them run on tourist visas, which is not what those visas permit.

## Why a tourist visa does not cover it

A visa on arrival permits tourism, visiting, meetings and conferences. It does not permit **work**, and Indonesian immigration practice treats productive activity as work **regardless of whether you are paid**.

An intern producing designs, writing code, serving guests, teaching or running a programme is working. That an organisation is receiving the benefit rather than paying a salary does not change the analysis.

This surprises people who assume payment is the test. It is not.

## What the route requires

**A sponsoring entity in Indonesia** that is properly registered and in a position to sponsor. This is the step that most often fails, because not every company hosting interns is set up for it.

**Documentation of the placement** — what the intern will do, for how long, under whose supervision, and the relationship with any educational institution.

**The entity's own compliance.** A sponsoring company with missed filings, an inactive NIB or a classification that does not cover the activity has a standing problem that attaches to anything it sponsors.

**Personal documents**, financial evidence and often a police clearance, particularly where the placement involves children.

Index codes and requirements change, so verify the current position rather than following an older checklist.

## Where this catches people in Bali

**Hospitality placements.** Hotels and villas take interns, and a number of them arrive on tourist visas with an arrangement that was never formalised.

**Design, architecture and development studios**, where the intern is producing deliverable work.

**NGOs and volunteer-adjacent placements**, where the language of "volunteering" obscures that it is still work in immigration terms.

**Your own company.** A foreign owner's family member "helping out" in the business over a summer is working.

## The exposure

Working without authorisation carries consequences from fines through deportation to a **re-entry ban**.

For a young person at the start of a career, an Indonesian immigration record is a worse outcome than it looks. For the hosting organisation, it is their exposure too.

The practical risk is rarely an inspector arriving. It is that the exposure exists continuously and materialises at a bad moment — a visa extension, a company review, a complaint from a competitor or a former employee.

## What to ask the host organisation

**What visa are you sponsoring me on, and under what index?**

**Have you done this before, and for how many people?**

**Is your entity registered and current?**

If the answer is that a tourist visa is fine, that tells you something about the organisation rather than about the rules. Organisations that handle interns properly can and will sponsor the correct permit.

## The alternatives

**A student permit**, if the placement runs through an accredited educational institution as part of a programme. Sponsored by the institution rather than the employer.

**A work KITAS**, if the placement is substantial enough and the host will sponsor it with RPTKA approval.

**Restructuring the arrangement** so what you actually do falls within a visit visa — attending, observing, meeting, learning without producing. That is a genuinely different activity and it is what some placements really are.

## For a property owner hosting someone

If you have a villa business and someone is coming to help run it, establish what permit covers them before they arrive.

The same principle applies to you: **owning the property is not work; operating it is.** Handling bookings, managing staff and instructing contractors is work whether you own the asset or not, and the permit has to match.

## Common questions

### Can you do an internship in Indonesia on a tourist visa?
No. Immigration treats productive activity as work regardless of payment, so an internship needs a permit covering the activity, sponsored by a registered entity.

### Does unpaid work count as work in Indonesia?
Yes. Payment is not the test. Producing designs, writing code, serving guests or running a programme is work whether or not you receive anything.

### What does an internship visa in Indonesia require?
A properly registered sponsoring entity, documentation of the placement and supervision, the entity's own compliance being current, and personal and financial documentation.

### What happens if I intern without the right visa?
Consequences range from fines through deportation to a re-entry ban, and the hosting organisation carries its own exposure.

### What should I ask a host organisation in Bali?
What visa they are sponsoring and under what index, whether they have done it before, and whether their entity is registered and current. "A tourist visa is fine" is information about them.
