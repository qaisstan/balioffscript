Families moving to Bali worry about education and then discover that what actually shapes daily life is whether there is anything for their children to do.

## Where it is concentrated

**Canggu and Umalas.** The densest offering — swim schools, surf lessons, football, gymnastics, martial arts, music, art, and a large homeschool and co-op network.

**Sanur.** Strong for families, with calm swimmable water, the safest beach environment in the south, established swim programmes and a settled residential community.

**Ubud.** Creative and outdoor-focused, with art, music, nature programmes and alternative education settings.

**Nusa Dua and Jimbaran.** Calm water, resort facilities and a quieter environment.

**The Bukit.** Surf-dominated, growing, with less structured provision for younger children.

Where you live determines what your children can do, more than anything else you decide.

## What is available

**Swimming**, which is the priority most parents name and which is well served in the main areas. Lap pool availability for older children is thinner.

**Surfing**, with lessons and junior programmes at the beginner beaches — Kuta, Batu Bolong, Seminyak and Sanur.

**Football**, with several established clubs and leagues.

**Martial arts**, including Muay Thai and Brazilian jiu-jitsu, with junior classes.

**Gymnastics and dance.**

**Music and art**, particularly around Ubud.

**Tennis and padel**, both growing.

**Scouts, clubs and holiday programmes**, run through schools and independently.

**Balinese culture** — dance, gamelan, carving — which is genuinely available and which children who take it up remember.

## The safe water question

This is what separates areas for families more than anything.

**Calm, swimmable beaches**: Sanur, Nusa Dua, Jimbaran, Melasti and Sawangan on the eastern Bukit.

**Not calm**: much of the west coast, including Canggu, Echo Beach, Pererenan, Seseh and the western Bukit surf beaches, where currents are strong and there is frequently no lifeguard.

Families consistently underestimate this, and it is the single most useful thing to know when choosing between areas with children.

## The practical friction

**Traffic.** Getting children to activities in the south takes longer than the map suggests, and doing three pick-ups across the week is a real constraint on where you live relative to where things are.

**Heat.** Outdoor activity happens early or late.

**Wet season.** November to March limits outdoor options and indoor provision is thinner than at home.

**Transience.** Children's friends leave, repeatedly, which is harder on children than on adults and is worth anticipating rather than discovering.

**Cost.** Individual activities are inexpensive. International school fees are the large expense and are covered separately.

## For a property owner

Families are one of the largest segments in the Bali market, particularly Australian families in school holidays, and the properties that win them are explicit.

**Say whether the beach nearby is swimmable.** It is the first question a family has and most listings do not answer it.

**Provide cots and high chairs**, which is a small cost and a direct filter in search.

**A shallow pool area**, visibility of the pool from the living space, and a clear statement of whether it is fenced.

**A kitchen a family can cook in**, because families cook.

**Shade away from the pool**, and somewhere for children that is not the water.

**A washing machine.**

**Mention what is nearby** — swim school, surf lessons, a park, a supermarket — because that is what a parent is actually assessing.

Airport transfer time matters more with small children than with any other guest, which makes Jimbaran at fifteen minutes and Sanur at twenty-five meaningfully different propositions from Uluwatu or Canggu at forty-five to ninety.

## Common questions

### Is there much for children to do in Bali?
More than people expect, concentrated in Canggu, Umalas, Sanur and Ubud — swimming, surfing, football, martial arts, gymnastics, music, art and Balinese culture.

### Which Bali beaches are safe for children?
Sanur, Nusa Dua, Jimbaran, Melasti and Sawangan have calm swimmable water. Much of the west coast including Canggu and Pererenan has strong currents and no lifeguard.

### Where do families with children live in Bali?
Canggu and Umalas for the density of activities and schools, Sanur for calm water and a settled community, Ubud for creative and outdoor programmes.

### What should a family-friendly villa provide?
Cots and high chairs, a shallow pool area visible from the living space with the fencing position stated, a usable kitchen, shade away from the pool and a washing machine.

### Does a villa listing need to mention the beach conditions?
Yes. Whether the nearby beach is swimmable is the first question a family has, and most listings do not answer it, which loses bookings to the ones that do.
