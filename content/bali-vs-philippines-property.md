Siargao gets called "the next Bali", Palawan sells the postcard beaches and Cebu sells condos. The Philippines is cheaper and English-speaking. Here is how it compares with Bali for someone who wants to buy.

## What a foreigner can own in the Philippines

- **Condominiums:** foreigners can own a unit, as long as foreign ownership of the whole building stays within **40%**.
- **Land:** the constitution bars foreigners from owning land.
- **Leases for investors:** since **Republic Act 12252** was signed on 3 September 2025, foreign **investors** can lease private land for a single period of up to **99 years**. This is tied to investment projects, not to a holiday home.
- **Leases for individuals:** a private person leasing land for their own use falls under older rules with **shorter terms** (25 years, renewable once for another 25).

## What a foreigner can hold in Bali

No ownership. A **lease**, usually 25 to 30 years with a written extension, **Hak Pakai** with residency, or **HGB through a PT PMA**. Bali gives more control over a villa and its land use than most Philippine island options.

## The market

**Bali** has a mature villa market: developers, managers, booking platforms and a constant flow of visitors from Australia, Europe and Asia. A well-run villa nets roughly **7 to 14%**.

**The Philippines** is earlier. Siargao and Palawan have strong tourist growth but thin infrastructure, limited flights and patchy power and water. Cebu and Manila are condo markets with long-term tenants and lower yields. The upside can be bigger; so is the risk of buying into a place that does not grow as fast as promised.

## Getting there

Bali has a large international airport with many direct routes. Siargao and El Nido have small airports with few flights, mostly via Manila or Cebu. Fewer flights means fewer guests and harder resale.

## Paperwork risk

Both countries punish nominee arrangements, where a local holds land for you on paper. In both, the safe structures are leases, a properly set up company, or a condo within the foreign cap. Land titles in remote Philippine islands can be unclear; land in Bali can have hidden heirs. Check everything before you pay.

## Which I would choose

- **You want a cheap entry and you are early on a growth story:** Siargao or Palawan, with a lawyer, a long lease and money you can afford to tie up.
- **You want a condo you own:** Cebu or Manila, within the 40% cap.
- **You want a villa that rents now, in a proven market:** Bali.

## Common questions

### Can foreigners own land in the Philippines?
No. Foreigners can own condominium units within the 40% foreign cap per building, and lease land.

### What is the 99-year lease in the Philippines?
Republic Act 12252, signed in September 2025, lets foreign investors lease private land for up to 99 years. It is aimed at investment projects; individual home leases follow shorter terms.

### Is Siargao the next Bali?
It has the surf and the vibe, but far fewer flights, rooms and services. It could grow a lot; it is not Bali yet.

### Is the Philippines cheaper than Bali?
Usually yes, for land and condos. Bali costs more but has deeper rental demand and more ways to exit.
