Yes, and the route is more straightforward than most people expect. It is also not the one most people are told about.

## The route

The **Second Home** family covers financially independent residence with no employer, no business and no family sponsor. The standard tier is commonly cited at either a deposit of around **USD 130,000** in an Indonesian state bank, or qualifying property of around **USD 1,000,000**.

Verify both at the point of filing. These thresholds get revised.

What you get is **five or ten years**, which is what makes it attractive against a permit you renew annually.

## The senior tiers people miss

Within that same family there are dedicated senior or elderly tiers, commonly cited at a minimum age of 55 or 60 depending on the sub-index, with income, pension or deposit conditions instead of the full standard threshold.

If you are over 55, ask about these specifically before defaulting to the standard tier. Agents frequently do not mention them.

## The thing that catches people

**Buying a villa does not let you stay.** Property and immigration are unrelated systems in Indonesia. You can own a house here and have no right to be in it beyond a tourist stay.

That surprises people constantly, and it is the most expensive misunderstanding in this market.

## What to think about beyond the visa

**Healthcare.** Routine care locally is fine. Serious care usually means Singapore. Comprehensive international insurance, and check the age limits on the policy before you commit to the move.

**Tax.** Presence, not passport. Beyond 183 days you are an Indonesian tax resident. That has consequences for pension income depending on your home country's treaty position, and it is a conversation to have before you arrive.

**The deposit is not a fee.** Confirm whether it must be maintained continuously, and in what account type, before you move the money.

## If the plan involves buying

Do the visa first. It costs less to discover a problem before you have committed capital, and the visa question is answerable in weeks while a property question is not.

## Common questions

### Can you retire in Bali as a foreigner?
Yes. There is a retirement route with age and financial requirements, alongside the Second Home visa and Golden Visa for those who meet the higher thresholds.

### What is the age requirement to retire in Bali?
The retirement permit carries a minimum age alongside income or savings evidence. Confirm the current figures, because both the age and the financial tests have been revised.

### How much money do you need to retire in Bali?
Beyond the visa threshold, plan the real budget: comfortable single living runs around USD 1,800 to 2,500 a month, plus health insurance with evacuation cover, which becomes more expensive with age.

### Can retirees buy property in Bali?
Holding valid residency opens Hak Pakai, which is the cleanest personal ownership route. That is one of the practical advantages of holding a retirement permit rather than repeated tourist stays.

### What is the biggest issue for retirees in Bali?
Healthcare. Routine care is good and cheap, complex care is limited, and evacuation to Singapore runs USD 55,000 to 100,000. Insurance is the whole plan, and age limits on policies matter.
