Raja Ampat sits off the northwest tip of Papua, about as far from Bali as London is from Cairo. It holds the highest recorded marine biodiversity of anywhere on the planet, and getting there is a project.

## Getting there from Bali

1. **Fly to Sorong**, usually via Jakarta, Makassar or Manado. Expect an overnight connection or a red-eye; total travel time from Bali is typically 8 to 16 hours including layovers.
2. **Ferry from Sorong to Waisai** on Waigeo, about two hours, running once or twice daily.
3. **Boat transfer** to your homestay or resort, 20 minutes to two hours.

Liveaboards depart from Sorong directly, which skips the last two steps.

## The permit

Raja Ampat requires a **marine park entry permit**, valid for the calendar year, bought on arrival or in advance. It costs several hundred thousand rupiah for foreign visitors and the money funds the marine protected areas. Carry the tag; it is checked.

## What you see

**The reefs.** More coral and reef fish species than anywhere recorded, in dense, healthy condition. Manta rays at Manta Sandy and Magic Mountain, walking sharks, wobbegongs, pygmy seahorses, schooling fish in numbers that surprise even experienced divers.

**The landscape.** Karst islands in turquoise water, viewed from the Piaynemo and Wayag viewpoints, which are the images you have seen.

**Snorkelling.** Unusually good for a dive destination: many reefs start in a metre of water, and homestay house reefs can be exceptional.

**Birds of paradise.** Red and Wilson's birds of paradise on Waigeo, seen at dawn from hides with local guides.

## Where to stay

**Homestays.** Simple wooden huts on stilts run by Papuan families, typically 400,000 to 700,000 rupiah per person per night including three meals. Basic: bucket showers, intermittent power, no air conditioning. The money goes directly to local communities, which is the strongest argument for this option.

**Resorts.** A handful, mid to high end, with dive operations, from roughly 200 to 800 USD a night.

**Liveaboards.** The way to reach the northern and southern extremes, from roughly 300 to 900 USD per person per day.

## Costs, honestly

A ten-day trip including flights from Bali, permits, transfers and diving rarely comes in under 2,000 USD per person even staying in homestays, and 4,000 to 8,000 is normal with a resort or liveaboard. Diving is charged per dive and the boats burn a lot of fuel at these distances.

## Season

**October to April** is generally considered the best window, with calmer seas and good visibility. **Mantas** are most reliable from roughly December to March at Manta Sandy.

**July and August** bring stronger winds in parts of the archipelago, though diving continues. There is no season with no rain: this is equatorial rainforest.

## Practical notes

**Cash.** There are ATMs in Sorong and Waisai and they cannot be relied on. Bring all the cash you need for homestays, permits and boat fuel.

**Malaria** is present in Papua. Take medical advice before travelling.

**Medical care is minimal** and the nearest hyperbaric chamber is a serious distance away. Dive conservatively and check your insurance covers evacuation from remote Indonesia.

**Connectivity** is limited and slow, which is part of the point.

**Respect local rules.** Many reefs are under community-managed sasi arrangements with seasonal closures. Follow your guide.

## Is it worth it

If you dive seriously, it is the best reef diving most people will ever do, and it justifies the journey. If you snorkel and want to see healthy coral rather than tick a bucket list, a homestay week is achievable and unforgettable.

If you want a beach holiday with good food and comfort, this is the wrong destination by a wide margin.

## Common questions

### How do you get to Raja Ampat from Bali?
Fly to Sorong via Jakarta, Makassar or Manado, then take the ferry to Waisai and a boat to your accommodation.

### How much does Raja Ampat cost?
Rarely under 2,000 USD per person for ten days including flights and diving, and commonly 4,000 to 8,000 with a resort or liveaboard.

### When is the best time to visit Raja Ampat?
Roughly October to April, with mantas most reliable from December to March.

### Do you need a permit for Raja Ampat?
Yes, a marine park entry permit valid for the calendar year, bought on arrival or in advance, and checked on site.

### Can you snorkel in Raja Ampat without diving?
Yes, and it is exceptionally good. Many reefs begin in shallow water and several homestays have excellent house reefs.
