Bali has a fitness infrastructure out of proportion to its size, and for a meaningful number of residents it is one of the reasons they stay.

## Where it is

**Canggu** has the densest concentration — large commercial gyms, CrossFit boxes, functional training, boxing and Muay Thai, pilates and reformer studios, and a very large yoga offering.

**Seminyak and Kerobokan** have established gyms and studios serving a residential population.

**Ubud** is yoga-centred, with the largest concentration of teacher trainings and retreat programmes anywhere in the region, alongside conventional gyms.

**Sanur and Nusa Dua** serve a more residential and family market.

**The Bukit** has a growing offer around Uluwatu and Ungasan, with surf as the dominant activity.

## Cost

Inexpensive by Western standards. Monthly gym memberships, class packs and personal training all cost a fraction of Australian, European or North American equivalents.

**Personal training** in particular is affordable here in a way it is not at home, which changes what is realistic for a lot of people.

**Drop-in classes** are widely available, which suits a transient population.

## The sports

**Surfing**, which is the dominant activity and which drives where a lot of people choose to live. The Bukit works April to October on dry season trade winds; the west coast around Canggu works November to March when winds are offshore there.

**Yoga**, at every level from casual to teacher training.

**Muay Thai and boxing**, with established gyms and a real community.

**CrossFit and functional training**, particularly in Canggu.

**Running**, which is hard in the south because of heat, traffic and the absence of footpaths. People run early, on the beach, or on a treadmill.

**Cycling**, which is genuinely dangerous on main roads and excellent in the interior and the highlands.

**Swimming**, with limited lap pool options — a real gap and one of the few things Bali does not serve well.

**Diving**, off Tulamben, Menjangan, Amed and the Nusa islands.

**Padel and tennis**, both of which have grown substantially.

**Golf**, at courses including Tanah Lot and the Bukit, which supports a specific and underserved accommodation market.

## Why it matters for a property owner

This is the commercially relevant part.

**Fitness infrastructure is part of why people stay long-term**, and long-stay guests fill February, March and November — the months that decide your annual occupancy, because peak fills itself.

A guest choosing between areas for a two-month stay is choosing on whether they can train, work and eat within reach. **Coworking, a gym and walkable food is the selection criteria**, not pool size.

That is a large part of why Canggu, Pererenan and Ubud hold occupancy through the quiet months and why areas without that infrastructure do not.

**For a villa in those areas**, mentioning proximity to specific gyms and studios in the listing is more useful than another adjective, because it is what that guest is actually searching on.

**A home gym** in a larger villa is a genuine differentiator for long stays and costs very little — a rack, weights and a mat in a covered outdoor area.

**Board storage and a rinse area** near a break, which almost no villa provides and surf guests value.

## The practical limits

**Heat.** Training outdoors in the middle of the day is not sensible most of the year. Early morning and evening.

**Air quality** in the congested southern areas is worse than the coast.

**Road danger.** Cycling and running on main roads is genuinely hazardous, and Bali's road injury rate is high with foreign visitors disproportionately represented.

**Injury.** Check your insurance covers the activity. Many policies exclude or limit adventure sports, and diving frequently requires a specific extension. Motorcycle exclusions catch people who were riding to the gym rather than doing anything adventurous.

## Common questions

### Are there good gyms in Bali?
Yes, with the densest concentration in Canggu — commercial gyms, CrossFit, boxing and Muay Thai, pilates and a very large yoga offering — and established options in Seminyak, Ubud, Sanur and the Bukit.

### How much does a gym cost in Bali?
A fraction of Western prices, with memberships, class packs and personal training all inexpensive by Australian, European or North American standards.

### Does fitness infrastructure affect villa bookings?
Substantially in long-stay areas. Guests choosing where to spend two months select on whether they can train, work and eat within reach, and those guests fill the quiet months.

### Is it safe to run or cycle in Bali?
Not on main roads, where traffic is heavy and road injury rates are high. People run early, on the beach or on treadmills, and cycle in the interior and highlands.

### Does insurance cover sport injuries in Bali?
Many policies exclude or limit adventure activities, diving frequently needs an extension, and motorcycle exclusions catch people riding to a gym rather than doing anything unusual.
