PLN supply in Bali is generally reliable and it is not continuous. How much that matters depends entirely on whether there are paying guests in the property when it fails.

## How often it happens

Varies by area. The developed south is better served than emerging corridors, the interior Bukit, the highlands, the east and the islands.

**The wet season is worse**, with storms causing faults and repairs taking longer.

**Planned maintenance** also causes outages, sometimes with notice and sometimes without.

Ask the previous owner, the manager and the neighbours about the specific property's history. This is not something you can research generally — it is street-level information.

## Why it matters for a rental

A villa without power has no aircon, no pool pump, no water pump, no internet, no lighting and a fridge warming up.

**Four hours of that with guests in residence produces a review**, and reviews drive ranking and rate. In peak season, in a full villa, it also produces a refund conversation.

For a property whose quiet months are filled by remote workers, a power cut is also a failed working day for a guest who chose your villa specifically because they could work there.

## The options

**A generator.** Diesel or petrol, sized to run essentials or the whole property. Reliable, proven, and it needs fuel, servicing, somewhere to sit and somewhere the noise does not reach guests or neighbours.

**Automatic transfer switch** is what makes a generator useful rather than theoretical. Without it, somebody has to be present to start it, which at 2am is nobody.

**Battery backup.** Silent, instant, no fuel, no emissions. Expensive per kilowatt-hour of capacity and finite in how long it runs. Excellent for essentials — lighting, internet, fridge, water pump and one aircon unit — for a few hours.

**Solar plus battery.** Where you already have solar, adding storage makes surplus useful and provides outage cover. Note that Indonesia's rooftop solar export arrangements have been constrained, so systems sized above daytime load need storage to be worthwhile at all.

**A UPS on the internet and essentials only**, which is cheap and covers the most common complaint for the shortest outages.

## What to actually back up

Sizing for the whole villa is expensive and usually unnecessary.

**Essentials**, in order of what guests notice: internet and router, lighting, water pump, fridge, and one or two aircon units in bedrooms.

**The pool pump** can wait. A few hours without filtration does no harm.

**Water heating** can wait.

A backup system covering essentials for four to six hours handles the overwhelming majority of outages at a fraction of the cost of whole-villa backup.

## The connection capacity point

Before considering backup at all, check your **PLN connection capacity**.

A villa whose supply trips under its own load in peak season has a sizing problem rather than an outage problem, and no generator fixes an undersized connection — it just means you are running a generator because your own aircon overloaded the supply.

Establish the contracted capacity, compare it against what the property actually runs, and upgrade if it is short. That is usually the first fix, not the backup system.

## Where backup is genuinely necessary

**Emerging corridors and remote parcels**, where supply is less robust and repairs take longer.

**The interior Bukit, the highlands, the east and the islands.**

**Any property marketing itself to remote workers**, where a working day lost is the specific thing your guest paid to avoid.

**Larger properties** where the guest expectation and the rate justify it.

## Where it is optional

A well-served address in central Canggu, Seminyak or Sanur, on a correctly sized connection, letting to holiday guests who will tolerate an hour without power.

Even there, a UPS on the internet and a few battery-backed lights costs very little and removes the most common complaint.

## Practical points

**Site the generator** where noise does not reach bedrooms, the pool deck or the neighbours. Generator noise in a residential Bali street is a community issue as well as a guest one.

**Service it and run it** periodically. A generator that has not run in eight months does not start when you need it.

**Keep fuel**, safely stored, and make sure your manager knows the routine.

**Brief guests.** A note explaining that outages happen occasionally and what the backup covers turns a problem into a minor inconvenience.

## Common questions

### Are power cuts common in Bali?
They happen, more frequently in the wet season and in emerging corridors, the interior Bukit, the highlands, the east and the islands than in the developed south.

### Does a Bali villa need a generator?
Any property in a less well-served area, or marketing to remote workers, benefits substantially. In central serviced areas a battery backup on essentials is usually sufficient.

### What should backup power cover in a villa?
Internet, lighting, water pump, fridge and one or two bedroom aircon units for four to six hours. The pool pump and water heating can wait.

### Why does my villa power trip when it is full?
Usually an undersized PLN connection rather than an outage. Check the contracted capacity against what the property runs and upgrade before considering backup.

### Where should a generator be placed?
Where noise does not reach bedrooms, the pool deck or neighbours, with an automatic transfer switch so it starts without someone being present.
