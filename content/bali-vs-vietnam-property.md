Vietnam is the newest name on Southeast Asian shortlists. Da Nang and Nha Trang sell beach apartments, Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi sell city condos, and prices still look low next to Bali. The rules are very different, so the comparison needs care.

## What a foreigner can own in Vietnam

- **Apartments in approved projects only.** A foreigner can own an apartment for **50 years**, with one extension possible. The project must be on the approved list for foreign buyers. Da Nang, Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City publish these lists.
- **A 30% cap per building.** Foreigners together can hold at most 30% of the apartments in a building, shared by all foreign buyers.
- **Houses.** There has been a separate limit for landed houses per ward. A 2026 draft would **remove landed houses** for foreigners and keep apartments only. Check the status before you look at a house.
- **No land.** As in Indonesia, land is not owned by foreigners.

## What a foreigner can hold in Bali

No ownership, of any kind. You hold a **lease**, usually 25 to 30 years with an extension option, or **Hak Pakai** with residency, or **HGB through a PT PMA** company. In return, Bali offers villas, land and freedom over what you build, which Vietnam's apartment-only model does not.

## The product is different

**Vietnam** is mostly an apartment market in large projects by big developers. You buy a unit in a tower, with a pink book (the ownership certificate) if all goes well. Certificates for foreign buyers have historically taken a long time to issue in some projects; ask how many foreign owners in the building already have theirs.

**Bali** is mostly a villa and land market, built by small and mid-sized developers. You get more space and more control, and more risk if you do not check the land, the permits and the developer.

## Rental and yields

- **Bali** villas earn from short-term holiday rentals, with high gross yields and high costs. A well-run villa nets roughly **7 to 14%**.
- **Da Nang and Nha Trang** apartments rent to tourists and long-stay visitors, with strong seasons and a lot of new supply. **Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi** are long-term rental markets with lower yields and less volatility.

Vietnam is cheaper to enter. Bali's holiday demand is deeper and spread over more source countries.

## Getting your money out

Vietnam has foreign exchange controls: to send sale or rental income back home, you need to show where the money came from, so bring the purchase money in through a proper bank channel and keep every record. Indonesia also requires documented flows, but repatriation of lease income is simpler in practice.

## Which I would choose

- **You want a cheap entry, an apartment, and ownership for 50 years:** Vietnam, in an approved project with certificates already issued to foreigners.
- **You want a villa with real holiday demand, and you will use it:** Bali, with a long lease, a written extension and a checked owner.

## Common questions

### Can foreigners buy property in Da Nang?
Yes, apartments in approved projects, for 50 years with one possible extension, within a 30% foreign cap per building. Da Nang publishes the list of projects open to foreign buyers.

### Is Vietnam property freehold for foreigners?
No. It is ownership for a fixed term of 50 years, with one extension possible. Vietnamese citizens get long-term rights; foreigners do not.

### Can a foreigner buy a house in Vietnam?
There has been a limited quota for landed houses, but a 2026 draft would remove houses for foreigners. Treat houses as not available until the final rule is clear.

### Is Bali or Vietnam cheaper?
Vietnam apartments are usually cheaper per square metre. Bali villas cost more but give land use, privacy and stronger holiday rental demand.

### Which is safer for a foreign buyer?
Vietnam gives a certificate in your name, if the project is approved and the certificate is issued. Bali gives no certificate in your name, so the lease and the due diligence must be right.
