Nusa Lembongan sits between Bali and Nusa Penida, connected to Nusa Ceningan by a yellow bridge. Small enough to cross in twenty minutes.

## What is there

Surf breaks, mangroves, diving, seaweed farming that has largely given way to tourism, and a compact accommodation market of villas and small resorts.

Day-trippers from Bali dominate arrivals. The overnight market is real but much smaller than the visitor numbers suggest.

## The constraints

| Constraint | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| Water | Limited. Desalination or shipped supply is normal |
| Power | Improved via undersea cable, still fragile |
| Waste | A visible problem on a small island |
| Construction | Every material arrives by boat, raising cost and time |
| Medical | Basic. Anything serious means a boat to Bali |
| Transport | No cars in most areas. Scooters and small trucks |
| Resale | Very thin |

That last row is the one buyers underweight. The pool of people who want to own on Lembongan and can lawfully do so is small, and it does not grow quickly.

## The demand pattern

Heavily seasonal and weather-dependent. Rough seas cancel crossings, which cancels arrivals you had already counted on. A property underwritten on mainland occupancy assumptions will disappoint.

## Who it suits

Operators who will be present and run the business themselves, with a long horizon and no need for liquidity.

It does not suit passive investors, and it does not suit anyone who may need to sell within five years.

## What to verify

Water supply and its monthly cost in full occupancy. Power arrangements and backup. Title, through a mainland notary you appointed. Access rights. Boat logistics for construction. And realistically, who buys it from you.

## Where people actually buy

**Jungutbatu** on the northwest coast has the harbour, most of the accommodation and the best infrastructure.

**Mushroom Bay** is smaller and more resort-oriented.

**The southern cliffs** have the dramatic outlooks and the hardest access, water and construction problems.

## The seaweed economy

Lembongan ran on seaweed farming before tourism, and parts of it still do. That matters because it shaped land ownership patterns: many parcels are family-held, often through inheritance without formal registration.

Title verification here needs a mainland notary and more time than a Bali equivalent, and unregistered land should be treated as a much longer process rather than a discount.

## Common questions

### Can foreigners buy property on Nusa Lembongan?
Yes, under the same national rules as Bali: leasehold, Hak Pakai with residency, or HGB via a PT PMA.

### Is Nusa Lembongan a good investment?
It suits present, hands-on operators with a long horizon. Thin resale, seasonal weather-dependent demand and hard infrastructure limits make it unsuitable for passive investors.

### Is there fresh water on Nusa Lembongan?
Limited. Properties commonly rely on desalination or shipped water, which is a real recurring operating cost.

### How do you get to Nusa Lembongan?
Fast boat from Sanur, roughly 30 minutes, subject to sea conditions which do cancel crossings.

### Is it expensive to build on Nusa Lembongan?
Considerably more than the mainland. Every material arrives by boat and onward transport is limited.
