Cleanliness is the single most consistently reviewed thing about a villa, it is the cheapest category to score well in, and Bali's climate makes it considerably harder than the equivalent property at home.

## Why it is harder here

**Humidity produces mould**, continuously, in wardrobes, on ceilings, behind furniture and in anything that does not dry. Cleaning it off is not the same as preventing it, and it comes back.

**Insects.** Ants find anything, geckos leave droppings, and mosquitoes are what guests actually mention.

**Open buildings.** Bali villas are designed to let air through, which also lets in dust, leaves and everything else.

**The garden grows constantly** and drops into the pool and onto terraces.

**Salt near the coast** corrodes fittings and marks surfaces.

A villa cleaned to a home standard once a week will not present well. It needs a schedule built for the environment.

## The turnover standard

**Every surface**, including the ones guests do not see, because what is behind a door is what a guest opens.

**Bathrooms properly** — grout, drains, behind the toilet, the shower screen. Bathrooms are what guests judge and what reviews mention.

**Linen changed and properly dried.** Damp-smelling linen in a humid climate is the most common complaint in this category and it comes from inadequate drying rather than inadequate washing. **Three sets per bed** is the working figure so nothing is rushed.

**Kitchen**, including inside the fridge, the kettle, the coffee equipment and the cupboards.

**Outdoor areas**: terrace swept, furniture wiped, cushions dry and not mouldy, pool deck clean.

**Pool** clean and clear, which is separate from housekeeping and should be verified at the same time.

**Aircon filters**, on a schedule. Dirty filters reduce output and produce the "aircon didn't work" review.

**Restocking**: consumables, toilet paper, water, basics.

## The weekly and monthly layer

Turnover cleaning is not enough on its own.

**Weekly**: deep-clean one area on rotation, check for mould in wardrobes and behind furniture, wipe down anything metal near the coast.

**Monthly**: aircon filters, windows, high surfaces, under furniture, drains, and a proper inspection of what is deteriorating.

**Quarterly**: soft furnishings cleaned, mattresses turned and checked, anything upholstered assessed.

## Mould specifically

The thing that separates a well-run Bali villa from a badly-run one.

**Airflow.** Wardrobes and cupboards left closed in an empty villa grow mould. Leave them open between guests.

**Dehumidifiers or moisture absorbers** in enclosed storage.

**Run the aircon periodically** in an empty villa, which dries the building.

**Fix water problems immediately.** A slow leak is a mould problem before it is a building problem.

**Check behind and under things**, because that is where it starts and where nobody looks until a guest does.

Cleaning mould off is treating a symptom. Ventilation and drying is the fix.

## Between-guest gaps and empty periods

A villa left closed for three weeks in February smells different from one attended.

**Someone should attend regularly** even when empty — which the pool and garden service does naturally, and which is one more argument for keeping them running through quiet periods.

**Open it up** before a guest arrives, not on the morning.

## Staffing it

**Directly employed** housekeeping brings the full Indonesian employment framework — BPJS, THR, overtime, leave and the termination provisions foreign employers consistently underestimate.

**Through a management company or contracted per turnover**, the obligations sit with them.

For most owners with one or two properties, contracted is the better trade.

**Whichever way: pay above the minimum.** The difference between adequate and excellent housekeeping costs little in absolute terms and shows up directly in reviews and therefore in ranking and rate.

## Verifying it

**Photographs after every turnover**, sent to you or to the manager. This takes the housekeeper two minutes and it changes behaviour.

**A written checklist**, signed off. Not because people are dishonest, but because a list is how standards survive staff changes.

**Visit unannounced** once or twice a year. What the villa looks like when nobody expected you is its real standard.

**Read your reviews for the word "clean"** and treat any mention as a signal rather than an outlier.

## Common questions

### How often should a Bali villa be cleaned?
Full turnover clean between every guest, a weekly rotating deep clean, monthly aircon filters and high surfaces, and quarterly soft furnishings and mattresses.

### Why do Bali villas smell musty?
Inadequate drying of linen and poor airflow in enclosed spaces. Humidity produces mould in wardrobes and behind furniture, and ventilation rather than cleaning is the fix.

### How many sets of linen does a villa need?
Three per bed, so nothing has to be rushed through washing and drying between guests in a climate where drying is slow.

### Should I employ housekeeping directly or contract it?
For most owners with one or two properties, contracting through a manager or per turnover is better, since the employment obligations and termination exposure sit with them.

### How do I verify housekeeping standards remotely?
Photographs after every turnover, a written signed checklist, and one or two unannounced visits a year. Reviews mentioning cleanliness should be treated as a signal.
