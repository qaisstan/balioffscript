Families are one of the largest segments in the Bali market, particularly Australian families in school holidays. The properties that attract them are the ones that answer the safety question before it is asked.

## The pool, which is the whole subject

Drowning is the risk that matters, and Bali villa pools are frequently designed without it in mind.

**What helps:**

**Visibility from the living space.** A parent in the kitchen who can see the pool is the single most effective safety feature a villa can have, and it is a design decision rather than a product.

**A clearly defined edge**, and a shallow area where small children can stand.

**Depth markings**, so guests know.

**Good lighting**, for evening.

**Non-slip surfaces** around the pool, which matter as much as the water does.

**A gate or fence**, which is the explicit option. It affects photographs, and a meaningful number of families search for it specifically. Whether you install one is a judgement about your market — but **state the position clearly in the listing** either way. A family arriving to discover an unfenced pool they assumed was fenced is a bad outcome for everyone.

**Pool alarms and safety nets** exist and are used by some operators.

## The other risks

**Steps and level changes**, which Bali villas have a great many of, frequently without handrails, and often wet.

**Open sides and balconies**, which are part of the architecture and a hazard with small children. Gaps in balustrades matter.

**Sharp landscaping** — stone edges, decorative features.

**Electrical**, where older installations frequently lack earthing and safety cutouts. This is worth checking regardless of families.

**Hot water**, at a temperature that can scald.

**Mosquitoes**, with dengue present in Bali, which is a genuine health issue rather than a nuisance. Eliminate standing water, provide repellent, consider screens.

**The beach.** Many Bali beaches have strong currents and no lifeguard. If yours is one, say so in the listing.

**Traffic**, if the villa opens directly onto a road.

## Public liability, which is not optional

Someone injured at your pool is the largest single exposure a villa owner has, and it is larger than property damage by some margin.

**Public liability insurance** with a realistic limit, on a policy that covers **commercial letting**.

A property insured as a private residence while taking paying guests is the most common reason Bali claims fail, and it fails at precisely the moment you need it.

**Read the exclusions.** Water-related incidents, guest activities and event use are all areas where policies differ.

## What families actually look for

Beyond safety, the things that win family bookings and that most villas do not mention:

**Cots and high chairs**, provided.

**A pool with a shallow area.**

**A kitchen a family can actually cook in**, because families cook.

**Enough bedrooms configured usefully** — a room children can share, near the parents.

**Somewhere shaded** that is not the pool.

**A washing machine.**

**Calm water nearby.** Sanur, Nusa Dua, Jimbaran, Melasti and Sawangan have swimmable beaches; much of the west coast does not. This is a genuine differentiator and it is why the eastern Bukit and the southeast serve families better than the surf coast.

**Distance from the airport**, because a long transfer with small children after a long flight is remembered.

## Being explicit is the differentiator

The single most useful thing an owner can do is **say what the property is**.

"Unfenced pool, shallow end at the western edge, visible from the living area and the kitchen" tells a family everything they need to decide.

Silence forces them to guess, and families guessing choose the villa that told them.

The same applies to steps, balconies, beach conditions and road access. A property honestly described attracts the families it suits and avoids the ones it does not, which is better for your reviews than a booking from a family who arrives and finds it unsuitable.

## Common questions

### Should a Bali villa pool be fenced?
It is a judgement about your market, and a meaningful number of families search for it specifically. Whichever you choose, state the position clearly in the listing rather than leaving it ambiguous.

### What is the most effective pool safety feature?
Visibility from the living space, so a parent can see the pool from where they are. It is a design decision rather than a product and it does more than any accessory.

### Do I need public liability insurance for a Bali villa?
Yes. Someone injured at your pool is the largest exposure you have, and the policy must cover commercial letting — a villa insured as a private residence is the most common reason claims fail.

### Which Bali areas suit families best?
Those with calm swimmable water — Sanur, Nusa Dua, Jimbaran and the eastern Bukit around Melasti and Sawangan — rather than the surf coast.

### What do families look for in a Bali villa?
A shallow pool area, visibility from the living space, cots and high chairs, a usable kitchen, a washing machine, shade away from the pool and a short airport transfer.
