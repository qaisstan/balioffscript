The default assumption is nightly rental, because that is what the projections show. It is worth testing.

## The two models

| | Short-term | Long-term |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rate | High per night | Lower per month |
| Occupancy | Seasonal, 50 to 75 percent typical | Effectively 100 percent while leased |
| Costs | Staff, laundry, consumables, management, commission | Minimal |
| Wear | Heavy, constant turnover | Much lower |
| Licensing | Accommodation licence required | Different regime, generally simpler |
| Management | Intensive | Light |
| Cash flow | Lumpy, seasonal | Predictable, often paid a year up front |

## The arithmetic that surprises people

Short-term gross revenue is genuinely higher. What closes the gap is everything underneath it.

Platform commission, management fee, cleaning and laundry per stay, consumables, full-time staff, higher utilities, faster wear, a shorter refurbishment cycle, marketing, and the months where occupancy is 40 percent rather than 75.

Long-term letting has almost none of that. A twelve-month lease paid annually in advance, which is normal here, produces predictable net income with a fraction of the operational load.

Run both models on your actual property with honest numbers and the gap is often much smaller than the marketing suggests. On a generic villa in an oversupplied corridor, long-term can win outright.

## The licensing dimension

Nightly accommodation is a licensed commercial activity, and Bali closed the villa and homestay classifications to new foreign-owned company registration on 22 July 2026.

If your property cannot lawfully operate nightly, long-term letting is not the fallback, it is the business. Price the property accordingly rather than on income it cannot legally earn.

## What long-term costs you

Flexibility. You cannot use the villa yourself. You have less control over condition. Tenants live differently from guests, and a year of daily living leaves different wear than a series of three-night stays.

You also give up the upside in a strong season.

## The hybrid

Some owners run long-term through the wet months and short-term through the peak. It captures some of both, at the cost of the operational overhead of running two models and the risk of gaps between them.

It works best where the property is genuinely in demand for both, which is not everywhere.

## Who should choose which

**Short-term** suits differentiated property in proven locations, with a lawful licence, an owner willing to run it as a business, and enough margin to survive a soft season.

**Long-term** suits generic property, oversupplied corridors, absentee owners, and anyone who values predictability over headline yield.

## Common questions

### Is Airbnb or long-term rental better in Bali?
Short-term produces higher gross revenue and much higher costs. After vacancy, staff, management and commission, long-term often nets similarly with far less work and less risk.

### How much can you rent a villa for long term in Bali?
It varies by area and specification, and annual leases are frequently paid in advance. Canggu long-term rentals start from around IDR 17.5M a month and rise sharply near the beach.

### Do you need a licence for long-term rental in Bali?
Long-term leasing sits under a different regime from nightly accommodation and is generally simpler. Confirm the position for your property and structure.

### Is long-term rental safer than Airbnb in Bali?
Operationally and from a licensing standpoint, generally yes. It also removes exposure to tourism seasonality and to oversupply in the nightly market.

### Can I do both short and long term?
Some owners run long-term in the wet season and short-term at peak. It captures some of both but doubles the operational complexity.
