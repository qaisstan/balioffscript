A leasehold loses value every year by design. Making money on one means the income has to outrun that decay, and most projections never mention it.

## The core arithmetic

Two things happen simultaneously:

**The asset decays.** A 30-year lease with 30 years left is worth more than the same lease with 20 left. The decline accelerates as the term shortens, because your buyer inherits the expiry.

**The property produces income.** Rent, nightly or annual.

Your actual return is the income **minus** the value the lease shed that year. A villa yielding 6 percent net on a lease losing 4 percent of its value annually is not a 6 percent investment.

Almost nobody models the second half.

## The four ways money is actually made

**One: income that clears the decay.** Requires strong, licensed, differentiated performance. Generic villas in oversupplied corridors frequently do not manage it.

**Two: buy long, sell shorter but still long.** Buy at 30 years, sell at 22. The decay between those points is shallower than between 12 and 4. Most of the value loss happens at the end.

**Three: improve the asset.** Buy a tired leasehold, refurbish, and capture the gap between the discount you bought at and what a functioning property commands.

**Four: fix the paperwork.** Buy a leasehold discounted for a weak extension clause, missing permits or a licence problem, then resolve it. This is the most reliable value creation available in this market and the least attempted, because it requires knowing what is broken.

## What kills leasehold returns

- A short remaining term, bought at near-freehold pricing
- **Prioritas** rather than **jaminan** on the extension
- Extension rights that do not pass to your buyer
- Assignment requiring unqualified consent, or an uncapped fee
- No operating licence, so the income you modelled cannot lawfully exist
- The building reverting at expiry with no compensation clause

## The honest rule

**Buy term, sell term.** Your entry price should reflect the years you are getting, and your exit price will reflect the years you are handing over. Everything else is operations.

## Common questions

### Can you make money on a leasehold in Bali?
Yes, when income clears the annual decay in lease value, or when you buy a discounted lease and fix what caused the discount.

### Does a leasehold lose value every year?
Yes, and not evenly. The decline accelerates as the term shortens because your buyer inherits the expiry.

### What is the best leasehold strategy in Bali?
Buying long, holding while income exceeds the decay, and selling while the term is still long enough to have a real buyer pool.

### Why do leaseholds sell at a discount?
Short terms, weak extension clauses, non-transferable rights and licence problems. Each is a quantifiable deduction and some are fixable.

### Should I buy a short lease cheaply?
Only if you have a verified path to extending it. Otherwise you are buying the steepest part of the decay curve.
