The comparison is usually made on nightly rate, which is the least important variable.

## Where each wins

| | Villa | Apartment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nightly rate | Higher | Lower |
| Occupancy | More seasonal | Steadier |
| Running costs | High: staff, pool, garden | Lower, often shared |
| Entry price | Higher | Lower |
| Break-even occupancy | Higher | Lower |
| Licensing route for a new foreign-owned company | **Closed** for villa classification | Apartment hotel remains open |
| Exit liquidity | Deeper buyer pool | Narrower in most Bali locations |

## The licensing difference is the big one

Since 22 July 2026, the **villa classification (55203) is closed to new PT PMA registration** in Bali. **Apartment hotel (55204) remains open, at any size.**

That is not a small administrative distinction. For a foreign investor forming a new company to operate nightly rentals, it changes which asset types have a lawful operating route at all. [The detail is here](/company/pt-pma-kbli-closure-bali/).

## The cost difference compounds

A villa carries its own staff, pool and garden. An apartment usually shares building costs across units, which lowers the fixed base, and the fixed base is what sets [break-even occupancy](/rental/bali-villa-break-even-occupancy/).

Lower fixed costs mean a lower break-even, which means more tolerance for a bad season. That resilience rarely appears in a comparison built on nightly rate.

## Where villas still win

Rate and demand. A well-located, well-run villa with a pool commands a premium an apartment cannot, and Bali's guest expectation is villa-shaped.

For a buyer who will use the property personally, that matters beyond the numbers.

## How to actually decide

Run both through the [calculator](/calculator/) with realistic figures for each. The property type selector adjusts the cost assumptions. Compare **net yield after amortisation** and **break-even occupancy**, not headline rates.

Then answer the question that comes first: on the specific plot or unit you are looking at, is nightly rental permitted, and does the operator hold a classification that covers it?

Neither asset type is a good investment if the answer is no.

## The structural difference

| | Villa | Apartment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership route for a foreigner | Leasehold, or HGB via PT PMA | Hak Pakai on strata title, where you qualify |
| Entry price | Higher | Lower |
| Guest demand | Deep. Most visitors want a private pool | Thinner in a villa-led market |
| Nightly rate | Higher | Lower |
| Running costs | Staff, pool, garden, high maintenance | Service charge, far less to maintain |
| Management intensity | High | Low |
| Resale pool | Deep in the main corridors | Shallow |
| Land value exposure | Yes, and it is the durable part | Minimal |

## The case for apartments

Strata-titled apartments are the simplest lawful ownership route for a qualifying foreigner: Hak Pakai, registered, in your own name, without a company. That alone removes a large amount of the complexity that catches villa buyers.

They are also far cheaper to hold. No staff, no pool chemicals, no garden that grows back within a fortnight, and a building envelope somebody else maintains.

## The case against

Bali's tourism demand is built around private villas. An apartment competes for a smaller slice of guests, at lower rates, in a market where the alternative is a private pool for a modest premium.

Resale is the harder problem. The local buyer pool for apartments is small and the foreign buyer pool mostly wants land. That combination makes exit slower and pricing weaker than the same money in a well-chosen villa.

## Land is the part that lasts

The durable value in Bali property is land, and an apartment gives you almost none of it. You own a right to a volume of air inside somebody else's building, in a climate that treats buildings harshly.

Over a long hold, that difference compounds against you.

## Who each suits

An apartment suits a buyer who wants a lawful, low-maintenance base with modest capital, and who values simplicity over return.

A villa suits a buyer with the capital to buy well-located land, the appetite to run an operating business, and a horizon long enough to make the land value matter.

## Common questions

### Is an apartment or a villa a better investment in Bali?
Villas dominate the nightly rental market and command higher rates. Apartments are cheaper to enter, cheaper to maintain and easier to hold passively, but the guest demand is thinner because most visitors come here for a private pool.

### Can foreigners buy apartments in Bali?
Yes. Strata-titled apartments can be held under Hak Pakai by a foreigner meeting the residency and minimum-value requirements, which makes them one of the simpler ownership routes.

### Do apartments in Bali hold their value?
Resale depth is thinner than for villas because the local buyer pool is small and the foreign buyer pool mostly wants land. Price your exit conservatively.

### What are the running costs of an apartment versus a villa?
An apartment carries service charges but no pool, garden, or full staff. A villa typically runs staff, pool chemicals, garden and higher repair frequency, which is why gross and net yield diverge so sharply on villas.
