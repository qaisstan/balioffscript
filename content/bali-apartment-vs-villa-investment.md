The comparison is usually made on nightly rate, which is the least important variable.

## Where each wins

| | Villa | Apartment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nightly rate | Higher | Lower |
| Occupancy | More seasonal | Steadier |
| Running costs | High: staff, pool, garden | Lower, often shared |
| Entry price | Higher | Lower |
| Break-even occupancy | Higher | Lower |
| Licensing route for a new foreign-owned company | **Closed** for villa classification | Apartment hotel remains open |
| Exit liquidity | Deeper buyer pool | Narrower in most Bali locations |

## The licensing difference is the big one

Since 22 July 2026, the **villa classification (55203) is closed to new PT PMA registration** in Bali. **Apartment hotel (55204) remains open, at any size.**

That is not a small administrative distinction. For a foreign investor forming a new company to operate nightly rentals, it changes which asset types have a lawful operating route at all. [The detail is here](/company/pt-pma-kbli-closure-bali/).

## The cost difference compounds

A villa carries its own staff, pool and garden. An apartment usually shares building costs across units, which lowers the fixed base, and the fixed base is what sets [break-even occupancy](/rental/bali-villa-break-even-occupancy/).

Lower fixed costs mean a lower break-even, which means more tolerance for a bad season. That resilience rarely appears in a comparison built on nightly rate.

## Where villas still win

Rate and demand. A well-located, well-run villa with a pool commands a premium an apartment cannot, and Bali's guest expectation is villa-shaped.

For a buyer who will use the property personally, that matters beyond the numbers.

## How to actually decide

Run both through the [calculator](/calculator/) with realistic figures for each. The property type selector adjusts the cost assumptions. Compare **net yield after amortisation** and **break-even occupancy**, not headline rates.

Then answer the question that comes first: on the specific plot or unit you are looking at, is nightly rental permitted, and does the operator hold a classification that covers it?

Neither asset type is a good investment if the answer is no.
