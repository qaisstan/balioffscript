Bali sits on an active plate boundary beneath an active volcano, and both facts have affected property owners within living memory. This belongs in a purchase decision rather than in the background.

## The seismic position

Indonesia sits on the Ring of Fire, at the convergence of major tectonic plates. Bali and the surrounding islands are seismically active.

The **2018 Lombok earthquakes** are the most relevant recent example: a sequence of significant events that caused extensive damage and loss of life on Lombok and the Gili Islands, with the Gilis evacuated and visitor numbers collapsing for a prolonged period. Bali itself experienced shaking and some damage.

Bali has been fortunate relative to its neighbours in recent decades. Fortunate is not the same as safe, and the geology does not change.

## Mount Agung

An active volcano in the east of the island, in Karangasem regency.

The **2017 to 2019 eruption sequence** raised alert levels, triggered evacuations of tens of thousands of people, and **closed Ngurah Rai airport repeatedly** as ash drifted across flight paths.

For property owners the airport closures were the significant part. No flights means no guests, island-wide, regardless of where your property is or how far from the volcano it sits. Occupancy collapsed across Bali, not only in the east.

Hazard zones around Agung are mapped and building within them is restricted. If you are buying in Karangasem or the eastern foothills, establish where the parcel sits relative to those zones before committing — it is specific, checkable information and it is not always volunteered.

## What this means practically

**Airport dependency is the real exposure.** Bali has one international gateway, and volcanic ash closes it. That is a systemic risk to every rental property on the island and it cannot be diversified away by choosing a better area.

**Model a bad year.** Bali has had several — the bombings, the Agung sequence, the pandemic. An investment that only works at seventy-five percent occupancy every year has no margin for an event that has happened repeatedly.

**Building standards matter.** Indonesian building regulation includes seismic requirements, and enforcement varies. A building constructed to proper standards performs very differently in an earthquake from one that was not, and much older Bali stock predates current expectations.

Ask your surveyor about structural adequacy specifically, not just condition.

## Insurance, which is where most people are exposed

This is the part that catches owners.

**Earthquake and volcanic cover is frequently excluded**, offered as a priced extension, or subject to a large deductible. Read the policy exclusions rather than the summary.

**Tsunami** is commonly excluded on coastal property.

**Landslide**, relevant on sloped land in Ubud, the Bukit, the highlands and the east, is frequently excluded too.

**Business interruption** following an insured event may be available as an extension, and is worth considering on an income property — though note that an airport closure that empties the island is not damage to your property and generally will not trigger a property policy at all.

Get at least three quotes, read the exclusions in full once, and know exactly what you bought.

## Where risk is higher

**Near Agung**, in Karangasem and the eastern foothills, where volcanic hazard zoning applies directly.

**On steep slopes** in Ubud, the highlands and the east, where landslide risk is real and locals know the history no document records.

**On the coast**, for tsunami exposure.

**On the Gilis and Lombok**, where the 2018 sequence caused direct damage.

**Older buildings** anywhere, built before current seismic expectations.

## What to actually do

Check volcanic hazard zoning if buying in the east.

Ask about slope stability and landslide history for any sloped parcel, locally as well as from documents.

Have structural adequacy assessed, not just condition.

Price seismic and volcanic insurance cover deliberately, and read the exclusions.

Model a year with a significant disruption, and check the investment still survives it.

Keep a capital reserve, which serves this purpose among others.

## The honest framing

None of this is a reason to avoid Bali. People have built and owned property here for generations and the overwhelming majority of years pass without incident.

It is a reason to know what you are exposed to, to insure deliberately rather than by default, and to underwrite an investment that can survive a bad year rather than one that only works if every year is good.

Karangasem and the eastern foothills near Mount Agung, where hazard zones are mapped and building within them is restricted.
