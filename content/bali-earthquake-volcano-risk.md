Indonesia sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, and Bali is part of that. This is not a reason to avoid the island. It is a reason to make a few decisions deliberately rather than by default.

## The actual risk

**Earthquakes.** Felt tremors are not unusual. Damaging earthquakes are infrequent but real: the 2018 Lombok sequence caused serious damage and fatalities on the neighbouring island and was strongly felt in Bali.

**Volcanoes.** Mount Agung in the east is active, with significant eruptive episodes in 2017 and 2018 that triggered evacuations within the exclusion zone and repeatedly closed Ngurah Rai airport. Mount Batur is also active.

**Tsunami.** A low-probability, high-consequence risk affecting low-lying coastal areas.

## What it means for buying

**Location.** Distance from Agung's exclusion zones matters in the east. Elevation matters on the coast. Neither should dominate the decision, but both belong in it.

**Construction standards.** This is the item within your control. Reinforced concrete frames built to proper standards perform well. Poorly detailed reinforcement, weak connections and unengineered additions do not.

Ask who the structural engineer was and whether the design was engineered rather than copied. On an existing building, a structural survey is worth the cost.

**Extensions.** Unpermitted additions are frequently the weakest part of a building, because they were built without engineering. That is a seismic problem as well as a licensing one.

## Insurance

Earthquake and volcanic cover is sometimes included, sometimes an extension, sometimes excluded. Confirm the treatment explicitly and in writing rather than assuming it sits inside a standard policy.

Insurers can also decline where PBG and SLF are missing, which turns a permit gap into an uninsured seismic exposure.

## Airport closure

The practical consequence most owners actually experience is ash closing the airport. It has happened repeatedly and it strands guests and cancels bookings.

For a rental business, that is a cash flow event rather than a safety one. It is another argument for a cash buffer rather than an operation run to the last rupiah.

## Perspective

Millions of people live here safely. The risk is real, infrequent, and largely managed through where you build and how well. Treat it as a design and insurance question rather than a reason for anxiety.

## Common questions

### Does Bali get earthquakes?
Yes. Felt tremors are common and damaging earthquakes are infrequent but real. The 2018 Lombok sequence caused serious damage nearby.

### Is Mount Agung still active?
Yes. It erupted significantly in 2017 and 2018, causing evacuations within the exclusion zone and repeated airport closures.

### Are Bali buildings earthquake resistant?
Properly engineered reinforced concrete performs well. Unengineered construction and unpermitted extensions are the weak points, which is another reason to verify PBG and SLF.

### Does villa insurance cover earthquakes in Bali?
Sometimes, sometimes as an extension, sometimes not at all. Confirm explicitly, and note that missing permits can void cover entirely.

### Should volcanic risk change where I buy?
It should inform choices in the east near Agung and on low-lying coast. For most of the island it is a construction and insurance question rather than a location one.
