Bali villa architecture is built around level changes, open edges, outdoor steps and polished stone. It is beautiful and it excludes a growing segment of the market almost entirely.

## Who is excluded

**Older travellers**, who are a large and growing share of the Australian and European markets.

**Multi-generational family groups**, which are one of the strongest reasons a group books a villa rather than hotel rooms. A family of nine including grandparents cannot use a property the grandparents cannot move around.

**Anyone with a temporary injury**, which in a place with Bali's road injury rate is not rare.

**Anyone with a permanent mobility limitation.**

Almost every villa listing is silent on this, which forces those guests to guess. Guests guessing book the property that told them.

## The specific obstacles in Bali villas

**Steps everywhere**, frequently without handrails and often outdoors and wet.

**Level changes between rooms**, which are a design feature and a trip hazard.

**Bathrooms with a step into the shower**, high thresholds and nothing to hold.

**Polished stone and terrazzo**, which is beautiful and slippery when wet.

**Open edges and balconies**, part of the architecture and a hazard.

**Pool entry by ladder** rather than steps.

**Bedrooms upstairs**, in a country with a three-storey height limit that pushes villas outward and upward on small plots.

**Uneven access** from the road to the property, and lanes without footpaths.

**Distance from medical care**, which matters more for this guest.

## What makes a villa genuinely usable

**One bedroom and bathroom on the entry level**, which is the single most important thing and which many villas could provide with a layout decision at design stage.

**A step-free route** from where a car can stop to that bedroom.

**Handrails** on any steps, internal and external.

**A level or ramped shower entry**, with a grab rail and somewhere to sit.

**Non-slip surfaces** in wet areas and around the pool, which matters for every guest and is cheap.

**Pool steps** rather than only a ladder, with a handrail.

**Good lighting**, including at night on any level change.

**Door widths** that a walking frame or wheelchair clears, which is a drawing decision costing nothing at design stage.

**Somewhere to sit** in the outdoor areas, not only loungers.

## The commercial case

This is an **underserved segment with money**, and the properties that serve it have very little competition.

**Multi-generational groups book large villas for longer periods**, frequently in shoulder season because they are not tied to school holidays — which is exactly when you need filling.

**Older travellers stay longer** and are less price sensitive.

**They book direct and return**, once they have found somewhere that works.

A four or five bedroom villa with a ground-floor accessible bedroom and bathroom, in an area with calm water and reasonable proximity to medical care — Sanur, Nusa Dua, Jimbaran — is a property competing in a very thin field.

## Being explicit is most of it

**Say what the property is.** "Fourteen steps from the parking area, all bedrooms upstairs" is more useful than silence and it costs you only the bookings you would have lost anyway on arrival.

**Photograph the access**, not just the pool.

**State step counts and level changes** in the listing.

**If you are accessible, say so prominently**, because guests search on it and almost nothing in Bali comes up.

A property honestly described attracts the guests it suits, which produces better reviews than a booking from someone who arrives and cannot manage it.

## Retrofitting

Some of it is cheap: handrails, non-slip treatment, better lighting, pool steps, a shower seat and grab rail.

Some of it is not: moving a bedroom to the ground floor, widening doors, ramping an approach.

**At design stage almost all of it costs nothing.** A ground-floor bedroom with a level bathroom and a step-free route to it is a layout decision, not a budget item — and it is the decision that opens the segment.

## The insurance point

Someone falling at your property is a **public liability** matter, and it is the largest exposure a villa owner has.

**Check the policy covers commercial letting.** A villa insured as a private residence while taking paying guests is the most common reason Bali claims fail.

## Common questions

### Are Bali villas accessible for older guests?
Very few are. Steps, level changes, high shower thresholds, polished stone and upstairs bedrooms are standard, and most listings say nothing about it either way.

### What makes a villa usable for less mobile guests?
A bedroom and bathroom on the entry level with a step-free route from the car, handrails, level shower entry, non-slip surfaces, pool steps and adequate door widths.

### Is accessibility a commercial opportunity in Bali?
Yes. Multi-generational groups book large villas for longer periods in shoulder season, older travellers stay longer, and almost nothing in Bali competes for them.

### Should I mention steps in my villa listing?
Yes. Stating step counts and level changes attracts the guests the property suits and avoids arrivals from guests who cannot manage it, which protects your reviews.

### How much does it cost to make a villa more accessible?
Handrails, non-slip treatment, lighting, pool steps and a shower seat are cheap. Moving a bedroom downstairs is not — which is why it is a design decision rather than a retrofit.
