Bali's property ecosystem has several roles that look similar from outside and have entirely different incentives. Knowing who is paid for what is the most useful thing you can understand about the market.

## The agent

**Paid a commission on a completed sale**, normally by the seller, at three to five percent.

**Works for the seller.** Almost always, even when they found the property for you, showed it to you and answered your questions helpfully.

**Not licensed.** There is no licensing regime for real estate agents in Indonesia — no professional standards body, no mandatory disclosure, no indemnity regime.

That does not make agents dishonest. Most are not. It means **the questions you do not ask do not get answered**, and that nobody in the room is working solely for you unless you engaged them.

**What to do:** use agents to find property, and verify everything independently. Ask directly who they act for and how they are paid. And never accept the notary they introduce.

## The notary

**The only genuinely independent check available to you**, and only if you engaged them.

A notary introduced by the agent has an ongoing commercial relationship with someone paid on the sale. Engaging your own costs a little more and it is the single most important decision in the transaction.

## The property manager

**Paid a percentage of gross revenue**, fifteen to twenty-five percent, sometimes with fixed fees on top or a markup on maintenance.

**Incentive:** revenue, which is broadly aligned with yours. The misalignment is in **effort** — a manager earns more from an extra booking and considerably more from an easier life across a portfolio, and the marginal booking on your property is the one that gets dropped first.

That is the mechanism behind the most common failure in Bali: a manager who performs adequately at first and quietly drifts, while nobody tells the owner.

**What to do:** compare your achieved rate and occupancy against four comparable villas each quarter. Two hours, four times a year, and it is the highest-return work an owner does.

## The buyer's agent

**Paid by you**, to find and assess property on your behalf.

Genuinely useful and uncommon in Bali. The value is having someone whose payment does not depend on you buying a specific property — which is only true if the fee structure actually works that way.

**Ask how they are paid.** If they receive anything from the seller's side, they are not a buyer's agent whatever the title says.

## The developer

**Paid by selling units**, frequently off-plan, frequently through a company that holds little.

Indonesia has **no developer licensing regime and no statutory deposit protection scheme**. Whatever protects your money is contractual and frequently there is nothing.

**What to do:** visit completed projects a few years old, find past buyers yourself, check whether the PBG is issued rather than applied for, and tie payments to milestones inspected by someone you appointed.

## The consultant

Company formation, tax, immigration. Paid for the work in scope, which is the important qualifier.

**Ask directly what is and is not included.** Many handle the filings they were asked to handle and not the ones nobody mentioned — the quarterly LKPM, the regional accommodation tax, the annual shareholder resolution. "We handle your company" does not mean all of it.

## The honest map

**Nobody in a Bali property transaction works for you by default.**

The agent works for the seller. The developer works for themselves. The manager works for revenue across a portfolio. The consultant works to a scope.

The people who work for you are **the ones you engaged and pay directly**: your notary, your surveyor, your architect, your tax adviser, and a buyer's agent if the fee structure is genuinely clean.

That is not cynicism. It is how the market is structured, and it is entirely workable once you see it. The buyers who do badly here are the ones who assumed someone was looking after them.

## Common questions

### Are real estate agents licensed in Bali?
No. There is no licensing regime for agents in Indonesia, no mandatory disclosure and no professional indemnity regime.

### Who does a Bali property agent work for?
Almost always the seller, who pays the commission, even when the agent found the property for you and answered your questions helpfully.

### What is the difference between an agent and a property manager?
An agent is paid a commission on a completed sale. A manager is paid a percentage of ongoing gross revenue for operating the property.

### Should I use a buyer's agent in Bali?
It is useful if their fee genuinely comes from you alone. Ask how they are paid, because receiving anything from the seller's side changes what they are.

### Who actually works for me in a Bali transaction?
Only the people you engaged and pay directly — your notary, surveyor, architect and tax adviser. Everyone else has a different payer and a different incentive.
