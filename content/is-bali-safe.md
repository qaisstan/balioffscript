Yes, broadly. Bali is not a dangerous place by international standards, and violent crime against foreigners is rare.

But the honest answer is that the actual risks here are not the ones people ask about.

## The real risk is the road

This is the one that hurts people, and it is not close.

Bali runs on scooters. Traffic is dense, lane discipline is loose, and a lot of foreigners ride for the first time in their lives here, on unfamiliar roads, often without a proper licence, sometimes in flip flops.

Wet season makes it worse. Roads flood, visibility drops, and the surface gets genuinely slippery.

**If you do one thing for your safety here, it is this.** Wear a real helmet. Do not ride at night on unlit roads if you can avoid it. And check whether your travel insurance actually covers scooter accidents, because a great many policies exclude them unless you hold the correct licence.

That last point catches people constantly. An excluded claim after a serious accident is how a holiday becomes a financial disaster.

## Petty theft

Bag snatching from scooters happens, particularly in busier areas at night. Phone snatching too.

It is opportunistic rather than violent. Do not hang a bag off your handlebars, do not walk with your phone out at the roadside, and it mostly stops being an issue.

## Health

Dengue is present. Use repellent, especially around dusk, and take standing water around a rental seriously.

Stomach problems are common in the first weeks while you adjust. Unpleasant, rarely serious.

The thing to plan for is **serious illness or injury**, because that is where costs escalate fast. [Healthcare, hospitals and what insurance actually needs to cover is here](/living/healthcare-in-bali/).

## Natural events

Bali sits in a seismically active region. Earthquakes and volcanic activity are real but infrequent. It is not something to organise your life around, but it belongs in a construction and insurance conversation if you are building.

## What is genuinely low risk

Walking around at night in most areas. Solo travel. Being a woman travelling alone, relative to most destinations. Scams beyond the ordinary tourist-pricing variety.

## The honest summary

Bali is safe in the way that matters to most people and dangerous in one specific way that most people underestimate.

Treat the roads with the respect you would give a motorbike anywhere else in the world, get insurance that actually covers you on one, and the rest of it is mostly noise.

## Common questions

### Is Bali safe for tourists in 2026?
Broadly yes. Violent crime against foreigners is rare and most visits pass without incident. The genuine risk is road traffic, particularly scooters, which causes far more serious injuries to foreigners than crime does.

### Is Bali safe for solo female travellers?
Yes, relative to most destinations. The usual precautions apply at night and in quieter areas, but Bali compares favourably with most of Southeast Asia and with much of Europe.

### What is the biggest danger in Bali?
Scooter accidents, by a wide margin. Dense traffic, loose lane discipline, unlit roads at night and inexperienced riders combine badly. Wet season makes it worse.

### Does travel insurance cover scooter accidents in Bali?
Often not. Many policies exclude motorbike accidents unless you hold the correct licence for the engine size. Check that clause specifically, because a denied claim after a serious accident is financially catastrophic.

### Are earthquakes a risk in Bali?
Bali sits in a seismically active region. Earthquakes and volcanic activity are real but infrequent. It matters most for construction standards and insurance rather than daily life.
