Nusa Penida is the largest of the three Nusa islands, 20 kilometres off Bali's southeast coast. It is limestone rather than volcanic, which is why the water is clear, the cliffs are white and the interior is dry scrub rather than rice fields.

## The famous sights

**Kelingking Beach.** The T-Rex headland, and the most photographed view in Indonesia after Borobudur. The viewpoint is a short walk from the car park. **The descent to the beach is not a walk**: it is a steep scramble down a cliff on a rough staircase with rope handrails, taking about 45 minutes down and an hour or more back up in full sun. People are injured doing it every season. The view from the top is the photograph; the beach is for the fit and determined.

**Angel's Billabong.** A natural rock pool on the coast that fills from the sea. **People have been swept off it and killed** when a set arrived. Look at it, take the photograph, and do not get in unless a local guide tells you the conditions are safe.

**Broken Beach (Pasih Uug).** A collapsed sea cave forming a natural arch, next to Angel's Billabong. Spectacular and safe.

**Crystal Bay.** The most accessible swimming beach on the west coast, good for snorkelling, and the classic sunset spot.

**Diamond Beach and Atuh**, on the east coast, with white sand, rock pinnacles, and a carved staircase down the cliff. Diamond is the best beach on the island and the drive over is long.

**Peguyangan waterfall**, reached by a blue staircase bolted to a cliff face down to sea-level spring pools at a temple. Not for anyone uneasy with heights.

**Manta Point**, off the south coast, where reef mantas gather at cleaning stations. Snorkelling and diving trips run daily.

## What people get wrong

**The roads.** Nusa Penida's roads have improved a lot and parts are still rough, narrow and steep. Crossing the island takes far longer than the map suggests, and the drive from the harbour to Diamond Beach can be well over an hour each way.

**The distances.** The west sights (Kelingking, Angel's Billabong, Broken Beach, Crystal Bay) and the east sights (Diamond, Atuh, Thousand Islands viewpoint) are on opposite sides. Doing both in a day is a rush.

**The scooter question.** Riding here is harder than on Bali: steep gradients, poor surfaces and long distances. Every season produces a queue of visitors with road rash. If you are not a confident rider, hire a driver for about 600,000 to 800,000 rupiah a day.

**The crowds.** Day-trip boats arrive together mid-morning and the west-coast sights are queued between about 10am and 2pm. Staying overnight gets you Kelingking at 7am with nobody there.

## How long to stay

**One day:** west coast only. Kelingking viewpoint, Broken Beach, Angel's Billabong, Crystal Bay. Rushed but workable.

**Two nights:** west coast one day, east coast the next, with a manta snorkel trip in the morning. This is the right length.

**Three nights or more:** add diving, the interior, the Goa Giri Putri cave temple, and the empty north coast beaches.

## Practicalities

**Water and power.** Penida is dry and infrastructure is basic outside the main strip. Power cuts happen; water is precious.

**ATMs** exist in the main village and are unreliable. Bring cash.

**Medical facilities are limited.** There is a small clinic. Anything serious means a boat to Bali. This matters if you are riding a scooter here.

**Accommodation** ranges from simple guesthouses to a handful of good clifftop places. Book ahead in high season; there is not much of it.

**Food** is warungs and simple restaurants. Do not come for the dining.

## When to go

Dry season, April to October, for calm crossings and the best water. Mola mola season is roughly July to October. Wet season brings rougher crossings, occasional cancellations and slippery cliff paths.

## Common questions

### Is Nusa Penida worth visiting?
Yes. The coastline is the most dramatic in the region. Two nights beats a day trip by a wide margin.

### How long do you need in Nusa Penida?
Two nights to see both coasts properly. One day covers the west side only.

### Is the walk down to Kelingking Beach hard?
Yes. It is a steep cliff scramble taking 45 minutes down and over an hour back, in heat, with rope handrails. The viewpoint at the top is where the photograph comes from.

### Can you swim at Angel's Billabong?
Only when a local guide says conditions allow. People have been swept out and killed there when swell arrived.

### Should I rent a scooter on Nusa Penida?
Only if you are a confident rider. The roads are steep, rough and long, and injuries are common.
