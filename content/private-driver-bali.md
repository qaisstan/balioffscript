The private driver is the best-value thing in Bali tourism and the most misunderstood. You are not hiring a chauffeur. You are hiring a car, a driver, local knowledge and somebody who will wait in the sun while you walk to a waterfall.

## What it costs

- **Full day, eight to ten hours, standard car:** roughly 600,000 to 900,000 rupiah.
- **Larger vehicle for six or more:** roughly 900,000 to 1,400,000 rupiah.
- **Half day, four to five hours:** roughly 400,000 to 600,000 rupiah.
- **Airport transfer only:** roughly 250,000 to 600,000 depending on distance.

Fuel is usually included in the quoted day rate in the south; on long routes to the north or east, drivers often quote the rate plus fuel. Clarify this in the first message and there is no argument at the end.

## What is not included

**Your entry tickets.** Temples, waterfalls and attractions charge per person at the gate, in cash.

**The driver's meal.** Not obligatory, and buying lunch for someone who has driven you for eight hours is normal and appreciated.

**Parking fees**, which are small and usually absorbed by the driver.

**A tip.** Not compulsory. For a good day, 50,000 to 100,000 rupiah is a normal gesture.

## How to find one

The reliable routes, in order:

1. **Your accommodation.** Villas and guesthouses all have a driver they use, and their reputation is attached to the recommendation.
2. **A recommendation from someone who has been.** Drivers here run almost entirely on returning clients and WhatsApp forwards.
3. **WhatsApp groups and community boards** in Canggu, Ubud and Uluwatu.
4. **Booking platforms**, which cost more and insert a middleman into a business that does not need one.

Once you have a good one, take their number. A driver who knows you will move a booking for you, pick up at 4am, and tell you honestly that the thing you are asking to see is not worth two hours in the car.

## The shopping-stop problem

Some tour drivers earn commission from coffee plantations, silver workshops and batik showrooms, and the itinerary quietly bends toward them. A luwak coffee stop that was not on your list is the tell.

Fix it in advance, politely: send your route by message before the day and say you would rather not do shopping stops. Good drivers say "of course" and the day runs as planned.

## What a sensible day looks like

Bali's roads mean the difference between a good day and a bad one is whether the route is a loop or a scatter.

**Ubud loop from the south:** Tegalalang or Jatiluwih, Tirta Empul, lunch in Ubud, Goa Gajah, home. Nine hours with traffic.

**East Bali:** Lempuyang, Tirta Gangga, Sidemen, a beach at Amed. Long, beautiful, and a full 11 hours from Canggu.

**North and highlands:** Munduk waterfalls, Ulun Danu Beratan, Jatiluwih on the way home. Cold, green, and a different island.

**Bukit day:** Uluwatu temple, the cliff beaches, kecak at sunset, seafood at Jimbaran. Short distances, heavy sunset traffic.

Trying to combine east and north in one day is the classic mistake. It is fourteen hours of car.

## Etiquette that makes the day better

- **Agree the route and the price by message**, so both exist in writing.
- **Start early.** Leaving Canggu at 7am instead of 9am changes everything about the day.
- **Let them park where they know.** They know which gate has a shortcut.
- **Do not leave valuables in the car** and do not make a show of checking.
- **Tell them when you want to stop.** You are paying for the car; a coffee break in a rice field costs nothing.

## Multi-day and one-way trips

For a route that ends somewhere else — the airport, the port at Padang Bai for the Gilis, a few nights in Amed — agree a one-way price rather than a day rate. Drivers will usually quote it happily because they can pick up a return fare.

For a trip of several days, some drivers will stay with you and arrange their own accommodation for a modest addition to the daily rate. For anyone visiting the east or north with a tight schedule, this is the easiest version of Bali travel there is.

## Common questions

### How much is a private driver in Bali for a day?
Roughly 600,000 to 900,000 rupiah for eight to ten hours in a standard car, including fuel in most southern itineraries.

### Do you tip a driver in Bali?
It is not expected, but 50,000 to 100,000 rupiah after a good day is a normal and well-received gesture.

### Can a Bali driver pick us up from the airport and keep us for the day?
Yes, and it is common. Agree the total in advance; it is usually cheaper than a transfer plus a separate day.

### Are Bali drivers also guides?
Most are excellent informal guides and will explain what you are seeing. Some temples and treks require a separate licensed guide at the gate.

### How do I avoid shopping stops on a Bali day tour?
Send the route in advance and say you would prefer no shopping stops. Any good driver will simply agree.
