Both markets attract the same buyer and both restrict land to nationals. What differs is what they offer instead, and how they police the workarounds.

## The legal position

| | Thailand | Indonesia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land | No foreign ownership | No foreign ownership |
| Condominium or unit | Freehold within a 49% building quota | Hak Pakai on strata, with conditions |
| Villa on land | Lease, typically 30 years | Lease, Hak Pakai or HGB via PT PMA |
| Company workaround | Nominee Thai shareholders. Prosecuted | Nominee arrangement. Prohibited |
| Enforcement | Arrests and asset seizures reported | Perda Bali 4/2026 criminalised facilitation |

The condominium quota is the real difference. A foreigner in Thailand can hold a unit outright, permanently, with a title deed. Indonesia has no equivalent for land-based property.

## The workaround both countries are closing

In Thailand it is the company with Thai nominee shareholders holding land. In Indonesia it is the nominee holding the certificate.

Both are the same idea and both are being enforced against, in Thailand through prosecutions and seizures, in Indonesia by making facilitation itself an offence under Perda Bali 4/2026.

Anyone telling you either is normal because it is common is describing prevalence, not legality.

## Where each one wins

**Thailand** for genuine unit ownership, a deeper and more transparent market, better medical infrastructure and a larger domestic economy.

**Bali** for tourism-driven rental demand, lower build and operating costs, scarce well-zoned coastal land, and a foreign community concentrated enough to support resale.

## The practical comparison

Phuket and Bali are the closest like-for-like. At USD 250,000, Phuket buys a well-located condominium held freehold. Bali buys a villa on leased land with more space and higher potential yield, and more work to verify.

Neither is obviously right. It depends on whether you want a title you can hold forever or a business that pays more while you run it.

## What to check either way

Remaining term on any lease, the extension clause, who is actually on the title, whether the operating licence exists and transfers, and what the building approvals say.

The verification burden is high in both markets and higher in Indonesia.

## Common questions

### Can foreigners own property in Thailand?
Condominium units, freehold, within a quota of 49 percent of a building's saleable area. Land remains closed to foreign ownership.

### Is Thailand safer than Bali for property?
Thailand offers stronger unit title and a more transparent market. Both restrict land and both are enforcing against nominee structures.

### Did Thailand really arrest foreign homeowners?
Enforcement actions against nominee shareholding structures used to hold land have been reported. The structure, not the ownership of a lawful condominium, is what is targeted.

### Phuket or Bali with $250,000?
Phuket for freehold title and a more liquid resale market. Bali for space, yield potential and lower operating costs, with more verification work.

### Are nominee structures illegal in both countries?
Yes in substance. Thailand prosecutes nominee shareholding used to circumvent land rules; Indonesia prohibits nominee land arrangements and, since Perda Bali 4/2026, facilitating them.
