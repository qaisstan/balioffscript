Most people do this backwards. They find land, fall for it, pay a deposit to hold it, then start checking. By then the money is committed and the pressure is on.

Here is the order that protects you.

## Before anything

Decide what you will do with it. Live in it, rent it nightly, or hold it. That single answer changes which structure you need, which zoning works, and whether the plot is viable at all.

## The structure

A foreigner cannot own land in Indonesia. Not through a company, not through a spouse. Three lawful routes exist:

- **Leasehold.** A contract for a fixed period, commonly 25 to 30 years. Simplest and cheapest to set up.
- **Hak Pakai.** A registered right in your own name. Requires residency and the property must be residential.
- **HGB through a PT PMA.** A registered right held by an Indonesian company you own. The commercial route.

[The differences matter more than the price does.](/ownership/hgb-vs-leasehold-bali/)

## The sequence

**1. Check before you commit.** Certificate verified at the land office by your own notary. Zoning designation for that exact parcel. Building coefficients. Setbacks. Access rights. No money moves yet.

**2. Commit.** A letter of intent fixes the price. A deposit, commonly 10%, goes down with a written window for due diligence and a written refund trigger if it fails.

**3. Verify properly.** Two to four weeks realistically. Title history, heirs, spousal consent, existing mortgages, permits on anything standing.

**4. Complete.** The deed is executed in front of a PPAT. Your notary, not the seller's.

## Where the money should sit

The deposit is where deals go wrong. Ask one question: if due diligence fails, who returns this, from which account, and by when? Get the answer in the document, not in a message.

## What kills deals here

- Zoning that does not permit the intended use
- A second certificate over the same land
- Heirs who never consented
- Buildings with no permit, sold at a discount that does not cover the fix
- A lease with no enforceable extension clause

## The sequence that protects you

Buyers lose money in Bali in a predictable order: they commit emotionally, they pay a deposit, and then they start checking. Reverse that and most of the risk disappears.

1. **Establish the structure first.** Hak Pakai, leasehold, or HGB through a PT PMA. Decide which applies to you before you look at plots, because it changes what you can buy.
2. **Appoint your own notary.** Not the seller's, not the agent's. A PPAT you selected and pay.
3. **Verify the certificate** at the land office. Owner identity, title type, remaining term, boundaries, and any registered charges.
4. **Check the zoning** on the specific parcel against what you intend to build.
5. **Walk the boundaries** with the certificate in hand and confirm they match the ground.
6. **Confirm access rights** are registered, not merely customary.
7. **Only then** pay a deposit, and only against a written refund condition.

## Zoning is not negotiable

The single most common expensive surprise is agricultural land bought on the understanding that a villa can go on it. Green zone land is cheap because it is agricultural, and sellers are frequently relaxed about the point.

An existing villa nearby proves nothing. It may predate the current spatial plan, it may be unlicensed, or it may sit in a different designation. Every parcel is assessed on its own.

## Access rights catch people

A plot reached across a neighbour's land, on the strength of a long-standing arrangement, is a plot with no registered right of way. That arrangement holds until the neighbour sells, dies, or changes their mind. Then you own land you cannot reach, which is worth a fraction of what you paid.

Access road width matters separately: construction vehicles need more room than a scooter, and a lane that cannot take a concrete truck adds significant cost to any build.

## Money movement

Transfer by bank, traceably, to the registered owner. Not in cash, not to a third party, not to an agent's account "for convenience". Condition every payment on a verification milestone that has actually been met.

If you are told a deposit must be paid today or the plot goes, that is a sales technique, not a market condition. Land that has sat for eight months does not become urgent because you looked at it.

## Common questions

### Can a foreigner buy land in Bali?
Not as freehold in a personal name. Land is held through a leasehold, Hak Pakai where you qualify, or HGB through an Indonesian company such as a PT PMA.

### How do you buy land in Bali safely?
Verify the certificate at the land office, check zoning against what you intend to build, confirm access rights and boundaries on the ground, and use your own independent notary rather than the seller's.

### What should you check before buying land in Bali?
Title type and remaining term, zoning colour, road access width, drainage and flood behaviour, setbacks from rivers and the coast, and whether the seller is the registered owner.

### How much are land purchase costs in Bali?
Budget around 7 percent of the price for transfer tax, notary and PPAT fees, and independent legal review. Independent review is the cheapest line on that list and the one buyers most often skip.

### What is an are of land in Bali?
Land here is priced per are, which is 100 square metres. Always confirm whether a quoted price is per are or total, because the confusion is expensive.
