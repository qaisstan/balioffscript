Cemagi sits immediately north of Pererenan, before Seseh, on the stretch of coast where the Canggu corridor thins out.

## What it is

Black sand beach with a temple on the headland, rice fields running back inland, and villa development that is present but nothing like Canggu's density.

**Gria Beach** and the Cemagi shoreline are quieter than anything to the south, and the area retains a working agricultural character that Pererenan has largely lost.

Ten to fifteen minutes to Pererenan's amenity, twenty to thirty-five to Canggu's centre depending on traffic.

There is no walkable strip — a handful of beach clubs and cafes have appeared, and you drive for anything substantial.

## The market

Land is **cheaper than Pererenan** and more available, with larger plots and lease terms that run meaningfully longer at the same budget.

Prices have moved substantially as Pererenan matured and pushed demand north, so a good deal of the growth story is already in the number.

Stock is a mix of newer villa development aimed at investors and land. This is more of a build market than a buy market.

## Rental performance

Real and improving, sitting between Pererenan's established demand and Seseh's emerging one.

The guest wants quiet, space and a beach, and accepts a short drive to amenity. That market has grown as Canggu has densified and as families and repeat visitors have looked for something calmer.

**What is absent** is walkable demand and the digital nomad long-stay population that fills Canggu's February and March. Occupancy is more seasonal than the prime corridor, and a projection built on Canggu comparables will overstate it.

Properties that perform are larger, well-designed villas with good pools and rice field or ocean outlook — the things that justify the drive. Small units without a view compete poorly against a much larger market to the south.

## The zoning issue

A meaningful amount of Cemagi land remains **classified agricultural**, and commercial accommodation is not permitted in a green zone.

Nearby villas are not evidence that a specific parcel permits one. Check the spatial plan for the exact parcel with Badung regency before anything else — this is the check that determines whether the land is an asset or a field.

## View protection, which is the local risk

Cemagi's value is substantially in outlook — rice fields and ocean.

**A view over land somebody else owns is not protected**, and the whole area is developing. The thing that makes your property worth a premium can be built on.

Establish what lies between you and the view, who owns it, and what their zoning permits. This is checkable and it is checked far less often than it should be.

## What else to check

**Coastal setback** on anything near the beach, and erosion history for that stretch.

**River setback** — the sempadan sungai — which catches more parcels here than buyers expect and is strictly applied.

**Flood and drainage** for the specific parcel and its access road, which in rice field areas is street-specific.

**Access**, documented rather than merely used.

**Water and power capacity**, and what upgrading would cost.

**Realistic comparables from Cemagi and Seseh**, not Pererenan or Canggu.

## Against its neighbours

**Against Pererenan:** cheaper land, longer terms, larger plots, quieter — and thinner rental demand with a deeper low season.

**Against Seseh:** more established, closer to amenity, slightly more expensive, with a rental market a year or two further along.

Cemagi is the middle position on that stretch, which for a buyer wanting some of the upside without the full early-stage risk is a reasonable place to be.

## Who it suits

Buyers who want more land, more term and a view for their money, and who will build.

Owner-occupiers wanting quiet and a beach within reach of the corridor's amenity.

Anyone taking a five to ten year position on the corridor continuing north.

## Who it does not

Anyone needing strong occupancy from year one, or who wants walkable amenity.

## Common questions

### Is Cemagi a good investment?
Land is cheaper than Pererenan with longer terms and larger plots, and rental demand is real and improving, though more seasonal than the prime corridor.

### What is the main risk buying in Cemagi?
Zoning. A meaningful amount of land remains classified agricultural, where commercial accommodation is not permitted, and nearby villas are not evidence your parcel permits one.

### Is Cemagi better than Pererenan?
Cheaper land, longer terms and more space in Cemagi; stronger and more established rental demand in Pererenan. Cemagi is the middle position between Pererenan and Seseh.

### Does Cemagi have good rental demand?
Real and growing, driven by families and repeat visitors wanting quiet and space. There is no nomad long-stay market to fill the quiet months.

### What should I check about a Cemagi view?
Who owns the land between you and the outlook and what their zoning permits, because a rice field or ocean view over someone else's parcel is not protected.
