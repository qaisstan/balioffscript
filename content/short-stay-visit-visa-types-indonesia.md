Indonesia has more visit visa categories than most people realise, aimed at specific short-term purposes. They matter because the alternative — using a tourist visa for something it does not cover — is the exposure.

## The categories

**Short course or training.** Attending a course, a training programme or a certification, at a recognised provider. Common in Bali for yoga teacher training, diving certification, language study and professional courses.

**Work trial or probation.** Where a company is assessing a foreign candidate before committing to an employment arrangement, within defined limits.

**Event participation.** Conferences, exhibitions, trade fairs, seminars — attending, exhibiting or speaking.

**Business visit.** Meetings, negotiations, site visits and due diligence.

**Sports and competition.** Participating in events.

**Filming and journalism**, which have their own requirements and are more tightly controlled than people assume.

Index codes and requirements **change**, so verify the current category and its conditions rather than following an older list.

## The line that decides everything

**Attending, learning, observing, discussing and assessing** are visit activities.

**Producing, delivering, teaching, performing and directing** are work.

**Payment is not the test.** Indonesian immigration practice treats productive activity as work regardless of whether you receive anything.

So: attending a yoga teacher training is a visit activity. Teaching on one is work. Attending a conference is a visit activity. Speaking at it commercially, running a workshop, or exhibiting and selling is closer to work and may need different authorisation.

The distinction sounds fine and it is the one that gets enforced.

## Short courses specifically

Bali's course economy is large — yoga, diving, surfing, language, wellness, culinary.

**Attending** a course at a recognised provider is straightforward and there is a category for it where a stay exceeds what a visa on arrival allows.

**The provider's status matters.** An accredited institution can sponsor properly. A loosely organised operation may not be in a position to, and "a tourist visa is fine" tells you about the operation rather than about the rules.

**For longer study**, a student permit sponsored by an accredited institution is the route, and it can provide lawful residency while you study — an underused option for someone without an employer, spouse or investor basis.

## Work trials

Where a company wants to assess a candidate before committing to an RPTKA application and a work KITAS.

**It is limited**, in duration and scope, and it is not a route to working indefinitely while nobody decides.

If the role becomes real, the employer needs to obtain **RPTKA** approval and a work permit. A trial that quietly becomes employment is employment without authorisation.

## Events

**Attending** a conference or exhibition is covered by a business visit.

**Exhibiting**, which involves setting up, staffing a stand and selling, sits closer to work. Established organisers handle the permit position for exhibitors routinely — ask them what they are arranging.

**Speaking**, particularly for a fee, is worth clarifying.

## What every one of them requires

**A defined purpose**, documented.

**Frequently a sponsor or inviting party** in Indonesia — the provider, the employer, the organiser — that is registered and in a position to sponsor.

**Personal documents**, financial evidence and, for some categories, a police clearance.

**A defined duration**, with extension within limits for some categories.

## Where people go wrong

**Using a visa on arrival for everything**, because it is easy. It covers tourism, visiting, meetings and conferences — not courses beyond its duration, not trials, not delivering at events.

**Assuming the organiser has handled it.** Ask specifically what they are sponsoring and under what index, and get the answer in writing before booking flights.

**A trial that becomes a job.** The most common drift, and the exposure sits with you as well as the employer.

**Repeated consecutive entries.** Someone attending courses back to back on successive tourist visas attracts the same scrutiny as anyone else living here on them, and immigration can decline entry.

## For a property owner

The same principle that runs through all of this: **your permit has to match what you actually do.**

Owning a villa and receiving rental income is not work. Operating it — bookings, staff, contractors, suppliers — is, regardless of ownership and regardless of payment.

If you are here for a course, be here for a course. If you are running a business, get a permit that covers it.

## Common questions

### What visa do I need for a course in Indonesia?
A short course or training visit visa where the stay exceeds what a visa on arrival allows, or a student permit sponsored by an accredited institution for longer study.

### Can I do a work trial in Indonesia?
There is a category for it, limited in duration and scope. If the role becomes real, the employer must obtain RPTKA approval and a work permit.

### Do I need a visa to attend a conference in Bali?
Attending is covered by a business visit visa. Exhibiting, staffing a stand or speaking commercially sits closer to work and should be clarified with the organiser.

### Is attending a yoga teacher training considered work?
Attending is a visit activity. Teaching on one is work, regardless of payment, and requires a permit that covers it.

### What happens if I use a tourist visa for a course or trial?
It covers tourism, visiting, meetings and conferences only. Anything productive is work in immigration terms, with exposure ranging from fines to deportation and a re-entry ban.
