Komodo's best sites are spread across a park you cannot cover from a harbour. A liveaboard solves that, and the difference between a day trip and a boat trip here is larger than in most places.

## What a liveaboard gets you

**Sunrise at Padar**, before the day boats arrive, which is a completely different experience from the 10am crowd.

**The far sites.** Castle Rock, Crystal Rock, Batu Bolong and the northern dive sites are hours from Labuan Bajo and are the reason divers come.

**Three to four dives a day** instead of two.

**Sleeping at anchor** in bays with nothing else in them.

## The options

**Budget open-deck boats, two or three nights.** Sleeping on mattresses on deck, shared facilities, simple food. From roughly 2,500,000 to 5,000,000 rupiah per person for three days and two nights, plus park fees.

**Mid-range cabin boats.** Private or shared cabins, air conditioning, better food. Roughly 6,000,000 to 15,000,000 rupiah for three or four days.

**Dive liveaboards.** Purpose-built, with compressors, nitrox, tenders and dive guides. From roughly 250 to 600 USD per person per day.

**Luxury phinisi charters.** Traditional wooden schooners converted to high-end charters, sold by the boat. Thousands of dollars a night.

## What to check before booking

**The safety record and the boat itself.** There have been boat fires, sinkings and mechanical failures in this region. Ask about life jackets, life rafts, radio and satellite communication, and whether there is a second engine.

**Insurance.** Ask what the operator carries, and check that your own travel insurance covers boat travel and diving to your planned depth.

**The itinerary.** Some cheap trips spend most of the time motoring. Ask how many sites per day and how much of each day is sailing.

**Group size.** Twelve people on a small deck boat is different from six.

**Food and water.** Ask how drinking water is supplied; the good operators provide refills rather than cases of plastic bottles.

**Whether park fees are included.** Frequently they are not, and they are substantial.

## Seasickness and comfort

The crossings between islands can be lively, particularly in the shoulder months. Open-deck boats have nowhere to escape sun, rain or motion. If you are prone to seasickness, take a cabin boat and medication.

Nights on deck are hot and sometimes wet. Bring a light sleeping bag liner, a torch and a dry bag.

## Diving specifics

Komodo's currents are strong and some sites are advanced. Operators should ask about your certification and recent experience; one that does not is a warning sign.

A reef hook, a surface marker buoy and a whistle are standard kit here. Nitrox is widely available.

**The nearest hyperbaric chamber is not close.** Ask the operator what their evacuation procedure is before you book. This is not a formality in an area this remote.

## Day trips instead

If a liveaboard is not for you, day boats from Labuan Bajo cover Padar, Komodo or Rinca, Pink Beach and Manta Point in a long day. Private charters give you a dawn start, which is most of the benefit of a liveaboard for one long day.

## When

April to October. Boats reduce or stop in January and February when the seas are at their worst.

## What a typical three-day itinerary looks like

**Day one:** board at Labuan Bajo late morning, sail out, snorkel or dive Sebayur or Siaba, anchor overnight near Rinca.
**Day two:** dragons with a ranger at first light, then Padar for the climb, Pink Beach, and Manta Point in the afternoon. Anchor in a quiet bay.
**Day three:** a dawn dive or snorkel, Kanawa or Kelor on the way back, into Labuan Bajo by mid-afternoon.

Divers swap the island stops for three or four dives a day at Batu Bolong, Castle Rock, Crystal Rock and Shotgun.

## What to bring

A dry bag, reef-safe sunscreen, a hat and long sleeves for sun, motion sickness tablets, a torch, a refillable water bottle, and cash for park fees and tips. Power on smaller boats is limited and often only available while the engine runs, so bring a power bank.

Nights are hot. On open-deck boats a sleeping bag liner is more useful than a sleeping bag, and a light rain layer matters if a squall comes through.

## Common questions

### How much is a Komodo liveaboard?
From roughly 2,500,000 rupiah per person for a basic open-deck three-day trip to several hundred dollars a day for a dedicated dive liveaboard.

### Is a Komodo liveaboard worth it?
For divers, clearly. For non-divers, a two-night trip gets you Padar at sunrise and empty anchorages, which day trips cannot.

### Are Komodo boats safe?
Standards vary widely. Check life-saving equipment, communications, engine redundancy and the operator's record before booking.

### How many days do you need for Komodo?
Three nights minimum in Labuan Bajo with one full boat day, or a two to four-night liveaboard for the full park.

### Do you need diving experience for Komodo?
For the famous sites, yes. Currents are strong and several are advanced. There are easier sites and excellent snorkelling for everyone else.
