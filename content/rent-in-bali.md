Almost everyone rents here before they buy, and the rental market tells you more about an area than any listing site will.

## What a villa costs

**One-bedroom villas** start from around **IDR 17,500,000 a month in Canggu**, and around **IDR 15,600,000 in Seminyak**.

**Two-bedroom villas** run roughly **IDR 15 to 45 million a month**, and that spread is not a typo. Location, pool, condition and how good you are at negotiating all move it.

Within Canggu, **Berawa and Batu Bolong** carry a premium over almost everywhere else. Central Ubud is not the bargain people expect either, though the outskirts drop quickly. **Sanur is consistently cheaper** than Canggu for equivalent space.

## The thing nobody warns you about

**Yearly contracts are usually paid in full, up front.**

That is the single biggest gap between the budget people plan and the reality they meet. You do not pay monthly like at home. A landlord quoting you a good annual rate frequently wants twelve months in one transfer before you get the keys.

Monthly rentals exist and are more flexible, but you pay a real premium for that flexibility. Most long-term listings want a **minimum of one to three months**, and plenty want six or twelve paid at once.

For anything over six months, a yearly contract is normally better value if you can fund it.

## What to check before you sign

- **Is electricity included?** Usually not, and air conditioning across several bedrooms is a genuine monthly cost.
- **Who maintains the pool and garden?** If it is you, that is a staff cost nobody quoted you.
- **Is there a proper contract?** A one-page note is common and gives you very little.
- **What happens if you leave early?** On an annual prepayment, often nothing good.
- **Water supply.** Ask where it comes from and whether it is reliable in dry season.

## Why this matters if you plan to buy

Rental prices are the closest thing Bali has to an honest signal about an area. If long-term rents are soft somewhere, the nightly rental market there is usually softer than the projections claim.

More practically, renting for a year in the area you think you want is the cheapest due diligence available. People are certain about their area for roughly six months, and then a lot of them move.

**Rent first. Buy second.** In that order it costs you one year of rent. In the other order it costs considerably more.
