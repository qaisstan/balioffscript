Koh Samui is what Bali was before it got busy: palm-lined beaches, hillside pool villas with sea views and a slower pace. People who find Bali too crowded look at Samui. Here is how the two really compare for a buyer.

## Ownership

**Koh Samui** follows Thai law. Land cannot be owned by a foreigner. A villa is held on a **registered lease of up to 30 years**, recorded on the title deed at the Land Department. Renewals beyond 30 years are promises in the contract, not registered rights. Condos can be owned freehold within the building's **49% foreign quota**, but Samui has far fewer condos than Phuket.

**Bali** gives no ownership to foreigners either. You buy a **lease**, usually 25 to 30 years with a written extension, or use **Hak Pakai** with residency, or **HGB through a PT PMA**. The Bali lease is not registered on the land certificate, so checking the owner and every signature matters more.

## Getting there

This is Samui's biggest weakness. The island's airport is small and privately run, with a limited number of flights, mostly from Bangkok and a few regional hubs, and fares are high. Many visitors fly to Surat Thani and take the ferry.

Bali's airport handles direct flights from Australia, the Middle East and across Asia. More flights means more guests for a rental villa.

## Rental demand

- **Bali** has many markets at once: surfers in Uluwatu, families in Sanur, digital nomads in Canggu, wellness in Ubud. A villa can rent most of the year if it is in the right place.
- **Samui** is more seasonal, with a rainy season in the last months of the year and fewer guests overall. Luxury villas rent well in peak weeks; the rest of the year is quieter.

A well-run Bali villa nets roughly **7 to 14%**. Samui villas can match that in a good year but with fewer booking nights to work with.

## Living there

Samui is quieter, with fewer international schools, a smaller hospital network and less nightlife. That is the attraction for some buyers and the problem for others. Bali has more of everything, including traffic.

## Which I would choose

- **You want peace, a sea-view villa and a registered lease:** Samui, if you accept fewer guests and harder access.
- **You want rental income and resale demand:** Bali, in an area that rents all year, with a long lease and a fixed extension.

## Common questions

### Can a foreigner own a villa on Koh Samui?
Not the land. The villa sits on a registered lease of up to 30 years. Some buyers also use a company for the building, which must be a real company, not a nominee.

### Is Koh Samui cheaper than Bali?
Sea-view villas on Samui are often priced similar to or above good Bali villas. Bali has more choice at the lower end.

### Which island has better rental occupancy?
Bali, in most areas, because of more flights and more visitor types. Samui depends more on peak season.

### Is the Samui lease safer than a Bali lease?
It is registered, which makes it more transparent. A Bali lease can be just as solid if the owner, the heirs, the zoning and the extension are checked before you sign.
