Tropical humidity and year-round warmth mean Bali has a pest load that temperate-climate owners are not prepared for. Termites are the expensive one.

## Termites

**Subterranean termites** are the serious threat. They travel underground, enter through the slab or foundations, and consume timber from the inside out. Damage is frequently invisible until something fails.

A Bali villa typically contains a great deal of timber — structure, roof framing, joinery, decking, furniture — and all of it is at risk.

**Drywood termites** also occur and attack timber directly without ground contact, including furniture.

## Prevention at the build stage

This is where it is cheap.

**Soil treatment before the slab is poured.** A chemical barrier applied to the ground beneath and around the foundations. It costs a small fraction of what remediation costs and it is the single most effective step available.

**Physical barriers** at entry points, where the design allows.

**Treated timber** for anything structural. Untreated timber in this climate has a short life.

**Timber choice.** Some species — notably good quality teak and ulin — have far better natural resistance than others. Cheap softwood is an invitation.

**Design.** Keep timber off the ground, ventilate under decking and subfloors, avoid direct soil contact, and keep planting away from the building where it bridges barriers.

If you are building, insist the contractor includes soil treatment and get it in the specification. It is routinely omitted from quotes and it is not the place to save money.

## Prevention on an existing building

**Regular inspection.** Annually at minimum, by someone who knows what they are looking for. Mud tubes on foundations and walls, hollow-sounding timber, discarded wings, and doors or windows that suddenly stick.

**Perimeter treatment** and baiting systems, maintained on a schedule.

**Remove conducive conditions.** Timber stored against the building, garden beds against walls, leaking plumbing keeping soil damp, and blocked drainage.

**Fix water problems immediately.** Damp timber is far more attractive to termites than dry timber, which is one more reason drainage failures compound.

## What treatment costs

Soil treatment during construction is modest relative to the build.

Ongoing preventive treatment on an existing property is an annual maintenance item, not a large one.

**Remediation after structural damage** is a different order entirely — replacing roof framing, structural timber or joinery runs into thousands and sometimes tens of thousands, and the work is disruptive enough to take a rental property out of service.

The economics are obvious and owners still skip the cheap end.

## The other pests

**Mosquitoes.** Dengue is present in Bali and is a genuine health issue rather than a nuisance. Eliminate standing water — pot bases, gutters, unused containers, pool covers — and consider screens and treatment. Guests who get bitten mention it in reviews; guests who get dengue mention it more loudly.

**Ants**, constantly, which is mostly a nuisance and occasionally a problem where they nest in electrics.

**Geckos**, harmless and part of the experience, though they leave droppings that need cleaning.

**Rats and mice**, particularly near rice fields and where waste is not managed. They damage wiring, which is a fire risk.

**Snakes**, occasionally, particularly near water and rice fields. Keep grass short and clear debris.

**Mould**, which is not a pest and belongs on the same list because it behaves like one. Humidity, poor ventilation and any water ingress produce it, and it is a guest complaint and a health issue. Ventilation and drying are the answer; cleaning alone is not.

## For a rental villa specifically

Pest presence appears in reviews and affects your rating, which affects your ranking and your rate.

**A pest control schedule** — monthly or quarterly, depending on the property — costs little and prevents the complaints that cost more.

**Mosquito management** is the one guests notice most. It is worth doing properly.

**Do not spray aggressively immediately before a guest arrives.** The smell is worse than the problem.

**Brief your manager** to report early signs rather than handle them quietly. A termite problem reported at the mud tube stage is cheap; the same problem reported when a beam fails is not.

## Common questions

### Are termites a problem in Bali?
Yes, seriously. Subterranean termites enter through foundations and consume timber from the inside, often invisibly until something fails, and Bali villas contain a great deal of timber.

### How do you prevent termites when building in Bali?
Soil treatment before the slab is poured, treated or naturally resistant timber, keeping timber off the ground, and good ventilation and drainage. Soil treatment is routinely omitted from quotes.

### How much does termite treatment cost in Bali?
Soil treatment during construction is modest relative to the build and ongoing prevention is a small annual item. Remediation after structural damage runs into thousands or tens of thousands.

### What pests affect Bali villas?
Termites, mosquitoes carrying dengue, ants, rats which damage wiring, occasional snakes, and mould, which behaves like a pest and results from humidity and poor ventilation.

### Does pest control affect rental reviews?
Yes. Mosquitoes in particular are what guests notice and mention, and a regular pest control schedule costs far less than the rating damage it prevents.
