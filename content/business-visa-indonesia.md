The business visa is the most misunderstood permit in Indonesia, because the line it draws is between two things that feel similar and are legally very different.

## What it covers

Meetings. Negotiations. Contract discussions. Site visits and inspections. Conferences, exhibitions and trade fairs. Supplier and factory visits. Due diligence on an investment. Signing documents.

In short: **commercial activity that involves representing a business from outside Indonesia**, without performing productive work inside it.

## What it does not cover

Employment, paid or unpaid. Providing services to Indonesian clients. Delivering a project. Installing or commissioning equipment. Managing staff. Running operations. Anything that produces output rather than conducts a discussion.

The line is not about whether you receive a salary. An unpaid founder running their own Indonesian company's daily operations is working. A salaried executive attending a two-day negotiation is not.

## Where it catches property investors

This is the specific pattern worth naming.

Buying a villa, attending viewings, meeting a notary, signing a lease, inspecting a build — all of that sits comfortably within a business visit, and a visa on arrival covers most of it anyway.

**Running the villa afterwards does not.** Handling bookings, managing the cleaner and the pool service, dealing with suppliers, instructing contractors, making operational decisions — that is work in Indonesia, regardless of whether anyone pays you for it and regardless of the fact that you own the asset.

People do this constantly on tourist and business visas. It is exposure, and enforcement has become more visible.

If you intend to operate the property yourself from Indonesia, the honest answer is a permit that authorises it. If you do not, engage a manager and let your involvement be that of an owner rather than an operator.

## The types

**Single entry business visit visa.** One entry, a defined period, extendable within limits. Suits a specific trip with a specific purpose.

**Multiple entry business visa.** Valid for a longer period with multiple entries, each of a limited duration. Suits someone travelling in and out repeatedly for meetings across a year.

Both are visit visas. Neither is a stay permit and neither authorises work.

Requirements typically include a sponsor or inviting party in Indonesia, supporting correspondence explaining the purpose, and evidence of the business relationship.

## Why enforcement has tightened

Indonesia has been more active on foreign nationals working without authorisation, and Bali specifically has seen visible enforcement.

The practical risk is rarely an inspector arriving unannounced. It is that the exposure exists continuously and materialises at a bad moment: a permit application, a dispute with a former partner or employee, a complaint from a competitor, or a company compliance review.

Consequences run from fines through deportation to a re-entry ban. For someone holding a villa here, a re-entry ban is disproportionately damaging — you are separated from an asset you cannot manage remotely and cannot easily sell from abroad.

## What to use instead

**Work KITAS**, if you are employed by an Indonesian company including your own, with RPTKA approval.

**E33G remote worker KITAS**, if you work remotely for a company outside Indonesia earning income from outside Indonesia. This is the permit for the digital nomad situation and it does not permit working for Indonesian clients.

**Investor KITAS**, if you hold shares meeting the threshold — noting that its scope is narrower than a general work permit.

**Second home or golden visa** routes, if you want to be here without working.

## The practical rule

Ask what you will actually be doing, hour by hour, for the weeks you are here. If the honest answer includes producing something, delivering something, or directing people in Indonesia, a visit visa is the wrong instrument.

If it is meetings, inspections and signatures, a business visit visa is exactly right and there is nothing to worry about.

## Common questions

### What can you do on an Indonesian business visa?
Attend meetings, negotiations, conferences and exhibitions, conduct site visits and due diligence, and sign documents. It does not permit performing work in Indonesia.

### Can I manage my Bali villa on a business visa?
No. Handling bookings, managing staff, instructing contractors and making operational decisions is work, regardless of whether you are paid or own the asset.

### What is the difference between a single and multiple entry business visa?
Single entry covers one trip for a defined period. Multiple entry is valid across a longer period with repeated entries, each of limited duration. Neither authorises work.

### What happens if I work on a business visa in Indonesia?
Consequences range from fines through deportation to a re-entry ban, which is disproportionately damaging for anyone holding property here.

### Which permit should I use if I work remotely from Bali?
The E33G remote worker KITAS, which covers employment by a company outside Indonesia with income from outside Indonesia. It does not permit working for Indonesian clients.
