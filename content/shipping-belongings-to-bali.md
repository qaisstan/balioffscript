The instinct is to ship everything. For most people moving here it is the wrong call, and the reasons are practical rather than sentimental.

## Why shipping usually loses

**Furniture is cheap and good here.** Bali has a large furniture industry. Teak, rattan, custom joinery and upholstery are made locally at prices well below Europe, Australia or North America, and the styles suit the climate.

**The climate destroys imported furniture.** Humidity warps veneered board, delaminates glued joints and grows mould in upholstery. Furniture built for a dry temperate house does not enjoy a Bali wet season.

**Electrical goods do not match.** Indonesia runs 230V at 50Hz on European style plugs. North American appliances need transformers. Warranties do not travel, and local service for a foreign model is difficult.

**Duty and clearance.** Personal effects can attract duty depending on your status and how the shipment is declared, and clearance is a process rather than a formality.

## What is worth bringing

- Clothing, especially anything hard to find in your size
- Specialist professional equipment and tools
- Laptops, cameras, hard drives
- Prescription medication with documentation
- Documents: certificates, apostilled paperwork, medical records
- Anything genuinely irreplaceable or sentimental

## What to leave

- Furniture, in almost all cases
- Large appliances
- Books, which mould here
- Bedding and towels, which are cheap locally and better suited to the climate
- Anything you have not used in a year

## If you do ship

Sea freight is the only sensible option for volume, and it is slow: expect a couple of months door to door depending on origin, plus clearance time.

Use an agent experienced with Indonesian customs. Have a detailed inventory with values. Ship after your stay permit is settled rather than before, since your status affects what relief is available.

Insure it. Salt air, handling and long transit are hard on a container.

## The arithmetic

Price a container against the cost of furnishing a villa locally. For a modest household the local option is frequently cheaper, arrives immediately, and is built for the climate.

That comparison is worth doing on paper before you commit, because the emotional pull toward shipping is strong and the numbers usually disagree with it.

## Common questions

### Should I ship furniture to Bali?
Usually not. Local furniture is cheaper, better suited to the humidity, and available immediately. Imported furniture built for a temperate climate deteriorates here.

### How long does shipping to Bali take?
Sea freight typically takes around two months door to door depending on origin, plus customs clearance.

### Do I pay customs duty on personal effects in Indonesia?
It depends on your status and how the shipment is declared. Relief exists in some circumstances but it is not automatic. Use an agent who handles Indonesian clearance regularly.

### What voltage does Indonesia use?
230V at 50Hz, with European style two pin plugs. North American appliances need transformers and are usually not worth bringing.

### What should I bring to Bali?
Clothing, professional equipment, electronics, documents, prescription medication and anything irreplaceable. Buy furniture, appliances and household textiles locally.
