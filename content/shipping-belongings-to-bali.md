Most people who ship a container to Bali regret it. The ones who do not are usually shipping something specific that cannot be replaced here.

## The case against shipping

**Almost everything is available and cheaper here.** Furniture in solid teak, suar and reclaimed timber, made to specification by local workshops, costs a fraction of shipping the equivalent from Australia or Europe — and it is better suited to the climate.

**The climate destroys the wrong things.** Upholstered furniture, particle board, leather, paper, musical instruments and anything with foam or fabric suffer badly in Bali's humidity. Items that survived twenty years at home can deteriorate within two here.

**Electrical differences.** Indonesia runs 230V at 50Hz with European-style plugs. Australian and European appliances generally work with a plug adapter; North American 110V appliances do not without a transformer, and a transformer running a fridge continuously is not a sensible arrangement.

**Customs.** Duty, taxes, clearance and agent fees. Relief exists for returning residents and for certain permit holders under defined conditions, but it is conditional and the conditions have to be met.

**Prohibited and restricted items.** Indonesia restricts a range of goods and enforcement is real. Check before packing rather than at the port.

**The cost.** Sea freight, insurance, clearance, duty, local transport and delivery to a property down a narrow lane. The total is frequently more than replacing the contents locally.

## What is worth shipping

**Nothing you can buy here**, which is the only test that matters.

**Specialist tools or professional equipment** you actually use.

**Genuinely irreplaceable personal items** — though consider the humidity and whether they will survive.

**Quality mattresses**, if you are particular. Mattress quality here is variable and a bad one produces bad guest reviews.

**Specific appliances** that are hard to source locally to the standard you want.

**Books**, if you cannot part with them, accepting that they will need dehumidified storage.

## The process

**Sea freight** is the only sensible option for volume, taking weeks from origin to delivery. Air freight is available and expensive, for small quantities.

**Use an experienced international mover** with Indonesian customs experience. This is not a self-service exercise; the paperwork is unforgiving and errors cost storage fees while goods sit at the port.

**Produce a detailed inventory**, valued. Customs will want it and vagueness causes delay.

**Establish your duty position in advance.** Relief for a returning resident or a permit holder is conditional, and knowing whether you qualify before shipping changes the whole calculation.

**Allow for delays.** Port clearance times vary and the estimate you are given at origin is optimistic.

**Insure it properly**, and read what the policy excludes.

## The alternative that usually wins

Bring **two suitcases** of what you actually need. Buy the rest here.

Furniture made locally to your specification costs less than the shipping would, suits the climate, and you can have exactly what you want rather than what happened to fit in a container.

Appliances bought locally have local warranties and local service, which matters when something fails.

And you arrive without a container sitting at a port accruing storage fees while a customs question is resolved.

## For a property owner specifically

If you are furnishing a rental villa, ship nothing. **Furnish locally.**

A three-bedroom furnished to a standard that rents and photographs well costs USD 25,000 to USD 50,000 here, using local makers, in materials that survive the climate. Shipping furniture from home to do the same job costs more, takes months, and delivers items that will deteriorate faster.

The one exception is mattresses, where paying for quality — imported or from a good local supplier — is worth it because the alternative shows up in reviews.

## Common questions

### Should I ship my belongings to Bali?
Usually not. Most things are available and cheaper here, the climate destroys upholstery, particle board and leather, and shipping costs frequently exceed replacing the contents locally.

### What is worth shipping to Bali?
Only what you cannot buy here — specialist tools, professional equipment, genuinely irreplaceable items, and quality mattresses if you are particular.

### Do I pay import duty on personal effects in Indonesia?
Duty and taxes apply, with conditional relief available for returning residents and certain permit holders. Establish your position before shipping rather than after.

### Will my appliances work in Bali?
Indonesia runs 230V at 50Hz with European-style plugs. Australian and European appliances generally work with an adapter; North American 110V appliances need a transformer.

### How should I furnish a rental villa in Bali?
Locally. A three-bedroom furnished to a standard that rents costs USD 25,000 to USD 50,000 using local makers in materials suited to the climate, which shipping cannot match.
