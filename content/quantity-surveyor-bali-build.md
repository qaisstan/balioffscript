On a Bali build, the money leaves in variations and in payments made against work nobody independently verified. A quantity surveyor or project manager is how you stop both.

## What each role does

**A quantity surveyor** prices the work from the drawings and specification, produces a bill of quantities, evaluates tenders on a like-for-like basis, values work completed for payment, and prices variations.

**A project manager** runs the programme, coordinates the trades and consultants, inspects work, certifies payments and manages the contractor relationship day to day.

**An architect with supervision in scope** covers part of both — checking that what is built matches the design — without necessarily doing the commercial side.

On a small build one person may cover it. Above roughly USD 500,000 the roles usually separate.

## Why it matters here specifically

Indonesia has **no residential builder licensing regime and no statutory domestic building warranty**. Whatever protection you have is the contract and the money you have not yet paid.

That means the **valuation of work and the verification of payment milestones** is the mechanism through which you actually control the project. If the contractor certifies their own progress, you have no mechanism at all.

And a build supervised closely finishes better, faster and cheaper than one run remotely — substantially so. Owners who are not present and pay for nobody to be present get a building that varies from the drawings in ways they discover at handover, when leverage is gone.

## Where a QS earns their fee

**Comparing tenders properly.** Three quotes against a complete bill of quantities are comparable. Three quotes against a set of drawings are three different interpretations, and the cheapest is usually the one that excluded the most.

**Pricing variations before they happen.** Verbal variations priced afterwards are the single most common cause of dispute and budget overrun on Bali builds. A QS prices the change and its time impact before the work is instructed.

**Valuing work for payment.** You pay for what is actually complete, verified, rather than for what a schedule says should be complete by now.

**Catching what the quote excluded.** Pool, landscaping, furniture, permits, architect fees, site works, utility upgrades, contingency. A structure quoted at USD 200,000 commonly finishes at USD 280,000 to USD 320,000 all in, and a QS puts that number in front of you at the start rather than at the end.

**Final account.** Reconciling what was contracted against what was built and what was paid.

## What it costs

Fees are market practice and scale with scope and project size, quoted either as a percentage of construction cost or as a fixed fee for a defined service.

**Architect with construction supervision** typically runs five to ten percent of construction cost for a full service including permit documentation.

Against a fifteen to twenty percent saving from proper tendering, variation control and payment verification, the fee is generally self-funding on a project of any size.

## The payment structure it enables

**Milestones tied to verified physical completion**, inspected by your QS or project manager — not calendar dates, not the contractor's own certification.

**Retention of five to ten percent**, held through a defects liability period after handover. This is the only leverage that survives handover, and a contractor with nothing outstanding has no reason to return.

**A meaningful final payment.** A structure where ninety-five percent is paid before handover leaves you powerless at the moment you most need power.

**Prompt release once verified**, because a contractor waiting on payment stops work and that is your delay too.

## When you can skip it

**A small, simple build** with a contractor you have used before, where you are present throughout and the specification is genuinely complete.

Below roughly USD 250,000, a properly scoped architect with supervision in the contract is frequently enough.

Above that, the fee is small relative to what it controls.

## What to ask before engaging one

What exactly is in scope — tendering, valuation, variations, site inspection frequency, final account.

Whether they are independent of the contractor, and how they are paid. A "project manager" recommended and effectively paid by the builder is not independent.

What they have delivered in Bali, and whether you can speak to those clients.

How often they visit site and what the reporting looks like.

## Common questions

### Do I need a quantity surveyor to build in Bali?
Above roughly USD 250,000, yes, or an architect with genuine supervision in scope. Below that a properly scoped architect is frequently enough.

### What does a quantity surveyor do on a build?
Prices the work from the drawings, produces a bill of quantities so tenders are comparable, values completed work for payment, prices variations before they happen, and reconciles the final account.

### How much does construction supervision cost in Bali?
An architect with full service including supervision typically runs five to ten percent of construction cost, which is generally self-funding against proper tendering and variation control.

### Why do Bali builds overrun on cost?
Incomplete specifications producing variations, verbal instructions priced afterwards, and quotes that excluded pool, landscaping, furniture, permits, site works and utility upgrades.

### How should payments be structured on a Bali build?
Against milestones verified by someone you appointed, never calendar dates or the contractor's own certification, with five to ten percent retained through a defects liability period.
