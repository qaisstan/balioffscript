In Indonesia the notary is not a formality. A land transfer is not valid until executed before a PPAT and registered, which makes this person central to whether you actually own anything.

## Notaris and PPAT

A **Notaris** is a public notary who draws up deeds generally: company formation, agreements, powers of attorney.

A **PPAT**, Pejabat Pembuat Akta Tanah, is specifically authorised to make land deeds and lodge them for registration. Most practitioners hold both roles, but the land transfer requires the PPAT capacity.

For a purchase you need a PPAT. For a PT PMA you need a Notaris. One person usually covers both.

## What they actually do

- Verify the certificate against the land office record
- Confirm the seller's identity and authority to sell
- Check for registered charges over the land
- Confirm spouse consent where the land is marital property
- Calculate and handle the transfer taxes
- Draft and execute the deed of transfer
- Lodge the transfer for registration
- Hold funds in some arrangements

That verification list is the value. It is also why the notary must be yours.

## Why the seller's notary is a problem

The seller's notary is not corrupt by default. But they were selected by the counterparty, they have an ongoing relationship with them, and their commercial interest is in the deal closing.

Your interest is in the deal being sound, including the possibility that it is not. Those are different jobs. Appoint and pay your own, and treat the fee as the cheapest insurance in the transaction.

Where the seller insists on their notary, you can still instruct your own lawyer to run parallel diligence. Refusal to allow that is itself information.

## Fees

Notary and PPAT fees run roughly 1 to 2.5 percent of value and are negotiable in practice. Alongside the buyer's transfer duty at around 5 percent, budget about 7 percent of the purchase price in total transaction costs.

Get the fee quoted in writing, with what it covers. Registration, tax handling and certificate collection should be specified rather than assumed.

## Choosing one

- Someone who works regularly with foreign buyers and will explain in English
- Someone independent of the seller and the agent
- Someone who will give you the verification findings in writing
- Someone who will tell you plainly when something is wrong

That last point matters most. A notary whose only output is "it is fine, sign here" is not doing the job you are paying for.

## What to insist on

Written confirmation of what was verified: certificate authenticity, registered holder, title type, remaining term, encumbrances, zoning, and boundaries. If it is not written down, it was not checked.

## Common questions

### What is a PPAT in Indonesia?
Pejabat Pembuat Akta Tanah, the official authorised to draw up land deeds and lodge transfers for registration. A land transfer is not effective without one.

### Do I need my own notary in Bali?
Yes. The seller's notary was chosen by the counterparty and has an interest in the deal closing. Independent verification is the point of the role.

### How much are notary fees in Bali?
Roughly 1 to 2.5 percent of value, negotiable. Total transaction costs including transfer duty and legal review run around 7 percent.

### What is the difference between a Notaris and a PPAT?
A Notaris draws up deeds generally; a PPAT is specifically authorised for land deeds. Most practitioners hold both, but the land transfer needs the PPAT capacity.

### Can a notary hold my deposit in Bali?
Some arrangements provide for it. Whatever the mechanism, the release conditions should be written, objective and tied to verification milestones.
