A Balinese cooking class is the most consistently well-reviewed activity on the island, and unlike most tourist activities it leaves you with something you keep.

## What a good class involves

**A market visit**, usually at 6 or 7am, to a local produce market, where you are shown the ingredients: galangal, kencur, lesser galangal, turmeric root, candlenut, shrimp paste, palm sugar and a dozen chillies with different jobs.

**Base genep**, the Balinese spice paste that underpins almost everything, ground by hand in a stone mortar. This is the actual lesson. Once you can make it, you can cook Balinese food anywhere.

**Five or six dishes**, typically including sate lilit, a curry, a vegetable dish with grated coconut, sambal, and a dessert.

**Eating what you made**, which takes an hour and is the point.

Most classes run three to five hours including the market, finish at lunch, and include hotel pickup within a defined area.

## What it costs

**300,000 to 600,000 rupiah** for a standard group class including market visit, ingredients, the meal and often a recipe booklet.

Private classes run 800,000 to 1,500,000. Classes at luxury resorts cost several times as much and are not necessarily better.

## Where

**Ubud** has the greatest concentration, many in villages outside the centre with a family kitchen and a rice field.

**Canggu, Seminyak and Sanur** have plenty, more convenient and usually less atmospheric.

**Amed, Sidemen and Munduk** have small family-run classes with almost no other visitors, which are often the best of all.

## How to pick one

- **Group size.** Six to ten is good. Twenty is a demonstration.
- **Does everyone cook?** Some classes have one wok per pair; some have one for the room.
- **Is the market included**, and is it a real market rather than a staged stall?
- **Vegetarian option**, which almost all offer and should be confirmed.
- **Is it a family kitchen or a hotel classroom?** Both work; they are different experiences.
- **Do you take the recipes home?**

## What you learn beyond the food

Balinese cooking is bound up with ceremony: the offerings that get made daily, what is prepared for a temple anniversary, why pork matters here and not on Lombok, why the spice paste is ground rather than blended. A good teacher will explain all of it, and the class becomes the best cultural hour of a trip.

## Other food experiences worth doing

**A warung crawl or food tour** in Denpasar or Ubud, which gets you into places you would never find.

**A coffee and cacao farm visit** in the highlands, at a working farm rather than a tourist plantation with caged civets.

**A night market**, at Gianyar or Sanur, which costs nothing and teaches you more about what people actually eat than any class.

**Learning to make offerings**, canang sari, which some homestays and cultural centres teach and which is a genuinely lovely thing to be shown.

## What you will actually use at home

Three things survive the trip. **Base genep**, the spice paste, which freezes well and turns any protein into something Balinese. **Sambal matah**, raw shallot, lemongrass, chilli and lime, which takes five minutes and improves everything. And the habit of grinding rather than blending, which changes the texture in a way that is obvious once you have done both.

Ingredients are the limitation at home: galangal, kencur, candlenut and fresh turmeric are the four to look for in an Asian supermarket, and the paste is a poor thing without them.

## Booking

Book a day or two ahead directly with the school rather than through a platform, which takes a commission and often puts you in a larger group. Most include pickup within a set radius; confirm yours is inside it, because a Canggu pickup for an Ubud class means a 6am start.

Morning classes include the market and are the ones to take. Afternoon and evening classes skip it and are shorter.

## Common questions

### How much is a cooking class in Bali?
300,000 to 600,000 rupiah for a group class including a market visit and the meal, and 800,000 to 1,500,000 for a private one.

### Where is the best cooking class in Bali?
Ubud has the widest choice, with village classes in family kitchens generally better than hotel classrooms.

### Do Bali cooking classes include vegetarian options?
Almost all do, and it is worth confirming when booking since shrimp paste is used in many base recipes.

### How long does a Bali cooking class take?
Three to five hours including a morning market visit, finishing with lunch.

### Can children do cooking classes in Bali?
Most classes take children, often at a reduced price, and it is one of the better family activities in wet weather.
