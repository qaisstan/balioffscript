Most people looking at Bali property arrive on a visa on arrival, spend thirty days trying to do due diligence properly, extend once, and leave with the work half done.

There is a category built for exactly this, and almost nobody uses it.

## The pre-investment visas

| Index | Type | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `C12` | Single-entry pre-investment | Commonly around 60 days, extendable |
| `D12` | Multiple-entry pre-investment | **180 days, extendable by a further 180: up to 12 months total** |

`D12` is the useful one. Multiple entry, six months at a time, up to a year in total. It is designed for site visits, due diligence, and meetings with counsel and agents before committing to an investment.

For anyone seriously evaluating a purchase, walking plots, verifying certificates at BPN, sitting with a notary, checking zoning at the regency office. That is the difference between doing it properly and doing it in a rush.

## What it does not authorise

Neither C12 nor D12 permits:

- Signing on as an employee
- Running a business day to day
- Drawing Indonesian income

They are visitor categories. Once the investment is actually made, you move to the relevant [E28 investor route](/visas/investor-kitas-e28a/).

## Why this matters for the due diligence itself

The checks that actually protect you take time, and several cannot be rushed:

- Certificate verification at the local BPN office through your own notary
- Zoning and RDTR confirmation at the regency planning office
- Confirming KDB and KLB for the specific parcel
- Establishing every setback in writing
- Confirming PBG and SLF status for anything already built
- Verifying the operator's NIB and business classification

Two to four weeks is a realistic minimum for thorough due diligence, and that assumes offices respond promptly. A thirty-day tourist visa with a flight home at the end of it produces exactly the pressure that makes people skip steps.

> If you are being told to move fast, that is the pressure working. Nothing legitimate in Indonesian land transfer requires you to skip a step.

## The comparison

| Route | Realistic for due diligence? |
| --- | --- |
| Visa exemption (under 30 days) | No |
| Visa on arrival: 30 days + one extension | Tight, and the extension eats a week |
| `C12` single entry | Workable for a focused trip |
| `D12` multiple entry, up to 12 months | Built for this |

If you are spending several hundred thousand dollars, the visa that lets you verify it properly costs a rounding error by comparison.

## What a visitor visa covers

Coming to Bali to view property, meet notaries, walk plots, sit with a lawyer and eventually sign as a buyer is not employment. You are spending money, not earning it.

That distinction is the whole basis of the visitor visa, and a property search sits comfortably inside it.

## What would cross the line

- Working remotely for your employer while you are here, which needs the E33G
- Operating a business, taking Indonesian clients, or earning locally
- Acting as an agent, introducing buyers, or taking commission
- Anything the enforcement position treats as work, including unpaid activity

Since April 2026, enforcement has treated activity rather than payment as the test. Unpaid volunteering, teaching and sponsored social posts have all been treated as violations. A property search is none of those things, but a "property trip" during which you also work your normal job is.

## How long a diligence trip should be

Longer than most buyers plan. Two weeks is a realistic minimum for a serious purchase, and more if construction is involved.

You need to see a plot at different times of day, in traffic, and if at all possible in rain. Wet season runs roughly November to March, and it is the only honest way to see how a street, a plot and its drainage actually behave. A road that floods in February looks perfect in July.

## What to accomplish while you are here

- Appoint your own notary, not the seller's
- Verify certificates at the land office
- Check zoning on the specific parcel
- Walk boundaries with the certificate in hand
- Confirm access rights and road width
- Drive the commute to the school, hospital and airport at peak hour
- Meet two owners in the area who are not connected to your seller

## Residency and buying

You do not need residency to buy through a leasehold or a PT PMA. You do need valid residency for Hak Pakai, which is the only route to a registered land right in your own name.

If personal ownership matters, resolve the visa question before the property one, because it changes which structures are available to you.

## Common questions

### What visa do you need to look at property in Bali?
A standard visitor visa covers viewing property, meeting notaries and doing your own research. You are not working, you are spending money.

### Can you sign a property contract on a tourist visa?
Executing a purchase in your own name as a buyer is not employment. What you cannot do is operate a business or earn locally, which is a different activity.

### How long should a property due diligence trip be?
Long enough to see the plot at different times of day, in traffic, and ideally in rain. Two weeks is a realistic minimum for a serious purchase.

### Do you need residency to buy property in Bali?
For Hak Pakai in your own name, yes. For a leasehold or a purchase through a PT PMA, residency is a separate question from the transaction itself.
