A wedding at a Bali villa can generate more revenue in two days than a fortnight of ordinary bookings. It also introduces a set of issues that nightly letting does not, and the ones that matter most are not commercial.

## The commercial case

**Rates are high.** Event hire is priced well above nightly accommodation, and a wedding typically books the villa for several days around the event.

**Bali is a genuine wedding destination**, particularly for Australian and Asian regional couples, with an established supplier ecosystem — planners, caterers, florists, photographers, celebrants.

**It fills shoulder season**, because weddings are planned around availability and price rather than around school holidays, and they book far ahead.

**Repeat and referral** are strong. Guests at a good wedding become guests.

## The licensing question

This is where it gets complicated and where most operators are not where they think they are.

**Accommodation and events are different activities.** A villa licensed for accommodation — where that licensing exists at all — is not automatically permitted to operate as an event venue.

Commercial event hosting may engage **separate licensing**, and the **KBLI classification** of the operating entity determines what it may lawfully do. A company classified for accommodation is not classified for event management.

**Bali closed new foreign-owned villa and homestay registration to PMA companies on 22 July 2026**, which affects the underlying accommodation position for foreign investors before you even reach the event question.

**Serving alcohol** at an event is a licensed activity in its own right, tiered by beverage category and tied to specific premises.

Confirm the position for your property and your entity specifically, with a local adviser, before marketing the villa for events. "Other villas do it" is not a licence.

## The neighbours, which matter more

This is the part that decides whether events work at your property long term.

**Noise carries**, particularly at night, particularly in a residential Bali street where houses are open-sided and close together.

**Bali communities are organised through the banjar**, the customary neighbourhood association, and it has real authority over what happens locally. A villa generating noise complaints is a villa with a problem the banjar can make persistent.

**Ceremonies.** Local temple ceremonies happen frequently and a wedding scheduled during one is a serious misjudgement.

**Traffic and parking.** Eighty guests arriving in a narrow lane is disruptive in a way eight guests are not.

The practical answer: **talk to the banjar before you start**, explain what you intend, agree what is acceptable — a cut-off time, a limit on numbers, a contribution to the community — and stick to it.

A villa hosting events with the community's agreement operates indefinitely. One doing it against local objection does not, and no permit protects you from that.

## The operational reality

**Wear.** Eighty people at a villa causes more wear in one night than a fortnight of normal guests. Budget for it.

**Staffing.** Events need more people than a normal stay, and the standard is higher.

**Insurance.** Public liability with a limit appropriate to the number of people attending, and confirmation your policy covers event use. A policy written for accommodation may exclude it.

**Damage deposits**, at a level proportionate to an event rather than a stay.

**Supplier coordination**, which a good planner handles and an owner should not be doing.

**Restoration time.** The villa is not available the day after.

## Where it works and where it does not

**Works:** properties with space, a view, a large outdoor area, and — crucially — distance from neighbours. Clifftop Bukit, larger inland plots, estates. Villas built or repositioned for it.

**Does not work:** dense residential streets in Canggu or Seminyak, small plots, anywhere the noise reaches a neighbour's bedroom. The revenue does not compensate for a permanently difficult relationship with the people around you.

## The honest recommendation

If your villa has the space, the setting and the distance from neighbours, events are a genuinely strong revenue line and worth pursuing properly — with the licensing confirmed, the insurance right, the banjar on side and a planner handling delivery.

If it does not, the money is not worth what it costs you locally. A villa that is unwelcome in its own street is a villa with a problem that appears every time you need cooperation on anything.

## Common questions

### Can I host weddings at my Bali villa?
Accommodation and events are different activities. Commercial event hosting may require separate licensing and a KBLI classification permitting it, so confirm your specific position before marketing.

### Do I need a licence to serve alcohol at a Bali event?
Yes. Alcohol licensing is tiered by beverage category and tied to specific premises, held by an entity with the appropriate classification.

### What is the main risk of hosting events at a Bali villa?
Neighbours. Noise carries in residential Bali streets, and the banjar has real authority. A villa generating complaints has a persistent problem no permit resolves.

### Should I talk to the banjar about events?
Yes, before you start. Agree a cut-off time, limits on numbers and a community contribution. A villa hosting events with local agreement operates indefinitely.

### Is event hire profitable for a Bali villa?
Rates are several times nightly accommodation and it fills shoulder season, but it requires space, distance from neighbours, higher staffing, event-appropriate insurance and restoration time.
