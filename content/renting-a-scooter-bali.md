Renting a scooter here takes about four minutes. Doing it in a way that does not cost you later takes a little longer.

## What it costs

- **Daily:** roughly 70,000 to 100,000 rupiah for a 110 to 125cc automatic.
- **Monthly:** roughly 900,000 to 1,500,000 rupiah, often including delivery to your villa.
- **Bigger bikes:** 150cc scooters and above cost more and are worth it only if you are riding long distances or carrying two adults up hills.
- **Fuel:** a few tens of thousands of rupiah fills the tank. Roadside stalls sell it in bottles, petrol stations are cheaper and cleaner.

## The licence question, answered properly

Indonesian law requires a licence valid for the vehicle. For visitors, that means **an international driving permit carrying the motorcycle category, issued in your home country before you travel**, alongside your home licence.

Two consequences follow.

**Police checks.** Random checks happen, more often in tourist areas, and an unlicensed rider is fined on the spot.

**Insurance.** This is the one that matters. Travel policies almost universally exclude motorcycle claims where the rider was not licensed for the machine, and many also exclude riding without a helmet. A hospital transfer and treatment after a serious scooter accident in Bali runs into tens of thousands of dollars, and medical evacuation home is more. People discover this from a hospital bed.

## What to check before you ride away

- **Brakes.** Squeeze both. Front should bite.
- **Tyres.** Look for tread and no bald patches. Wet-season roads with slick tyres are how people fall.
- **Lights and indicators**, both sides, plus the horn, which you will use constantly.
- **The mirrors.** Many rentals have them folded in. Fold them out and use them.
- **Photograph the bike** from all four sides before you leave, with the date visible. This is how you avoid paying for damage that was already there.
- **The registration document, the STNK.** It should be with the bike. Police ask for it.

## The passport question

Some rental shops ask to keep your passport as a deposit. **Do not hand it over.** Offer a cash deposit or a photocopy instead. A shop that insists on holding your passport is a shop that has leverage over you in any dispute, and there are hundreds of others.

## Helmets

The one supplied is usually adequate and rarely good. If you are here for a month, buy one for a few hundred thousand rupiah. Fasten the strap; an unfastened helmet does nothing and is also what police stop you for.

## Riding here, honestly

Traffic moves on the left. It flows rather than follows rules: people pull out expecting you to adjust, and the horn is used to say "I am here", not "I am angry".

The specific hazards are consistent:

- **Sand and gravel** on bends near beaches.
- **Dogs**, which run into the road without looking.
- **Rain**, which makes the first ten minutes of a downpour the most slippery roads of the year as oil lifts.
- **Trucks and ceremony processions** on narrow roads.
- **Other tourists** riding badly, often in swimwear with no helmet, which is a picture of exactly what not to do.
- **Alcohol.** Riding after drinking is how the worst of it happens.

## Where not to ride

If you are new to it, avoid the Kuta and Seminyak main roads, the Ubud centre one-way system, and the coastal run at sunset. Learn in the quiet lanes inland from Canggu or in Sanur, which is flat, wide and calm.

## What to wear

Cover your legs and feet at minimum. Shorts and flip-flops are what turn a slow fall into a month of dressings. Long trousers and trainers are unglamorous and they are what the residents wear when they are riding any distance.

## If you have an accident

Stop, move off the road, and take photographs. Call your insurer before treatment if you can. BIMC and Siloam are the international-standard clinics in the south and they are used to handling insurer paperwork. Keep every receipt.

## Common questions

### Do I need a licence to rent a scooter in Bali?
To ride legally, yes: an international driving permit with the motorcycle category plus your home licence. Rental shops often do not ask. Police and insurers do.

### How much does it cost to rent a scooter in Bali for a month?
Roughly 900,000 to 1,500,000 rupiah, usually with free delivery and collection.

### Should I give my passport as a scooter deposit in Bali?
No. Offer cash or a copy. Never leave the original with a rental shop.

### Is petrol easy to find in Bali?
Yes. Pertamina stations are everywhere in the south, and roadside stalls sell bottled fuel in the villages. Station fuel is cheaper and cleaner.

### What happens if police stop me on a scooter in Bali?
They will ask for your licence, the bike's registration and check your helmet. Without a licence expect an on-the-spot fine. Stay polite and ask for the official receipt.
