The Bukit has two coasts and foreign buyers overwhelmingly look at one of them. The eastern side — Sawangan, Melasti, Ungasan's southern edge — is cheaper, calmer and largely overlooked.

## What it is

**Sawangan** sits below Nusa Dua's southern edge, with a long white sand beach, several large resort properties and residential land on the slopes above.

**Melasti** is a dramatic beach reached down a cut through the limestone cliff, with calm turquoise water, beach clubs and a rapidly developing strip above it.

Both face east and southeast, which means **sunrise rather than sunset**, calmer water, and a different swell exposure from the famous western breaks.

## How it differs from the western Bukit

**Calmer water.** Melasti and Sawangan are swimmable in a way Uluwatu, Padang Padang and Bingin frequently are not. For families that is the whole difference.

**White sand**, against the west's mix.

**No surf culture.** The western Bukit's economy runs on waves and the loyal, repeat, direct-booking surf market that comes with them. The east does not have that, and it does not have the counter-seasonal calendar it produces.

**Cheaper land**, meaningfully, than western clifftop positions.

**Resort presence**, which anchors the area's positioning and brings a different guest.

**Closer to the airport** than Uluwatu, and much closer than Canggu.

## The market

Land on the eastern Bukit is cheaper than the western clifftops and there is more of it, with generous plots and long available lease terms.

Development has accelerated around Melasti as the beach's profile has grown, and prices have moved accordingly. It is no longer undiscovered and it is not yet expensive.

This is largely a land and build market — finished villa stock of the kind foreign buyers want is limited.

## Rental performance

Growing, family-weighted, and less seasonal than the western Bukit.

The guest is a family or a couple wanting calm swimmable water, space and a beach club within reach. That market is real and it overlaps with Nusa Dua's without the enclave's constraints or prices.

**What is absent** is the surf market, the nomad long-stay population and any walkable amenity beyond the beach clubs. There is no year-round demand floor of the kind Canggu has.

Properties that perform are three and four bedrooms with good pools and, where the topography allows, an ocean outlook.

## The water problem applies here too

The Bukit is **limestone** regardless of which coast you are on. Groundwater is deep, boreholes are expensive and not always successful, and mains supply is inconsistent.

A large number of Bukit properties run on **trucked water**, which is a genuine recurring cost and an operational risk in peak season when the whole peninsula needs it at once.

Establish in writing where the water comes from, what it costs annually and what happens in August. Vague answers here are the most useful warning sign available on the Bukit.

## The setback and enforcement position

The **coastal setback** — the sempadan pantai — applies, and **demolition action has been taken against unpermitted structures inside it on the Bukit, notably at Bingin**.

That is a realised outcome, not a theoretical risk, and it applies to this coast as much as the western one. Anything on or near the cliff needs a valid PBG and SLF matching the structure and confirmation it sits outside the setback.

## What else to check

**Zoning for the exact parcel**, and whether commercial accommodation is permitted.

**Access**, and whether rights of way are documented, since several parcels here are reached by lanes.

**Power capacity**, which is limited and expensive to upgrade.

**View protection** — an outlook over land somebody else owns and has not yet built on is not protected.

**Realistic comparables** from the eastern Bukit, not from Canggu or from western clifftop positions.

## Who it suits

Buyers wanting a Bukit position with calm swimmable water, at a lower entry price than the western clifftops, primarily serving families.

Anyone building, since good plots with full terms remain obtainable.

Buyers who want airport proximity and a beach without Canggu's traffic.

## Who it does not

Anyone wanting the surf market, its loyalty and its counter-seasonal calendar. That is on the other side of the peninsula.

Anyone unwilling to do the water diligence.

## Common questions

### Is Melasti a good place to buy property?
Land is cheaper than the western Bukit clifftops with generous plots and long terms, and the calm swimmable beach serves a family market the west does not.

### How is the eastern Bukit different from Uluwatu?
Calmer swimmable water, white sand, sunrise rather than sunset, cheaper land and no surf culture — which means no surf market and no counter-seasonal calendar.

### Does the water problem affect Sawangan and Melasti?
Yes. The whole Bukit is limestone with deep groundwater and inconsistent mains supply, and many properties run on trucked water regardless of which coast they are on.

### Are there setback restrictions on the eastern Bukit?
The coastal setback applies here as elsewhere, and demolition action against unpermitted structures inside it has been taken on the Bukit, so permits and setback position must be verified.

### Who books villas in Sawangan and Melasti?
Families and couples wanting calm swimmable water, space and beach clubs within reach, overlapping with Nusa Dua's market without the enclave's constraints.
