Most Bali villas sell furnished, and the owners who strip them first usually wish they had not.

## Why furnished sells better

**Empty villas photograph badly.** They read smaller, they look unfinished and they give a buyer nothing to react to. A furnished villa in good photographs sells on the images; an empty one has to sell on the floor plan.

**Buyers are frequently investors**, and an investor wants an asset that can take a guest next month. A furnished villa with an existing listing is a business. An empty one is a project with a USD 25,000 to USD 50,000 fit-out bill and three months before it earns.

**Furniture here is worth little on resale** and a great deal in place. Used pieces from a humid climate have a thin secondhand market, and the cost of storing, moving and selling them usually exceeds what they fetch.

**It removes a negotiation.** A sale where furniture is separately valued and argued over is a sale with an extra way to stall.

## What "furnished" should actually mean

Be specific in the agreement, because ambiguity here causes disputes at handover.

**An inventory**, itemised and photographed, attached to the contract. What stays, what goes.

**Personal effects excluded** explicitly.

**Anything you intend to remove named**, so it is not discovered missing on the final walkthrough.

**Consumables and linen** addressed — a buyer taking over an operating villa expects the towels and sheets that were in the photographs.

**Equipment**: pool gear, garden tools, maintenance items, spare parts. These are worth almost nothing and their absence is immediately annoying.

## When to sell empty

**If the furniture is genuinely tired.** Worn pieces make a good property look neglected, and a buyer mentally deducts the replacement cost anyway.

In that case, either refresh it before listing — paint, soft furnishings, styling, then rephotograph — or clear it and present a clean empty space rather than a shabby furnished one.

**If you want the pieces.** Legitimate, and price the sale accordingly rather than pretending it makes no difference.

**If the buyer asks.** Some buyers have their own plan and would rather not pay for what they will replace.

## What actually adds value at sale

In order of return per dollar:

**Professional photography**, which is the highest-return expenditure in the whole process and the thing sellers most often skip. It routinely moves the sale price by more than the entire agent commission.

**Paint**, the highest return per dollar of any physical work.

**The pool** — resurfaced if it needs it, spotless if it does not.

**The garden**, cleaned up with structure and lighting added.

**Fixing visible defects**: cracked tiles, stained grout, failing aircon, dead plants.

**Decluttering and depersonalising**, while leaving it furnished.

## What transfers besides furniture

This is where sales get complicated and it belongs in the agreement.

**Existing tenancy**, which binds the buyer. If the tenant paid twelve months in advance to you, apportion it.

**Management agreement**, with its notice period, termination cost and — crucially — who controls forward bookings on exit.

**Forward bookings**, where guests have paid and the obligation to house them transfers.

**Staff**, with accrued entitlements under Indonesian labour law, which for long-serving employees runs to many months of salary.

**The platform listing and its reviews.** This is genuinely valuable — an established listing skips the six to twelve month ramp-up a new one faces — and it does not always transfer cleanly, because platform accounts have their own rules. **Confirm in writing what happens to it**, from whoever controls the account.

## The practical recommendation

**Sell furnished**, with an itemised inventory, after refreshing anything tired and rephotographing the result.

Keep only what you genuinely want, name it in the agreement, and replace it before the photographs are taken so the images show what the buyer is getting.

The furniture is not where the money is. The photographs are, and furniture is what makes them work.

## Common questions

### Should I sell my Bali villa furnished?
Usually yes. Empty villas photograph badly and read smaller, investors want an asset that can take a guest next month, and used furniture has a thin secondhand market here.

### How much is villa furniture worth on resale in Bali?
Little, because humidity-worn pieces have a weak secondhand market and storage, moving and selling costs usually exceed what it fetches. It is worth far more in place.

### What should a furnished sale agreement include?
An itemised photographed inventory attached to the contract, personal effects excluded, anything you intend to remove named, and linen, consumables and equipment addressed.

### What else transfers when selling a Bali villa?
Existing tenancies, management agreements with their notice and forward booking provisions, forward bookings themselves, staff entitlements, and potentially the platform listing and its reviews.

### Does the platform listing transfer with a villa sale?
Not automatically, and it is genuinely valuable because it skips the ramp-up a new listing faces. Confirm in writing what happens to it from whoever controls the account.
