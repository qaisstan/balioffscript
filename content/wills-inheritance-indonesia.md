Most foreign owners here have never written this down, and the people who deal with the consequences are their families.

## The disposal rule

Indonesian land rights are tied to who may lawfully hold them. If a foreigner inherits a title reserved for Indonesian citizens, they generally must dispose of it within a set period. If they do not, the state's interest in it takes over.

That applies to a foreign spouse, foreign children, and anyone else who cannot hold the right in question. Grief plus a legal deadline plus a foreign language is a bad combination, and the deadline does not pause for any of it.

## Your home will is not enough

A will drafted in Australia, the UK or the Netherlands may be recognised in principle, but it does not by itself transfer or resolve an Indonesian land title. Practical problems follow immediately:

- Translation and legalisation of foreign documents takes time the deadline does not allow
- The executor has no standing here until it is established
- Bank accounts and company shares are separate questions from the land
- Where the title cannot be held by the heir, no will changes that

## What to actually do

**Write an Indonesian will covering Indonesian assets.** Keep it consistent with your home will so they do not contradict each other. Use an Indonesian notary.

**Know which right you hold.** Leasehold, Hak Pakai and HGB through a company all pass differently. A lease is contractual and should bind heirs and successors explicitly. HGB sits in a company, so the succession question becomes a shares question.

**Consider the company route deliberately.** Where property sits in a PT PMA, what passes is shares rather than land, which can be considerably simpler for foreign heirs. That is a real planning advantage and it is worth weighing when you choose the structure in the first place.

**Name someone here.** A trusted contact who knows which notary holds what, where the certificates are and who to call. Families lose months simply establishing what exists.

**Keep documents together.** Certificates, lease agreements, company deeds, tax numbers, insurance, the prenuptial or postnuptial agreement, and the will. One place, and someone who knows where it is.

## Mixed marriages

If you are married to an Indonesian citizen without a prenuptial or postnuptial agreement separating assets, deal with that first. It affects who owns what before it affects who inherits what.

## Timing

This is the item people postpone indefinitely because nothing forces it. The forcing event, when it comes, is the worst possible moment to start.

If you own property here and have not written an Indonesian will, that is the single highest-value legal task on your list.

## Common questions

### Does my foreign will cover my Indonesian property?
Not adequately. It may be recognised in principle but it does not resolve an Indonesian land title on its own, and translation, legalisation and establishing the executor's standing all take time.

### What happens if a foreigner inherits land in Indonesia?
Where the heir cannot lawfully hold the title, they generally must dispose of it within a set period. Failing to do so risks losing the value entirely.

### Can my children inherit my Bali villa?
It depends on the right you hold and their nationality. Leasehold can be drafted to bind successors. Property held through a PT PMA passes as shares, which is often simpler for foreign heirs than land held directly.

### Should I write a will in Indonesia?
If you hold property, a company or significant assets here, yes. Have it drafted by an Indonesian notary and kept consistent with your home country will.

### Is property in a PT PMA easier to inherit?
Often, because what passes is company shares rather than a land title the heir may not be able to hold. It is one of the genuine planning arguments for the company route.
