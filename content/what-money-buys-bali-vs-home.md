This comparison is usually made badly, by comparing a villa price to a house price. The useful version compares what a given monthly figure produces.

## USD 2,500 a month

**At home**, in most Australian, British, Dutch or North American cities: rent on a modest apartment, and not much else once bills are paid.

**In Bali**: a comfortable single life with room to spare. A decent villa or apartment, eating out regularly, a gym, a scooter, and help with cleaning.

## USD 5,000 a month

**At home**: a comfortable middle-class life, probably with a mortgage taking a large share.

**In Bali**: a family with children in international school, which is the single largest line, plus a villa, staff and a car with a driver.

## What the money buys that it cannot at home

**Space.** A pool and a garden are ordinary here at prices that buy a flat elsewhere.

**Time.** Domestic help and drivers are affordable in a way they are not in most Western countries. That is hours back every week.

**Climate and outdoors, year round.** Not a small thing over a decade.

**Proximity.** Six hours from Perth, well connected across Asia.

## What it does not buy

**Ownership.** No freehold, ever, for a foreigner. Whatever you spend, you hold a lease, a right of use, or a company's building right.

**Medical depth.** Routine care is excellent value. Complex care is limited and evacuation runs USD 55,000 to 100,000.

**Certainty.** Your right to be here is renewable, and the rules have moved quickly.

**Infrastructure.** Traffic, water, waste and drainage are real constraints, not inconveniences.

## The honest arithmetic

Bali buys more life per dollar and less security per dollar. That is the trade, and it is a good one for people whose security is elsewhere and a poor one for people putting everything here.

## Common questions

### Is Bali really cheaper than living at home?
For daily life, substantially. For education, insurance and anything requiring imported goods or complex healthcare, the gap narrows sharply.

### How much do you need to live well in Bali?
USD 1,800 to 2,500 a month for a comfortable single life, USD 2,500 to 3,500 for a couple, USD 4,000 to 6,500 for a family with school fees.

### What is more expensive in Bali than at home?
International schooling, health insurance with evacuation cover, imported goods, and anything requiring specialist medical care.

### Can I buy more property in Bali for the same money?
Considerably more space, yes. You cannot buy ownership at any price as a foreigner.

### Is it worth moving to Bali financially?
It works well for people with foreign income and security elsewhere. It works badly as a way to stretch limited capital, because the risks land on you.
