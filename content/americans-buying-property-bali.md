Yes, Americans can buy property in Bali, on exactly the same terms as every other foreigner. You cannot hold **Hak Milik**, Indonesian freehold. You can hold a **leasehold**, **Hak Pakai** if you hold an Indonesian residence permit, or **HGB** through your own foreign-owned company. Nothing about a US passport changes that, and nothing about it makes it harder.

What is different for Americans is the home side: the US taxes its citizens on worldwide income wherever they live, and it has reporting rules that catch people who did not know they existed.

## What you can own

| Route | What it is | Fits an American buyer who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Leasehold** | A notarised lease, usually 25 to 30 years, in your own name | Wants a holiday home or an income villa without moving here |
| **Hak Pakai** | A registered right of use in your own name | Lives here on a KITAS or KITAP |
| **HGB through a PT PMA** | A building right held by your own Indonesian company | Is running a genuine business |

Anything offered as freehold for foreigners is a nominee arrangement, which is void in Indonesian law and criminal in Bali since Perda 4/2026. The full comparison is in [leasehold vs freehold in Bali](/ownership/leasehold-vs-freehold-bali/).

## Three US habits that do not work here

**1. Expecting a title company, escrow and title insurance.** There is no title insurance, and no title company running the closing for you. Your protection is the checks your own notary runs before you pay: the certificate verified at the land office, the seller's authority, zoning, permits and the lease wording. See [the buying process](/ownership/buying-process-bali-step-by-step/).

**2. Assuming the agent works for you.** In Bali the agent is almost always paid by the seller. There is no buyer's agent duty in the US sense. Engage your own notary and, for anything significant, your own lawyer. See [agents, managers and who works for you](/rental/property-manager-vs-agent-bali/).

**3. Thinking of the villa as an asset you keep.** A lease ends, and at the end the land and building generally go back to the landowner. A US home appreciates on land you own; a Bali lease runs down every year. Price the term, and read the [extension clause](/ownership/leasehold-extension-clause/) before anything else.

## The US tax side, in plain words

This is general information, not tax advice. Talk to a US tax adviser who handles foreign property before you buy.

- **The US taxes citizens on worldwide income.** Rental income from a Bali villa goes on your US return, whether or not you bring the money home.
- **Indonesian tax paid can usually be credited** against US tax on the same income, so you are not simply taxed twice. Indonesia and the US have a tax treaty.
- **Foreign bank accounts.** If your foreign accounts together go above USD 10,000 at any point in the year, you file an FBAR. An Indonesian bank account used for the villa counts.
- **Owning a company abroad brings its own US forms.** If you hold a PT PMA, expect extra US reporting for foreign companies. This is where most Americans need an adviser most.
- **Selling.** A gain on selling the lease may be taxable in the US as well as in Indonesia, again usually with a credit for Indonesian tax.

On the Indonesian side, see [rental income tax](/tax/rental-income-tax-bali/) and [the tax when you sell](/tax/capital-gains-tax-indonesia-property/).

## Money, financing and currency

**No Indonesian bank will lend against a Bali lease**, because there is no registered interest to secure. Americans who borrow usually do it at home, against US property. See [financing property in Bali](/ownership/financing-property-bali/).

**Prices are often quoted in US dollars**, but contracts, taxes and running costs are in rupiah. Your return in dollars depends on the exchange rate, and moving a large sum through a bank at a 2 percent spread costs real money. See [paying for Bali property](/ownership/paying-for-bali-property/) and [currency risk](/tax/currency-risk-bali-property/).

## Living here, or just visiting

Buying property does not give you a visa. Americans usually come on a visa on arrival for shorter stays, and longer stays need a proper permit. See [does buying property give you a visa](/visas/does-buying-property-bali-give-visa/) and [the Second Home Visa](/visas/second-home-visa-indonesia/).

There are no nonstop flights from the US to Bali. Most routes connect through Asia or the Middle East, so most American owners visit less often than Australians, which makes a good manager more important. See [self-managing vs hiring a manager](/rental/self-manage-vs-villa-manager/).

## Common questions

### Can a US citizen own property in Bali?
A US citizen cannot hold Indonesian freehold, Hak Milik. You can hold a leasehold in your own name, Hak Pakai if you have an Indonesian residence permit, or HGB through your own foreign-owned company.

### Do Americans pay US tax on Bali rental income?
Yes. The US taxes citizens on worldwide income, so Bali rental income goes on your US return. Indonesian tax paid can usually be credited against the US tax on the same income.

### Do I need to file an FBAR for a Bali bank account?
If your foreign financial accounts together exceed USD 10,000 at any point in the year, yes. An Indonesian account used to run the villa counts toward that total.

### Can Americans get a mortgage for Bali property?
Not from an Indonesian bank on a lease. Americans who borrow usually do it at home, for example against US property.

### Is there title insurance in Bali?
No. Your protection is the checks your own notary runs before you pay, which is why using the seller's notary is the most expensive shortcut in the market.

### Does buying property in Bali give an American a visa?
No. Property does not come with a visa. Longer stays need a separate permit, such as a KITAS or the Second Home Visa.
