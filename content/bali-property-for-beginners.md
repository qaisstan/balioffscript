Most beginner guides start with areas and prices. Those are the last two things that matter.

## The one fact everything follows from

**A foreigner cannot own land in Indonesia.** Hak Milik, the freehold title, is reserved for Indonesian citizens. This is constitutional, long-standing, and no structure changes it.

Everything sold as "freehold for foreigners" is either one of the lawful alternatives described inaccurately, or a nominee arrangement described optimistically.

## The three lawful routes

| Route | Plain English |
| --- | --- |
| Leasehold | You rent the land for a fixed number of years under contract |
| Hak Pakai | A registered right of use in your own name, needs a KITAS |
| HGB via PT PMA | Your Indonesian company holds a registered building right |

If someone offers you a fourth, it is a nominee, and you are not the owner.

## The order to learn things

1. **Which route applies to you.** This changes what you can even look at.
2. **What the property can lawfully earn.** Short-term letting is licensed, and Bali closed the villa classification to new foreign-owned companies in July 2026.
3. **How to verify.** Certificate at the land office, zoning on the parcel, boundaries walked, access registered, PBG and SLF present.
4. **What it really costs.** Around 7 to 8 percent on top, plus fit-out, plus running costs.
5. **Then** areas and prices.

## What you can ignore at the start

Area debates. Yield league tables. Whether Canggu is finished. Anything about the next hotspot. All of it is downstream of whether you can hold and lawfully operate the thing you are buying.

## The single habit that protects you

Pay nothing until a named check has been completed and written down. Not a holding deposit, not a good-faith payment, not a reservation fee.

Everyone who lost money here broke that rule first, and discovered the actual problem second.

## When to get help

Before you view anything, not after you have fallen for something. The structure decision is cheap to get right at the start and expensive to unwind later.

## Common questions

### Can a foreigner buy property in Bali?
Not freehold. Through a leasehold, Hak Pakai with residency, or HGB held by a PT PMA.

### What is the safest way to buy in Bali as a beginner?
Hak Pakai if you hold residency and want a home, or a well-drafted long lease reviewed by a notary you appointed.

### How much money do I need to start?
Whatever the asking price, add roughly 7 to 8 percent for transaction costs, plus furnishing if you will rent it, plus a buffer for a slow first year.

### Do I need to live in Bali to buy?
No, though Hak Pakai in your own name requires valid residency. Leasehold and company routes do not.

### What is the first thing I should do?
Decide the ownership structure. It determines which properties are even available to you and stops you falling for the ones that are not.
