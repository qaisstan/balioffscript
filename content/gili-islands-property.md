The Gilis sit off northwest Lombok: Trawangan, Meno and Air. They are administratively part of West Nusa Tenggara rather than Bali, and they are small enough that everything about them is constrained.

## The three islands

**Gili Trawangan** is the largest and busiest, with the bars, dive shops and most of the accommodation.

**Gili Air** is the middle option, with a growing residential and long-stay community and a calmer feel.

**Gili Meno** is the quietest and least developed, aimed at couples and people who want nothing to happen.

## The legal position

The same national framework applies as everywhere in Indonesia. No freehold in a foreign name, with leasehold, Hak Pakai for those with residency, or HGB through a PT PMA.

Land title on the Gilis has historically been more complicated than on Bali. Customary holdings, incomplete registration and competing family claims are more common. Verification through a notary you appointed, against the land office record, is not optional here.

## The constraints

| Constraint | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| Fresh water | Very limited. Desalination and shipped water are normal |
| Electricity | Improved via undersea cable, still fragile |
| Waste | A genuine and visible problem on small islands |
| Transport | No motor vehicles. Everything moves by cart, bicycle or boat |
| Medical | Basic only. Anything serious means a boat to Lombok or Bali |
| Construction | Every material arrives by boat, which changes the cost base |

Building here costs meaningfully more than on Bali for exactly that last reason, and timelines are longer.

## Demand and risk

Tourism demand is real and has recovered strongly. It is also concentrated, seasonal, and dependent on boat conditions. Rough seas cancel crossings, which cancels arrivals you had already counted.

The 2018 Lombok earthquakes caused serious damage and a collapse in visitors that took years to recover. That is not a reason to avoid the islands, but it belongs in the risk assessment alongside seasonality.

## Who it suits

Operators rather than passive investors. People who will run a business here, or spend substantial time, and who can tolerate illiquidity and infrastructure that is genuinely limited.

The resale market is thin. Plan to hold.

## Common questions

### Can foreigners buy land on the Gili Islands?
Under the same rules as the rest of Indonesia: leasehold, Hak Pakai with residency, or HGB through a company. No freehold in a foreign name.

### Are the Gili Islands part of Bali?
No. They belong to West Nusa Tenggara, off northwest Lombok, and are reached by boat from Bali or Lombok.

### Is water a problem on the Gili Islands?
Yes. Fresh water is very limited and properties rely on desalination or shipped supply, which is a real operating cost.

### Is it more expensive to build on the Gilis?
Considerably. Every material arrives by boat and there are no motor vehicles for onward transport, which raises cost and lengthens timelines.

### Is Gili property a good investment?
It suits operators with a long horizon who can tolerate thin liquidity, seasonal and weather-dependent demand, and limited infrastructure.
