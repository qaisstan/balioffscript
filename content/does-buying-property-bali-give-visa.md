Does buying property in Bali give you a visa? No, not by itself. Buying a villa, a lease or a plot of land gives you no right to live in Indonesia. Residency is a separate application with its own conditions.

There is one route where property can count, and it is set high enough that it rarely applies.

## The short answer

- **A normal villa purchase gives you no visa or residency.**
- **The Second Home Visa** can be based on qualifying property of around **USD 1,000,000**, or on a deposit of around **USD 130,000** in an Indonesian state-owned bank. Almost everyone uses the deposit. See [the Second Home Visa](/visas/second-home-visa-indonesia/).
- **Buying a villa is not a basis for an investor visa.** Investor routes require a qualifying company, not property. See [the Golden Visa](/visas/golden-visa-indonesia/).
- **Residency matters for property in one specific way:** you need a KITAS or KITAP to hold [Hak Pakai](/ownership/hak-pakai-explained/) in your own name.

## Which visa property owners actually use

**Second Home Visa.** Five or ten years of residence, no sponsor, based on funds held. The right fit for owners who want to live here and have no business. The deposit must usually be maintained, not paid once, so confirm how it must be held before you move the money.

**Retirement KITAS.** For older buyers, with age and income conditions. See [the KITAS guide](/visas/kitas-bali-guide/).

**Spouse or family KITAS.** If you are married to an Indonesian citizen. Do the prenuptial agreement before buying if you want a registered right in your own name.

**Investor KITAS through a company.** Only if you genuinely run a qualifying foreign-owned company. The investment has to be the company, not the villa.

**Visiting to buy.** To view property and do due diligence, see [which visa to come and check out a property](/visas/visa-for-property-due-diligence/).

## Owning is not working

This boundary matters for every owner. **Owning a villa and receiving rental income is not work. Operating it is.** Handling bookings, managing staff and instructing contractors count as work, whatever visa you hold.

Engage a manager and stay an owner. It keeps your position clean and the villa usually runs better too.

## What this means when you buy

- **Do not structure a purchase around a visa you have not secured.** Requirements change. Get the visa position confirmed in writing first.
- **If you want Hak Pakai, plan the residency first**, because the right sits on it.
- **A leasehold does not need residency.** It is the most common route for buyers who come and go.
- **A large purchase does not shortcut immigration.** The USD 1 million property route exists, and the deposit route is usually the cheaper way to the same visa.

## Common questions

### Can you get residency in Bali by buying property?
Only through the Second Home Visa's property route, which requires qualifying property of around USD 1,000,000. A normal villa purchase gives no residency. Most people use the Second Home Visa's deposit route of around USD 130,000 instead.

### Does Indonesia have a golden visa for property?
Indonesia's Golden Visa is based on investment through a qualifying company or other defined categories, not on buying a villa. Property ownership alone does not qualify as the investment.

### Do you need a visa to buy property in Bali?
Not for a leasehold, which foreigners can hold without residency. You do need a KITAS or KITAP to hold Hak Pakai in your own name, and you need the right visa to visit and do due diligence.

### Can I live in my villa in Bali?
Yes, as long as you hold a visa that lets you stay for the time you spend here. Owning the villa does not give you that visa, so plan your residency separately.

### Can I manage my own villa rental on a tourist visa?
No. Owning the villa and receiving rental income is not work, but operating it, handling bookings, staff and contractors, is. Use a manager and stay an owner.
