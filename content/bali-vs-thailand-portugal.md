These get compared as if they are the same decision. They are not. The difference is not price or yield. It is what you actually end up holding.

## The structural difference

**Portugal.** A foreigner can own freehold outright. Your name, permanent, inheritable. `[Verify the current residency and visa position separately, it has changed recently]`

**Thailand.** A foreigner can own a condominium unit, subject to building quotas, but cannot own the land under a house. Leases are typically limited. Structurally closer to Indonesia than most people assume.

**Bali.** No outright ownership in any form. Leasehold, a registered right to use, or a company-held right. Every option runs on a clock.

If certainty of ownership is what you are buying, the answer is not Bali. That is not a criticism, it is just what the law says.

## What Bali has that the others do not

Demand density. Nearly 7 million foreign arrivals in 2025, up almost 10%, on an island you can drive across in a few hours. Nightly rates that support genuine hospitality yields.

Portugal gives you security and modest returns. Bali gives you returns and a clock. Thailand sits between them.

## The comparison people should actually run

Not gross yield against gross yield. Run **net return after every cost, and after amortising a time-limited right over the years remaining.**

Compare that against a freehold market where nothing amortises. It is the only comparison that means anything, and it usually narrows the gap considerably.

## How to choose

Ask what the money is for.

**Preserving capital and passing it on?** A market where you own the asset outright.

**Producing income over a defined period you are comfortable with?** Bali is competitive and the demand is not in question.

**Both?** They are rarely the same asset, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling one of them.
