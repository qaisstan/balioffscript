Furniture is one of the genuine advantages of living in Bali. Solid timber made to your specification costs less than flat-pack does at home, and it is better.

## The makers

**Jepara**, in Central Java, is Indonesia's furniture manufacturing centre, and a large share of what is sold in Bali is made there. Ordering through a Bali workshop that manufactures in Jepara is common and it is where the price advantage comes from.

**Bali workshops** across Kerobokan, Denpasar and the Gianyar corridor produce to order, in teak, suar, mahogany and reclaimed timber.

**Seminyak and Kerobokan showrooms** carry finished pieces at retail prices, which is the convenient and expensive end.

**Gianyar and Sukawati**, inland toward Ubud, are the traditional craft areas — carving, stone, textiles — and prices are lower than the tourist-facing south.

## Made to order versus off the shelf

**Made to order** is the reason to furnish here. You specify dimensions, timber, finish and detail, and it costs surprisingly little more than buying something that nearly fits.

For a villa with awkward dimensions, a specific aesthetic or built-in requirements, this is transformative. Custom joinery — wardrobes, kitchens, bedheads, shelving — built to fit costs a fraction of what it would elsewhere and looks considerably better than assembled furniture.

**Allow time.** Custom work takes weeks and makers are busy, particularly ahead of the season. Ordering furniture after a build completes delays your first booking by a month or more, during which the property costs money and earns nothing.

## What is genuinely good value

**Solid timber** — teak, suar, mahogany, reclaimed. Excellent, cheap by international standards and suited to the climate if properly treated.

**Rattan and natural fibre**, which suits the setting and costs little.

**Stone and terrazzo** for basins, tables and surfaces.

**Textiles**, locally woven and printed.

**Custom joinery**, which is the single best value item on this list.

**Lighting**, locally made in rattan, bamboo and metal.

## What to buy carefully or import

**Mattresses.** Quality varies enormously and this is where a cheap decision shows up in guest reviews. Pay for a good one.

**Appliances**, where reliability matters and budget options fail early in this climate.

**Anything with electronics**, in high humidity.

**Outdoor fabrics**, where UV and mould resistance determine whether it lasts six months or six years.

**Upholstered furniture**, which needs the right foam and fabric or it will smell and sag within two years.

## The climate rule

Humidity, salt near the coast and constant use are hard on furniture.

**Solid timber survives** if treated and maintained. **Particle board does not** — it swells and delaminates. **Foam and fabric degrade.** **Metal rusts**, particularly near the sea.

Buy timber and natural materials. Avoid anything with a laminated composite core. Specify marine-grade fixings near the coast.

## Budgeting for a rental villa

A three-bedroom furnished to a standard that rents and photographs well runs **USD 25,000 to USD 50,000**. One bedroom from around USD 12,000; four bedrooms up to USD 70,000.

It is almost never included in a build quote.

**Replacement is an operating cost**, not a surprise. Soft furnishings look tired in two to three years and outdoor pieces faster. Roughly ten to fifteen percent of the original fit-out value per year is a working figure for a busy villa, held in the capital reserve.

Cheap furniture is not cheap. It is replaced twice as often and photographs worse throughout.

## Practical advice

**Get quotes from three makers** against a drawing or a clear specification, not a description.

**See their completed work**, ideally something a few years old.

**Pay in stages against progress**, not fully in advance.

**Check the timber** actually is what was quoted. Teak and teak-finished are different things.

**Buy with the photographs in mind.** Which pieces appear in the cover image, what colour they are, and how the outdoor setting reads. A villa is sold by images.

**Do not over-furnish.** Space photographs well; a cluttered room reads smaller.

**Keep spares** of linen, glassware and crockery, because replacing by ones and twos is inefficient and things break constantly.

## Common questions

### Is furniture cheap in Bali?
Locally made solid timber is a fraction of imported equivalents and better suited to the climate. Custom joinery built to fit is the best value available.

### Where is furniture made in Bali?
Much of it is manufactured in Jepara in Central Java and ordered through Bali workshops, with local makers across Kerobokan, Denpasar and the Gianyar corridor producing to order.

### What furniture should I avoid buying in Bali?
Anything with a particle board or laminated composite core, which swells in the humidity, plus cheap mattresses, budget appliances and outdoor fabrics without UV and mould resistance.

### How long does custom furniture take in Bali?
Weeks, and makers are busy ahead of the season. Ordering after a build completes can delay a first booking by a month or more.

### How much does it cost to furnish a Bali villa?
USD 25,000 to USD 50,000 for a three bedroom to a rentable standard, from around USD 12,000 for one bedroom, and it is almost never included in a build quote.
