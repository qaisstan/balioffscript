The east coast around Amed and Candidasa is Bali's diving coast, and it is priced at a fraction of the south for reasons that are entirely rational.

## What it is

**Amed** is a string of small fishing villages along a dry, dramatic stretch of coast in the far east, below Mount Agung. Black sand and pebble beaches, traditional salt production, and the calm water of the Lombok Strait.

The **USAT Liberty wreck at Tulamben**, a short distance north, is one of the most dived sites in Indonesia and is accessible directly from shore. That single feature underpins much of the area's visitor economy.

**Candidasa** sits further southwest, a slightly more developed resort strip that has been quiet since coastal erosion damaged its beach decades ago, with breakwaters now in place.

Both are in **Karangasem regency**, and both are roughly two to two and a half hours from the airport.

## The market

**Land is very cheap** by southern standards, with coastal and elevated positions available at a fraction of Canggu or the Bukit. Plots are large and lease terms available are long.

Existing stock is small hotels, dive resorts and guesthouses rather than villa product of the kind foreign buyers usually look for. This is a land and build market.

## Rental performance

**Thin, seasonal and specific.**

The guest is a diver or a snorkeller. That is the core of the demand and it is a genuinely loyal market — divers return to sites they like, they book direct, and they stay several nights.

Beyond diving there is a small quiet-seeking market, and traffic from people on multi-day Bali itineraries.

What is absent: any nomad market, any meaningful long-stay foreign resident base, any walkable amenity of the Canggu type, and any year-round demand floor. The quiet months are genuinely quiet.

Occupancy that would be a failure in the south is normal here. A well-presented property with a sea view and easy dive access can hold decent rates because quality supply is limited, but filling the calendar is the constraint.

## The Agung question

**Mount Agung** is an active volcano and the 2017 to 2019 eruption sequence closed the airport repeatedly and emptied the east of visitors for an extended period.

Amed sits close to it. Hazard zones are mapped and building within them is restricted — establish where your parcel sits relative to them before committing, because this is specific, checkable information that is not always volunteered.

Insurance in the east is more expensive and harder to place, and **volcanic events are frequently excluded**. Read the exclusions rather than assuming cover.

This is not a reason to avoid the east. It is a reason to know exactly what you are taking on and to price it.

## The other constraints

**Water.** The east is markedly drier than the south, and supply should be established specifically rather than assumed.

**Access.** Roads in the coastal villages are narrow and steep in places, which affects construction logistics and guest arrival.

**Erosion**, particularly relevant at Candidasa, where the historical beach loss is the reason the area is quiet.

**Power capacity**, which is limited this far out and expensive to upgrade.

**Medical**, which is limited locally.

## Who it suits

Buyers who want land, a view and quiet at a price that makes the purchase a small part of their position, and who are not relying on rental income.

Anyone building a personal home on the coast.

Divers, and people with a specific connection to the area.

Long-horizon buyers taking a ten to twenty year view on the east developing.

## Who it does not

Anyone needing yield or liquidity. The resale market here is very thin and a sale can take years, during which a leasehold term runs down.

## What to check

Volcanic hazard zoning for the parcel. Karangasem zoning generally, including agricultural classification which is extensive. Water source and reliability. Road access and construction logistics. Power capacity. Coastal setback and erosion history. Insurance availability and exclusions. And comparables from the east coast itself, not from the south.

## Common questions

### Is East Bali coast worth buying?
As a lifestyle purchase or a long-horizon land position at very low prices, it can be. As a yield investment it is not — demand is thin, seasonal and driven almost entirely by diving.

### Why is Amed so cheap?
It is two to two and a half hours from the airport, has no walkable amenity or resident base, and sits close to an active volcano whose 2017 to 2019 eruptions emptied the region for an extended period.

### What drives tourism in Amed?
Diving and snorkelling, particularly the USAT Liberty wreck at Tulamben which is accessible from shore and is one of the most dived sites in Indonesia.

### Is it safe to build near Mount Agung?
Hazard zones are mapped and building within them is restricted. Establish where the parcel sits relative to them, and check whether insurance excludes volcanic events.

### Why is Candidasa quiet?
Coastal erosion damaged its beach decades ago and the area never recovered its earlier position, which is why breakwaters are in place and prices are low.
