Every month someone flies to Bali, sees how fast villas sell off-plan, and decides to become a developer. Some do well. Many end up with a half-built project, unhappy buyers and a company that cannot legally do what it is doing. The difference is almost always the order in which things are done.

## The company: a PT PMA

A foreigner cannot develop in their own name. You need a **PT PMA**, a foreign-owned Indonesian company.

- **Paid-up capital:** since October 2025, at least **IDR 2.5 billion**, held in the company's account.
- **Investment plan:** still more than **IDR 10 billion per business code (KBLI) per location**, not counting land and buildings.
- **Business codes:** you need the codes that cover what you actually do: real estate on your own or leased land, building, renting out, managing. In 2026 Bali restricted new PT PMA registrations in a number of business codes, and the list has moved during the year. Check what is open in OSS, with a notary, **before** you commit to land.

## The land: the title decides everything

A PT PMA can hold land on **HGB** (right to build), usually for 30 years with extensions, or lease land from an Indonesian owner. Check, before any deposit:

- the **zoning colour** of the plot and what may be built there;
- the **building limits** (KDB/KLB, height limit, setbacks);
- that it is not **agricultural land** under the province-wide ban, or in a regency under the hotel and accommodation moratorium;
- the **road access**, water and power;
- who really owns it, and whether every heir has signed.

## Permits before you sell

You need a **building permit (PBG)** before you build and a **certificate of function (SLF)** before anyone lives in it. Developers who sell off-plan without a PBG are the source of most of the horror stories in Bali, and buyers are learning to ask for it.

## Selling

Foreign buyers cannot own your villas outright. You sell **leases** (or sub-leases of your HGB), with a notary, with clear payment stages tied to real progress on site, a fixed handover date, a fair delay penalty and the buyer's right to cancel after a long-stop date. A fair contract sells better than a clever one.

## The money

- Budget more than you think for **permits, infrastructure (roads, drainage, power) and time**.
- Do not fund phase 1 with phase 2 money. When a project stalls in Bali, this is usually why.
- Keep the buyers' money traceable and separate where you can.

## Common questions

### Can a foreigner build and sell villas in Bali?
Yes, through a PT PMA with the right business codes, land on a proper title, permits in place and leases sold through a notary.

### How much money do I need to start as a developer?
The company itself needs IDR 2.5 billion paid up and an investment plan above IDR 10 billion per business code per location, before land and buildings. A real project usually needs far more.

### Can I sell villas off-plan before I have a building permit?
You should not. Buyers increasingly ask for the PBG, and selling without it is the most common reason projects end in disputes.

### Is it worth becoming a developer in Bali?
It can be, on the right land with the paper in order and enough money to finish without new buyers' cash. The market rewards developers who deliver on time, because so many do not.
