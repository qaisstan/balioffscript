Bali's interior is rivers, gorges and volcanic ridges, and there is a well-developed adventure industry built on it. Quality varies, and the difference between operators is mostly about equipment and guide ratios.

## White-water rafting

**The Ayung River**, near Ubud, is the gentle one: class II to III, about two hours on the water, through a gorge with carved rock walls and waterfalls. Suitable for families and complete beginners, and genuinely scenic.

**The Telaga Waja**, in the east near Sidemen, is more exciting: class III to IV depending on water level, faster, with a drop at the end.

**Cost:** roughly 350,000 to 700,000 rupiah per person including transfers, gear, a guide and lunch.

**Season:** year-round. Wet season means higher, faster water and occasional cancellations.

## Canyoning

Descending gorges by abseil, jump and slide, with guides and full kit, in the rivers around Gitgit, Munduk and Ubud. Several routes at different difficulty levels, from gentle half days to serious full days.

This is the activity where operator quality matters most: ropes, helmets, guide certification and group size. Use the established operators, ask what qualifications the guides hold, and do not choose on price.

**Cost:** roughly 1,000,000 to 1,800,000 rupiah per person.

## Cycling

**Downhill from Kintamani.** The standard tour: transfer to the crater rim, then a mostly downhill ride through villages, rice fields and plantations to the lowlands, with stops. Easy, suitable for most people, and one of the better ways to see rural Bali.

**Road cycling** is a real scene here, with long climbs to Kintamani and Bedugul and organised group rides from the south.

**Mountain biking** in the hills above Ubud and around Tabanan.

**Cost:** downhill tours roughly 400,000 to 800,000 rupiah including bike, guide and transfers.

## ATV and buggy

Widely advertised, usually on purpose-built tracks through mud, jungle and rice fields near Ubud. Fun, muddy, and the safety standards vary considerably. Check that helmets are provided and fitted, that there is a briefing, and what the guide-to-rider ratio is.

## Other things

**Ziplines and treetop courses** at Bedugul and Ubud.

**Paragliding** at Timbis on the Bukit, launching from the cliff with the coast below, conditions permitting.

**Sea kayaking** at Amed, Menjangan and the Nusas.

**Freediving** courses at Amed and on the Gilis.

**Bungee jumping** in Seminyak.

**Volcano trekking**, Batur for the accessible version and Agung or Rinjani for the serious one.

**Snorkelling and diving**, covered in [diving and snorkelling in Bali](/places/bali-diving-snorkelling/).

## The questions to ask before booking any of it

- What is the guide-to-participant ratio?
- What safety equipment is provided, and what condition is it in?
- What certification do the guides hold?
- What is the plan if someone is injured, and how far is the nearest clinic?
- Does my travel insurance cover this activity? Several policies exclude canyoning, quad bikes and volcano trekking outright.

The cheapest operator in any of these categories is cheap for a reason, and in rafting and canyoning that reason is usually equipment or staffing.

## When to do what

**Rafting** is best in wet season and shoulder months, when the rivers are high. In late dry season the Ayung can be slow.

**Canyoning** runs year round and is dictated by water levels; operators cancel when flow is dangerous, which is a sign of a good one.

**Cycling and ATV** are more pleasant in dry season, and the ATV tracks are much muddier, and much more fun, after rain.

**Volcano treks** are dry season activities. A clouded-out Batur sunrise in February is a common disappointment.

**Paragliding at Timbis** depends entirely on wind, which is most reliable in dry season afternoons.

## Booking and cost

Book directly with operators or through your accommodation rather than a street agent, and confirm what the price includes: transfers, gear, insurance, lunch and park or village fees. Most activities in Bali are cheaper booked the day before on the ground than through an international platform, and the operator keeps more of it.

## Common questions

### Is white-water rafting in Bali safe?
With established operators, yes. The Ayung is gentle class II to III and suitable for families; the Telaga Waja is faster and more demanding.

### How much does rafting cost in Bali?
Roughly 350,000 to 700,000 rupiah per person including transfers, equipment, guide and lunch.

### What is the best adventure activity in Bali?
Canyoning for experienced adventurers, downhill cycling from Kintamani for a gentler day, and the Ayung rafting trip for families.

### Does travel insurance cover adventure activities in Bali?
Often not by default. Canyoning, quad bikes, volcano trekking and diving beyond a set depth are commonly excluded or need an add-on.

### Can children do adventure activities in Bali?
Ayung rafting, downhill cycling and ziplines all take children, usually with minimum age or height limits.
