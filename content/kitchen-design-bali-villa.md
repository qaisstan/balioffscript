Kitchens in Bali villas are frequently specified as though nobody will use them. Families and groups cook, which makes it one of the few genuine differentiators available.

## Why it matters commercially

**Families cook.** A family on a two-week stay eats out some nights and cooks the rest, and a kitchen that cannot support that costs you the booking.

**Groups cook together**, which is part of why they took a villa rather than hotel rooms.

**Long-stay guests cook constantly.** The nomad and monthly segment that fills February and March is not eating out twice a day for six weeks.

**It appears in the listing filters.** Guests search on it.

A villa with a kitchen that works for eight people is competing for bookings a villa with a token kitchenette is not.

## What a working villa kitchen needs

**Bench space**, which is the thing most often short. Two people preparing food at once need room, and a kitchen with a sink, a hob and forty centimetres between them does not work.

**A full-size fridge**, and for larger villas a second one or a freezer. Groups arrive with a week of shopping.

**A hob with four burners**, and an oven, which a surprising number of Bali villas do not have.

**Storage**, properly ventilated.

**A dishwasher** in anything above three bedrooms. It changes the experience and it reduces housekeeping time.

**Equipment that matches the bedroom count** — enough plates, glasses, pans, knives that cut. A six-bedroom villa with four saucepans is a complaint.

**A kettle and good coffee equipment**, which is mentioned in reviews more than almost anything else in the kitchen.

**Somewhere to eat**, indoors and out, with enough seats for the maximum occupancy.

## The climate constraints

**Humidity destroys composites.** Particle board and MDF carcasses swell, delaminate and fail. This is the single most common kitchen failure in Bali villas and it happens within a few years.

**Specify solid timber, marine ply or blockboard** for carcasses. It costs more and it survives.

**Stone or terrazzo worktops**, made locally and inexpensively, rather than laminate.

**Stainless steel corrodes near the coast** unless it is a decent grade. Specify marine-grade fixings and hardware within reach of the sea.

**Ventilation matters more than extraction aesthetics.** A closed kitchen in this climate grows mould. Airflow, and the ability for things to dry, is a design requirement.

**Keep food storage sealed.** Ants find everything, and open shelving with loose packets is a housekeeping problem every week.

## The open kitchen question

Many Bali villas put the kitchen in the open living pavilion, which looks excellent and has consequences.

**Insects and dust** reach it constantly.

**Cooking smells and heat** spread through the living space.

**Aircon is impossible**, which in a kitchen in this climate is a real comfort issue for whoever is cooking.

**It photographs beautifully**, which is why it keeps being done.

The workable compromise most good villas use: **an enclosed, air-conditioned working kitchen** with a serving relationship to an open dining and living area. You get the photograph and the function.

## Custom joinery, which is the local advantage

Bali and Java give you access to craft at a price that does not exist elsewhere.

**A kitchen built to fit**, in solid timber with stone worktops, made to your specification, costs a fraction of an imported fitted kitchen and looks considerably better.

Use it. A villa with a generic imported kitchen has thrown away one of the few material advantages of building here.

**Allow time.** Custom work takes weeks and makers are busy ahead of the season.

## What to check when buying an existing villa

**Carcass material.** Open a cupboard and look at the edges — swelling or delamination means composite in a humid climate and it will get worse.

**Whether there is an oven**, and whether the hob works properly.

**Equipment against bedroom count**, which is cheap to fix and immediately noticeable.

**Ventilation and any mould** in cupboards.

**The fridge**, which is a significant item and frequently at end of life.

## Common questions

### Does a villa kitchen affect bookings in Bali?
Yes. Families, groups and long-stay guests all cook, and a kitchen that supports eight people is competing for bookings a token kitchenette is not.

### What kitchen materials last in Bali?
Solid timber, marine ply or blockboard carcasses with stone or terrazzo worktops. Particle board and MDF swell and delaminate in the humidity within a few years.

### Should a Bali villa kitchen be open or enclosed?
Most good villas use an enclosed air-conditioned working kitchen with a serving relationship to an open dining and living area, which gives the photograph and the function.

### What is most often missing from a Bali villa kitchen?
Bench space, an oven, a dishwasher above three bedrooms, and equipment matched to the bedroom count. A six-bedroom villa with four saucepans generates complaints.

### Is a custom kitchen worth it in Bali?
Yes. Built to fit in solid timber with stone worktops, made locally, it costs a fraction of an imported fitted kitchen and is better suited to the climate.
