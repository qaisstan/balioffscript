Indonesia does not generally permit dual nationality. The main exception runs through childhood and ends with a deadline that families miss.

## The window

Under the **2006 citizenship law**, a child of a marriage between an Indonesian citizen and a foreign national may hold **limited dual citizenship until the age of eighteen**, plus a period afterwards — commonly described as up to three years — within which the child must **declare** which nationality they choose.

During that window the child can hold **both passports**, travel on either, and is treated as Indonesian in Indonesia.

**If no declaration is made within the period, the Indonesian citizenship is generally lost.**

## Why it matters more than an identity question

**An Indonesian citizen can hold Hak Milik, Indonesian freehold. No foreigner can, ever, under any structure.**

So the declaration at eighteen decides whether that person can own land in Indonesia for the rest of their life.

For a family with property here, that is an inheritance and asset-holding decision. A child who declares foreign nationality becomes a **foreign heir**, and where a foreigner becomes entitled to Indonesian freehold through inheritance, disposal rules apply within a defined period.

A child who retains Indonesian citizenship does not have that problem.

## Getting it right from the start

**Register the child's status properly.** Where the birth occurred abroad, it must be **reported to the Indonesian representative** in that country and registered here, within time limits. Documents need **legalisation** — by apostille, since Indonesia is a party to the Hague Convention — and **sworn translation**.

A child whose Indonesian status was never registered may not have the dual position at all, which removes the choice before it is ever made.

**Obtain the Indonesian passport** and keep it current through childhood, because it is the practical evidence of the status.

**Keep the documents together** — birth registration, both passports, the parents' marriage registration — somewhere reachable from another country.

## The deadline, practically

**Diarise the eighteenth birthday and the declaration deadline.** Put it somewhere that survives house moves, changes of email address and the child leaving home.

This is the single most useful thing in this article. Families lose the entitlement through nothing more than nobody having written the date down.

## Making the declaration

It is a formal process, and the consequences are permanent.

**Take advice before it**, because the decision has property, inheritance, tax, military service and travel implications that differ by the other nationality involved.

Consider what the young person actually wants, not only what is convenient. They are the one living with it.

## Travelling on two passports

While the window is open, a dual national child can travel on either, and the practical rule is to **enter and leave Indonesia on the Indonesian passport** and use the other elsewhere.

Mixing them — entering on one and leaving on the other — produces immigration record inconsistencies that cause problems later, including at the declaration.

## If citizenship was already lost

A former Indonesian citizen, and in some routes a descendant of one, may access immigration routes that are more straightforward than those open to an unconnected foreign national.

**Recovering citizenship** is a separate and demanding process, and it means giving up the other nationality. It is worth understanding before committing capital to a thirty year lease, because holding Hak Milik and holding a wasting leasehold are not comparable positions.

**Residency alone**, without citizenship, still makes **Hak Pakai** available — a registered right of use in your own name, which is the strongest position available to a non-citizen.

## Common questions

### Can a child have both Indonesian and foreign citizenship?
Under the 2006 citizenship law, a child of a mixed marriage may hold limited dual citizenship until eighteen, plus a declaration period in which they must choose one.

### What happens if the declaration deadline is missed?
The Indonesian citizenship is generally lost, which permanently removes the ability to hold Hak Milik freehold in Indonesia.

### Why does my child's passport matter for property?
An Indonesian citizen can hold freehold land and no foreigner can, so the declaration at eighteen decides whether that person can ever own land in Indonesia.

### Which passport should a dual national child travel on?
Enter and leave Indonesia on the Indonesian passport and use the other elsewhere. Mixing them produces immigration record inconsistencies that cause problems later.

### Can Indonesian citizenship be recovered after it is lost?
There is a process, and it is demanding and requires giving up the other nationality. Former citizens and descendants may also access more straightforward immigration routes.
