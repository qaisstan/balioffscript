The Philippine rule is simple and strict: land is constitutionally reserved for Filipino citizens.

## What a foreigner can hold

**Condominium units**, provided foreign ownership stays within 40 percent of the building. Above that quota, no further foreign sales in that building.

**Buildings without the land**, through long-term lease arrangements on the land beneath.

**Through a corporation** that is at least 60 percent Filipino-owned, which carries the same nominee risk that Thailand and Indonesia now enforce against.

## Against Indonesia

| | Philippines | Indonesia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land | Constitutionally reserved | Reserved for citizens |
| Condominium | Yes, within 40% of a building | Hak Pakai on strata, with conditions |
| Term on a unit | Perpetual | Term-limited |
| Marriage route | Spouse may own, foreigner cannot | Requires asset separation agreement |
| Tourism demand | Domestic-heavy, dispersed | International, concentrated |

The Philippine condominium is a stronger instrument than anything Indonesia offers on land, because it is perpetual. The constraint is that the market for it is thinner and more dispersed.

## Why the money still favours Bali

Philippine tourism is real but geographically scattered across thousands of islands, with weaker international flight connectivity to any single destination. Bali concentrates international demand in one place with one airport and a mature villa rental economy.

For a buyer whose return depends on filling a calendar, concentration matters more than constitutional generosity.

## The marriage trap, in both countries

A foreigner married to a Filipino citizen cannot circumvent the land rule through the spouse without creating problems. Indonesia has the same issue and a defined solution: a prenuptial or postnuptial agreement separating assets.

In both countries, treating a spouse as a convenient title holder is the same prohibited structure with more at stake than money.

## Common questions

### Can foreigners own a condo in the Philippines?
Yes, provided foreign ownership in that building stays within 40 percent of the total units.

### Can foreigners own land in the Philippines?
No. Land ownership is constitutionally reserved for Filipino citizens, with narrow exceptions such as inheritance by former citizens.

### Is the Philippines better than Bali for property?
The condominium title is stronger because it is perpetual. Bali has far more concentrated international tourism demand, which matters more for rental income.

### Can my Filipino spouse buy land for me?
The spouse can own land, but structuring it so the foreigner is the real owner creates the same problems as a nominee arrangement elsewhere.

### What is the 60-40 rule?
A corporation holding land must be at least 60 percent Filipino-owned. Using nominee shareholders to satisfy that is the same risk being enforced against across the region.
