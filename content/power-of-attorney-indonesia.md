A power of attorney lets someone act on your behalf. In Indonesia it is a normal instrument with legitimate uses and one very common abuse.

## Legitimate uses

- Executing a purchase or sale when you cannot attend in person
- Company formation and administrative steps for a PT PMA
- Tax registration and filings
- Managing a property while you are abroad
- Banking, within limits set by the bank

For a foreign buyer who cannot be in Indonesia at the moment a deed is executed, a properly drafted and legalised power of attorney is the ordinary solution.

## Getting one recognised

A power of attorney executed abroad generally needs notarisation, then legalisation or apostille in the issuing country, then translation by a sworn translator here.

That chain takes time. Start it weeks before it is needed, not the week of the deed, because a document that arrives without the right stamp simply does not work.

## The limits

This is the part that matters.

**A power of attorney cannot authorise what the law prohibits.** It gives someone the ability to act for you within the law; it does not extend what either of you may lawfully do.

So a power of attorney does not let a foreigner acquire Hak Milik. It does not make a nominee arrangement enforceable. It does not convert a prohibited structure into a permitted one by adding paperwork.

## The nominee misuse

Nominee arrangements are typically wrapped in exactly this instrument: an irrevocable power of attorney allowing the foreigner to sell, mortgage or transfer the land, alongside a loan agreement and a statement of beneficial ownership.

The intent is to give the foreigner practical control over land they cannot lawfully own. That intent is the problem. An agreement whose purpose is circumventing a statutory restriction is not a clever structure, it is evidence of the circumvention.

Since Perda Bali 4/2026, facilitating such an arrangement is itself prohibited, which reaches the advisers who draft them.

## Practical safeguards

- Draft it narrowly. A power to complete one specific transaction, not a general power over your affairs
- Put an expiry date on it
- Name the specific property or company and the specific acts authorised
- Use a notary you appointed
- Keep the original secure and know how to revoke it
- Never sign a blank or undated transfer document

That last point comes up more than it should. A signed, undated deed handed to someone else is not a safeguard, it is an unlimited authority.

## Common questions

### Can I buy property in Bali without being there?
Yes, through a properly drafted power of attorney, legalised in your country and translated by a sworn translator here. Allow weeks for the process.

### Does a power of attorney let a foreigner own land in Indonesia?
No. It authorises someone to act within the law on your behalf. It cannot grant a right the law does not permit you to hold.

### Is an irrevocable power of attorney safe in a nominee structure?
No. It is the standard paperwork of an unlawful arrangement, and facilitating one has itself been prohibited in Bali since Perda Bali 4/2026.

### How do I make a foreign power of attorney valid in Indonesia?
Notarise it, then legalise or apostille it in the issuing country, then have it translated by a sworn translator. Confirm the exact chain with your Indonesian notary first.

### Can I revoke a power of attorney in Indonesia?
Yes, through your notary, and you should know the process before you grant one. Narrow scope and an expiry date reduce the need.
