When one person in a family obtains a work, investor or retirement permit, the rest of the family is covered through a dependant arrangement rather than through permits of their own.

## What it covers

A **spouse** and **children** of a KITAS or KITAP holder, sponsored by that person rather than by an employer or an institution.

It permits **residence**. It does not permit work, and it does not create an independent position.

## The dependency, which is the defining feature

The permit is derived. If the principal's permit ends — employment finishes, a company closes, a renewal is missed, a sponsor withdraws — **the dependants' permits end with it.**

That has two practical consequences.

**Plan changes together.** A principal changing employer needs a new permit, and so does everyone attached to them. Arrange both before the old employment ends, not after.

**A gap affects everyone.** A late extension by the principal puts the whole family into overstay simultaneously, with a daily fine accruing **per person**, including children.

## What the application involves

**The principal's permit and sponsor documents.**

**Proof of relationship** — a marriage certificate for a spouse, birth certificates for children. Foreign documents need **legalisation** by apostille, since Indonesia is a party to the Hague Convention, and **sworn translation**.

**Address documentation**, and post-arrival reporting including an **SKTT** for each family member.

**Personal documents** for each dependant.

Allow time. Legalising and translating foreign civil documents is slower than people expect and it is the step that delays most family applications.

## Work and study

**A dependant spouse cannot work** on the dependant permit. Employment requires the employer to obtain RPTKA approval and a work permit in the spouse's own name — which is a separate application and not automatic.

This catches couples where one partner has a job offer and the other assumes they can pick something up. Plan for it: either the second partner obtains their own permit on its own basis, or accepts that they will not be working in Indonesia.

**Children can attend school.** International and local schools enrol children on dependant permits routinely.

## Children who are Indonesian citizens

If one parent is an Indonesian citizen, the children's position is different and considerably better.

Under the **2006 citizenship law** a child of a mixed marriage may hold **limited dual citizenship until eighteen**, plus a declaration period afterwards in which they must choose one nationality.

**An Indonesian citizen child can hold Hak Milik, freehold land.** No foreigner can, ever. So that declaration at eighteen is an asset-holding and inheritance decision as much as an identity one.

Register the child's status properly, and record the deadline somewhere that survives house moves.

## Ageing out

A dependant permit for a child is available while they are a child. There is an age at which it ceases to apply, and at that point the young person needs their own basis — study, employment, or leaving.

That is not a surprise if you plan for it and it is a genuine problem if you do not. It arrives at the same time as university decisions, which is a busy moment to discover an immigration deadline.

## Toward KITAP

Dependants of a KITAP holder may in time have their own route to permanent status. Requirements differ by category and have changed, so verify the current position for the specific circumstances.

**Continuity matters** here as everywhere — a gap caused by a late renewal can reset a qualifying clock for the whole family.

## Practical advice

**One calendar for the whole family.** Every permit's expiry, diarised a month ahead.

**Update addresses for everyone** when you move, or the mismatch surfaces at renewal for each person individually.

**Legalise and translate civil documents early**, and keep the legalised originals somewhere reachable from another country.

**Understand the principal's position**, because everyone's depends on it.

## Common questions

### What is a dependant KITAS in Indonesia?
A residence permit for the spouse and children of a KITAS or KITAP holder, sponsored by that person, permitting residence but not work.

### Can a dependant spouse work in Indonesia?
No. Employment requires the employer to obtain RPTKA approval and a work permit in the spouse's own name, which is a separate application and not automatic.

### What happens to family permits if the main permit ends?
They end with it. A change of employer, a missed renewal or a sponsor withdrawal affects the whole family simultaneously, so changes have to be planned together.

### Can children on a dependant permit go to school in Indonesia?
Yes. International and local schools enrol children on dependant permits routinely.

### What documents prove a family relationship for a dependant permit?
Marriage and birth certificates, legalised by apostille since Indonesia is a party to the Hague Convention, and translated by a sworn translator.
