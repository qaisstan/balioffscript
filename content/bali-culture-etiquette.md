Bali is Hindu in a country that is overwhelmingly Muslim, and religious practice is woven into daily life to a degree foreigners consistently underestimate.

## Ceremony is the calendar

Temple ceremonies, family ceremonies, cremations and village obligations are frequent and take priority. Staff will need time off for them, and it is not negotiable in the way a foreign employer might assume a day off is.

Budget for it, plan around it, and treat it as a fixed feature of operating here rather than an inconvenience. Employers who fight it lose good staff.

**Nyepi**, the day of silence in March, shuts the entire island for 24 hours including the airport. No traffic, no lights, nobody outside, guests included. Plan bookings around it.

## Banjar

The banjar is the village community organisation, and it has real authority over what happens locally: construction, ceremonies, disputes, security, waste.

If you build or operate here, your relationship with the banjar matters more than most foreign owners expect. It can affect construction access, noise complaints, staff and whether small problems get resolved quietly or escalate.

Introduce yourself. Contribute when asked. This is not corruption, it is community obligation, and it is how the system works.

## Practical etiquette

- Step over offerings on the pavement, not on them
- Cover shoulders and knees at temples; a sarong is usually provided or required
- Do not enter a temple during menstruation, which is a stated rule rather than a suggestion
- Use the right hand for giving and receiving
- Do not touch heads, including children's
- Feet are impolite; do not point them at people or shrines
- Public anger loses you standing; raised voices are read as loss of control, not authority

That last one matters commercially. Negotiating hard is fine. Shouting marks you as someone to be managed rather than dealt with.

## The environment

Bali's waste and water problems are real and visible, particularly in wet season when rivers carry rubbish to the beaches.

As an owner you make choices here: waste handling, water use, whether the pool and garden are appropriate to a plot in a place with a finite water supply. Locals notice which foreigners take it seriously.

## Why this is commercial, not sentimental

Foreigners who understand the ceremonial calendar staff their villas better. Those who know the banjar build with fewer obstructions. Those who behave well locally get told about problems early rather than discovering them formally.

It is not a lecture about respect. It is how things actually function.

## Common questions

### What is Nyepi in Bali?
The Hindu day of silence, usually in March. The island shuts for 24 hours including the airport, and everyone including tourists stays indoors.

### What is a banjar?
The village community organisation, with real authority over local matters including construction, ceremonies and disputes. Your relationship with it matters if you build or operate here.

### Do Balinese staff get time off for ceremonies?
Yes, frequently, and it takes priority. Plan and budget for it rather than treating it as an exception.

### What should I wear at a Balinese temple?
Shoulders and knees covered, with a sarong and sash. These are usually available at the entrance and are required rather than optional.

### What is the biggest cultural mistake foreigners make in Bali?
Losing their temper in public. It reads as loss of control and damages your standing in a way that is hard to repair.
