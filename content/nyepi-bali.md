Once a year Bali switches itself off. Nyepi, the Balinese day of silence, is the new year of the Saka calendar, and it is observed by the whole island rather than by the devout.

## What actually happens

From 6am on Nyepi until 6am the next day:

- **The airport closes.** No arrivals, no departures, for 24 hours. This is the only airport in the world that closes annually for a religious observance.
- **No vehicles on the roads**, except ambulances and genuine emergencies.
- **Nobody leaves their property.** Including visitors. Hotels and villas ask guests to stay inside the grounds.
- **No lights visible from outside** after dark, no fires, and curtains drawn. Some hotels run dim interior lighting only.
- **No noise, no music, no work.** Television and radio stations traditionally suspend broadcasting, and mobile internet has been restricted in some years.

**Pecalang**, the traditional village security, patrol the streets and will ask anyone outside to go back in. They are polite and they are not negotiable.

## The four rules behind it

Nyepi observes four prohibitions: no fire or light, no work, no travel, and no entertainment or indulgence. The philosophical purpose is a full stop: a day of self-reflection at the turn of the year, and an island so quiet that any malevolent spirits passing overhead conclude it is deserted and move on.

## The night before is the opposite

**Ngrupuk**, the evening before Nyepi, is the loudest night of the Balinese year. Every village parades **ogoh-ogoh**, enormous demon effigies built over weeks from bamboo and papier-mâché, through the streets with gamelan, torches and crowds, then burns them.

If you are in Bali at Nyepi, this is the thing to see. Ask locally where the village parade starts, go early, and expect roads to close.

## What you can do on the day itself

Inside your accommodation: swim, read, eat what the kitchen prepared in advance, sleep. Hotels plan for it and most serve food normally. Small guesthouses may ask you to order in advance.

**What you cannot do:** go to the beach, go for a walk, drive anywhere, or use the pool after dark with the lights on in some properties.

The sky on Nyepi night is the clearest you will ever see over Bali, because the island's light pollution is switched off. That alone is worth the day.

## Should you plan around it?

**If your trip is a week, avoid landing in the two days either side.** Losing a day of seven to a locked gate, plus a day either side to airport closure, hurts.

**If your trip is longer, come for it.** The parade the night before and the silence the day after are among the most extraordinary things in Southeast Asia, and no photograph conveys the quiet.

The date moves each year and usually falls in March. Confirm it before you book flights, because airlines will not rebook you out of a closure they published years in advance.

## Practical preparation

- **Buy what you need two days ahead.** Shops, markets and delivery apps all stop.
- **Ask your accommodation** what they serve and whether the kitchen operates.
- **Charge everything.** Power stays on, but plan as if amenities are reduced.
- **Do not book a flight for the day itself or the first hours of the next morning**, when the backlog clears.
- **If you have a medical condition**, know where the nearest clinic is; emergency services do run.

## The other silent days

Nyepi is the island-wide one. Individual villages also observe their own quiet days and ceremonies through the year, including **Nyepi Segara** on some coastal and island communities, where the sea itself is closed to boats and swimming for a day. Nusa Penida and Lembongan observe their own versions, which occasionally strands day-trippers who did not check.

## Common questions

### When is Nyepi in Bali?
It follows the Saka lunar calendar and usually falls in March. The date changes every year, so confirm before booking flights.

### Does Bali airport really close for Nyepi?
Yes, completely, for 24 hours. No commercial arrivals or departures.

### Can tourists go outside on Nyepi?
No. Visitors stay within their hotel or villa grounds for the full 24 hours, and traditional village security enforce it.

### Do hotels serve food on Nyepi in Bali?
Most do, often as a set menu prepared in advance. Smaller guesthouses may ask you to order ahead. Delivery does not operate.

### Is it worth being in Bali for Nyepi?
For a longer trip, yes. The ogoh-ogoh parade the night before is spectacular and the silence is unlike anything else. For a short trip, plan around it.
