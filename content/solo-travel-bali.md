Bali is one of the easiest places in the world to travel alone. It has a large permanent foreign community, a dense layer of hostels, cafes, gyms and classes designed around people who arrived by themselves, and a culture that treats a solo traveller as normal rather than as a puzzle.

## Where to base yourself

**Canggu.** The most social place on the island. Hostels with pools, co-working, surf, gyms, running clubs, night markets. If you want to know 15 people by Thursday, stay here.

**Ubud.** The other magnet, with a different crowd: yoga, courses, wellness, quieter evenings, more conversation. Excellent for solo travellers who do not want a bar scene.

**Uluwatu.** Surf-oriented and spread out. Good if you ride and surf, isolating if you do not.

**Sanur and Amed.** Calm, older, easy, less social. Good for a few days of nothing.

**Nusa Lembongan and the Gilis.** Small enough that you meet everyone whether you intended to or not.

## Meeting people

The infrastructure is unusually good: hostel common areas, surf lessons, yoga studios with drop-in classes, cooking classes, free run clubs, co-working spaces with events, and language exchanges. Community boards and local groups on messaging apps list something every night in Canggu and Ubud.

The honest caveat is that a lot of the social layer in Canggu revolves around drinking, and if that is not what you are here for, Ubud will suit you more.

## Safety

Bali is safe for solo travellers by international standards, including for women, and the behaviour that keeps you safe here is the behaviour that keeps you safe anywhere: tell someone where you are going, keep an eye on your drink, and do not ride a scooter after drinking.

The specific risks, in order of how likely they are to affect you:

1. **The roads.** Solo or not, the scooter is the risk. Helmet, licence, no alcohol, no riding at night on unfamiliar roads.
2. **The sea.** Rip currents on the west coast are strong and most beaches are unpatrolled. Swimming alone at an empty beach is the bad version of this.
3. **Drink spiking and methanol.** Rare, and real. Stick to sealed bottles and busy licensed venues, avoid free-pour spirits at very cheap parties, and do not leave a drink unattended.
4. **Petty theft.** Bag snatching from passing scooters in busy streets. Bag across the body, on the far side from the road.

**For women travelling alone:** unwanted attention is generally mild compared to much of the region, and the usual firm, uninterested response ends it. Dressing modestly away from the beach draws less attention and is also simply respectful. Solo walking at night in the unlit lanes of Canggu and Pererenan is the one thing worth avoiding; a Gojek costs a dollar.

## Practical solo things

**Accommodation.** Hostels are excellent and cheap, private rooms in guesthouses run 20 to 35 USD, and single occupancy in a hotel costs the same as double, which is the one financial penalty of solo travel here.

**Eating alone** is completely unremarkable. Warungs, cafes and even good restaurants are full of people eating by themselves.

**Transport.** A scooter transforms solo travel here if you can ride. If not, budget for app rides and the occasional driver.

**Working.** Bali is one of the world's largest remote-work hubs, with the co-working and cafe infrastructure that follows. If you plan to work rather than holiday, understand which permit that requires before you arrive rather than after, and read [what the rules actually say](/visas/e33g-remote-worker-kitas/).

## The thing nobody says about solo trips here

Bali is very easy to spend three weeks in without ever being alone, and also very easy to spend three weeks in a bubble of other foreigners. If the second one bothers you, go east: Sidemen, Amed, Munduk, or across to Lombok. The island changes entirely twenty kilometres from the strip.

## Common questions

### Is Bali safe for solo female travellers?
Generally yes, and it is a popular destination for it. Roads and sea are bigger risks than crime. Avoid walking alone at night in unlit lanes and use a ride app instead.

### Where should solo travellers stay in Bali?
Canggu for a social scene and surf, Ubud for wellness and a quieter crowd. Both have excellent hostels and drop-in classes.

### Is it easy to meet people in Bali?
Very. Hostels, surf lessons, yoga studios, run clubs and co-working spaces all exist for exactly that.

### Is it awkward to eat alone in Bali?
No. It is completely normal and nobody notices.

### Is Bali expensive for solo travellers?
The accommodation cost per person is higher than for couples, since single occupancy costs the same as double. Everything else is the same.
