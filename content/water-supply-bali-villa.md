This is the diligence item most often skipped and most expensive to discover afterwards.

## The three sources

**PDAM**, the municipal piped supply. Reliable where it exists, cheap, and it does not reach everywhere. Coverage is patchy outside the main settled areas and pressure varies.

**A bore well.** Common on larger plots. Groundwater extraction is regulated and requires permits, and depth and yield vary enormously by geology.

**Trucked water.** Delivered by tanker into a storage tank. This is the fallback where the first two are unavailable or insufficient, and it is a real, recurring operating cost.

Many villas run a combination: piped supply where available, a well for the garden and pool, and a tanker in dry season when the others fall short.

## Why the Bukit is different

The Bukit peninsula is limestone: porous, elevated and historically thin on accessible groundwater. Properties there frequently rely on trucked water, and in a dry year the cost and availability both move against you.

If you are buying on the Bukit, ask specifically how the property is supplied, what it costs per month with the villa full in dry season, and what happens in a bad year. It is the question sellers there are least prepared for and the one that most affects your operating budget.

## What to ask before buying

- Is there a PDAM connection, and is it active?
- If there is a well, does it hold the required permits?
- What is the well's yield, and has it been tested in dry season?
- Where is the storage tank and what is its capacity?
- What did the property spend on water in the last twelve months?
- Is there a filtration system, and when was it last serviced?

Ask for actual bills or delivery invoices rather than an estimate. A seller who cannot produce twelve months of water cost is telling you something.

## Storage matters

Even on piped supply, a villa needs storage. Pressure drops, supply interrupts, and a pool plus guests plus laundry is a demanding load.

Tank capacity is a design decision that is cheap at build stage and expensive to retrofit.

## Drinking water

Tap water is not drinking water here. Villas either run a filtration system or buy the standard refillable gallon bottles. Guests expect drinking water provided, and it is a small consumable cost.

## The regulatory direction

Groundwater extraction has come under increasing scrutiny in Bali as tourism demand has grown against a finite resource. Assume regulation tightens rather than loosens, and be sceptical of a property whose entire supply depends on an unpermitted well.

## Common questions

### Do Bali villas have running water?
Yes, from municipal supply, a bore well, trucked delivery, or a combination. Which one determines both reliability and cost.

### Can you drink tap water in Bali?
No. Villas run filtration or supply bottled water. Guests expect drinking water to be provided.

### Do you need a permit for a well in Bali?
Groundwater extraction is regulated and permits are required. A villa relying on an unpermitted well carries a real risk, and scrutiny has been increasing.

### How much does trucked water cost in Bali?
It varies by area and season, and rises in dry season when demand peaks. Ask the seller for twelve months of actual delivery invoices rather than an estimate.

### Is water a problem on the Bukit?
It is the defining constraint there. The limestone geology holds little accessible groundwater, and many clifftop properties rely on trucked supply.
