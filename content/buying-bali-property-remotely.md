People buy Bali property without setting foot on it, and some of them do fine. The ones who do not are almost always the ones who skipped the same three things.

## What visiting actually gives you

**The street at different times.** Noise from a bar, a junction, a mosque or a construction site. Traffic at the hour guests actually travel. What the area is like on a Tuesday rather than in a photograph.

**The neighbours**, and what they might build. A rice field or ocean view over land somebody else owns is not protected, and standing there is how you understand what is at risk.

**The condition.** Water pressure at the highest point. Whether the pool holds water overnight. Whether there is a smell near the septic soakaway. Damp, mould, cracks, termite evidence.

**The drive.** From the airport, at the hour a guest lands. From the property to the beach, to a restaurant, to anything.

**The feel**, which is not a small thing. Plenty of people have bought a property that was correct on paper and wrong for them.

None of that is available through a screen.

## If you are buying remotely anyway

**Engage your own notary**, not one introduced by the agent. This is the only independent check in the transaction and it matters more, not less, when you are not present.

**Engage an independent surveyor** to inspect the building and walk the boundary against the certificate. A few hundred dollars, and they are your eyes. Instruct them directly and pay them directly.

**Commission your own photographs and video**, taken by someone you engaged, including the things a seller would not photograph — the access road, the neighbours, the equipment housing, the septic area, the ceiling in every room.

**Get someone to visit at different times** and report. Early morning, evening, after rain.

**Verify everything documentary**: certificate at the land office, seller's entitlement, zoning for the exact parcel, PBG and SLF against the building, encumbrances, existing leases and management agreements, PBB receipts.

**Read the extension clause** and have your notary state in writing whether it is *jaminan* or *prioritas*.

## The power of attorney

You will need one, and it is a legitimate use of the instrument — authorising your notary or a representative to complete while you are abroad.

**Have your own notary draft it.** Narrow scope: this specific transaction, this specific property, these specific acts, with an expiry date.

**Be careful about receiving funds.** Whether your attorney may receive money on your behalf is a decision to make deliberately, not a default.

**Executed abroad, it needs legalisation** — apostille, since Indonesia is a party to the Hague Convention — and **sworn translation** before an Indonesian authority will accept it. Allow time.

**Know how to revoke it**, and revoke it when the purpose is served.

## The payment structure

This matters more remotely than in person.

**Bank transfer from an account in your own name** to the registered owner or to a **notary's client account** — never to an agent's personal account, never to a third party "for tax reasons".

**Funds held by your own notary**, released against written conditions: verification complete, certificate confirmed, deed executed.

**Staged, against verified milestones** on anything off-plan or under construction, inspected by someone you appointed — not calendar dates, not the developer's own certification.

**Never pay before verification is complete.** Remote buyers are disproportionately pressured on this, because the seller knows you cannot easily reassess.

## What to refuse absolutely

**Any deal that will not survive one to three weeks of verification.** Urgency is the tool used to prevent checking, and it works better on someone six thousand kilometres away.

**Any structure offering you control of land you cannot lawfully hold.** Void under Indonesian law, prohibited to facilitate under Perda Bali 4/2026, and impossible to monitor from abroad.

**Anything where you cannot identify who you are actually contracting with.**

## After completion

**Appoint someone who can act** — a properly scoped power of attorney, a manager, a tax consultant with a clear instruction.

**Digitise everything** and store it somewhere reachable from another country.

**Visit within the first six months, unannounced.** What the property looks like when nobody expected you is its real condition, and it is the check that catches a manager drifting.

## The honest recommendation

**Do not buy your first Bali property remotely.**

Come, rent for a period if you can, look at two or three areas, and buy something you have stood in. The buyers who do badly here are overwhelmingly the ones who compressed that.

A second purchase, in a market you already understand, through people you have already used — that is a different proposition and it works.

## Common questions

### Can you buy property in Bali without visiting?
Yes, through a power of attorney, and it removes the checks that matter most — the street at different times, the neighbours, the building condition and the real drive times.

### What should I do if buying Bali property remotely?
Engage your own notary and an independent surveyor, commission your own photographs and video, verify every document, and hold funds with your notary against written release conditions.

### How does a power of attorney work for a remote purchase?
Your own notary drafts it, narrowly scoped to the specific transaction with an expiry date. Executed abroad it needs apostille legalisation and sworn translation before it is accepted.

### How should I pay for a property I have not seen?
Bank transfer from an account in your own name to the registered owner or a notary's client account, released against written conditions once verification is complete.

### Should a first-time buyer purchase remotely in Bali?
No. Come, look at two or three areas and buy something you have stood in. A second purchase in a market you understand is a different proposition.
