North Bali has the cheapest coastal land on the island and a rental market that has not arrived. Whether that is an opportunity depends entirely on your time horizon.

## What it is

The northern coast around **Lovina**, in **Buleleng regency**, with Singaraja as the regional centre — Bali's second city and its former colonial capital.

Black sand beaches, calm water on the Bali Sea side, dolphin trips at dawn, hot springs, waterfalls, and the Menjangan diving to the west. Quiet, unhurried, and almost entirely without the development pressure of the south.

Two and a half to three hours from Ngurah Rai airport, over the mountains. That drive is the defining commercial fact about the region.

## The market

**Land is the cheapest coastal in Bali**, by a wide margin. Beachfront positions that would be unobtainable in the south are available here at a fraction of the price, with large plots and long available lease terms.

Existing stock is limited and mostly small hotels, guesthouses and local housing. This is a land and build market.

Buleleng has its own spatial plan and the regency's approach differs from Badung's. Check zoning against Buleleng's rules.

## Rental performance

**Thin.** That is the honest position.

Visitor numbers to the north are a small fraction of the south. The guest is someone on a multi-day Bali itinerary spending a night or two, a diver heading to Menjangan, or someone specifically seeking quiet.

There is no nomad market, no long-stay foreign resident base of meaningful size, and no walkable amenity of the Canggu type. Occupancy that would be a failure in the south is normal here.

Nightly rates for a good property with a sea view can be reasonable, because supply of quality stock is very limited and competition is thin. Filling the calendar is the constraint, not the rate.

## The airport question

A **north Bali airport** has been discussed for many years and periodically re-enters the news.

It would transform the region if built. It is also a project that has been proposed, studied, shelved and revived repeatedly without breaking ground.

Treat it as a **long-horizon possibility, not a plan**. Anyone selling north Bali land primarily on the basis of an airport should be asked exactly what stage the project is at and what has actually been committed, and the answer is usually less than the pitch implies.

Buying here on the assumption of an airport is buying a speculation. Buying here because the land is cheap, the coast is beautiful and you are patient is a different and more defensible decision.

## The honest framing

North Bali is a **long-horizon land position**, not an income investment.

Buy cheaply, hold a long term, and if the region develops — through the airport, through the south's congestion pushing people north, or through the Menjangan and diving market growing — you do well eventually.

If it takes fifteen years, you have held an asset producing little while a leasehold term ran down. That argues for buying land on the longest term available, or for buying with a structure that does not decay, rather than for buying a villa and hoping to rent it.

## What to check

**Buleleng zoning** for the exact parcel, including whether it is classified agricultural.

**Water and power**, which are more constrained than the south.

**Access roads**, and whether rights of way are documented.

**Coastal setback**, on anything near the beach.

**Erosion history** on the northern coast.

**Realistic comparables from the north**, not the south. Using Canggu occupancy assumptions here will overstate a projection by a wide margin.

**The drive**, honestly. Do it, in both directions, and decide whether guests will.

## Who it suits

Patient buyers with capital they do not need working, buying land rather than income.

Owner-occupiers who want quiet, space, a beach and a view at a price the south cannot offer, and who are not relying on rental returns.

Divers and people with a specific reason to be on that coast.

## Who it does not

Anyone needing income or liquidity. The resale market is very thin and a sale can take years.

### Is North Bali a good investment?
As a long-horizon land position at the cheapest coastal prices on the island, potentially. As an income investment it is not — visitor numbers are a small fraction of the south.
