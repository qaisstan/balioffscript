Is Komodo or Raja Ampat better for diving?

Komodo is closer, cheaper and has stronger currents. Raja Ampat has the highest marine biodiversity on earth and costs twice as much to reach. Which one suits which diver.

By Kai, Bali property adviser Updated 2026-09-25 4 min read

Indonesia has two world-class dive destinations that people travel specifically for, and they are 2,000 kilometres apart.

Getting there

Komodo. 90 minutes by plane from Bali to Labuan Bajo, then a boat. One travel day.

Raja Ampat. Fly to Sorong via Jakarta, Makassar or Manado, typically 8 to 16 hours with connections, then a two-hour ferry and a boat transfer. One to two travel days each way.

The diving

Komodo. Strong currents, big fish, healthy hard coral, manta cleaning stations, and dramatic topography at sites like Batu Bolong, Castle Rock, Crystal Rock and Shotgun. Cooler water in the south, warmer in the north. It is exciting, fast diving that rewards experience.

Raja Ampat. The highest recorded marine biodiversity of anywhere on earth: more coral and fish species than any comparable area. Mantas at Manta Sandy and Magic Mountain, wobbegongs, walking sharks, pygmy seahorses, and reef systems in exceptional condition. Currents are present and generally more manageable than Komodo's, with some notable exceptions.

The honest difference: Komodo is more dramatic, Raja Ampat is richer. Divers who have done both usually say Raja Ampat is the better diving and Komodo is the better trip for the time and money.

Cost

Komodo. A week from Bali, including flights, liveaboard or land-based diving and park fees, typically 1,500 to 3,500 USD.

Raja Ampat. Ten days from Bali, including flights, permits and a homestay or resort, rarely under 2,000 USD and commonly 4,000 to 8,000.

Both charge marine park fees on top, and Raja Ampat's permit is an annual card bought on arrival.

Season

Komodo. April to October, with July to September peak. January and February are rough and many boats stop.

Raja Ampat. Roughly October to April, with mantas most reliable December to March. Some parts are divable year-round.

They are opposite seasons, which is useful: whichever half of the year you are travelling in, one of them is in season.

Non-diving value

Komodo has the dragons, Padar's viewpoint, pink beaches and Labuan Bajo, which now has good restaurants and hotels. A non-diving partner will have plenty to do.

Raja Ampat has the karst viewpoints at Piaynemo and Wayag, birds of paradise, and exceptional snorkelling. It also has very limited comfort, patchy connectivity and no restaurant scene. A non-diving partner needs to actively want that.

Snorkellers

Raja Ampat is unusually good for snorkelling, because many reefs start in a metre of water and homestay house reefs can be superb.

Komodo has excellent snorkelling at Manta Point and the shallower sites, with more current to contend with.

Which to choose

Choose Komodo if you have a week, a moderate budget, you want dragons and landscape alongside the diving, or you are diving with a mixed group.

Choose Raja Ampat if you have ten days or more, diving is the entire point of the trip, and you want to see reefs in the best condition remaining anywhere.

Do neither yet if you have fewer than 30 logged dives. Both have sites where current handling matters, and Komodo in particular is unforgiving of inexperience.

The third option nobody mentions

Alor and the Banda Sea, reached by liveaboard, sit between the two in cost and above both in remoteness. Alor has extraordinary muck and reef diving with almost no other boats, and the Banda Sea crossings in season are among the best big-animal diving in Indonesia, including hammerheads and sea snakes at Gunung Api.

These are liveaboard-only destinations for experienced divers, and they are what people do on their third or fourth Indonesian dive trip.

Planning either trip from Bali

Build in a night in Bali on each side. Both destinations involve connections that can slip, and neither is a place you want to arrive at after a missed flight with a boat leaving in the morning.

Leave at least 18 to 24 hours between your last dive and any flight, including the domestic legs, which is easy to forget when the itinerary has three flights in it.

Dive insurance that covers chamber treatment and evacuation from remote Indonesia is not optional for either of these.

Common questions

Is Raja Ampat better than Komodo?

For biodiversity and reef condition, yes. Komodo has more dramatic topography, stronger currents and much easier access from Bali.

How much does a Raja Ampat trip cost?

Rarely under 2,000 USD for ten days from Bali, and commonly 4,000 to 8,000 with a resort or liveaboard.

When is the best time to dive Komodo?

April to October, with July to September the peak. Seas are roughest in January and February.

Do you need experience to dive Komodo?

For the famous sites, yes. Currents are strong and several are advanced. There are easier sites for newer divers.

Can you do both Komodo and Raja Ampat in one trip?

Only with three weeks or more. They are 2,000 kilometres apart and have opposite peak seasons.
