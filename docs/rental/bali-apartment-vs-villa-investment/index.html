Apartment or villa: which returns more in Bali?

Apartments carry lower running costs and a licensing route that villas lost in 2026. Villas carry the higher rate.

The comparison is usually made on nightly rate, which is the least important variable.

Where each wins

VillaApartment
Nightly rateHigherLower
OccupancyMore seasonalSteadier
Running costsHigh: staff, pool, gardenLower, often shared
Entry priceHigherLower
Break-even occupancyHigherLower
Licensing route for a new foreign-owned companyClosed for villa classificationApartment hotel remains open
Exit liquidityDeeper buyer poolNarrower in most Bali locations

The licensing difference is the big one

Since 22 July 2026, the villa classification (55203) is closed to new PT PMA registration in Bali. Apartment hotel (55204) remains open, at any size.

That is not a small administrative distinction. For a foreign investor forming a new company to operate nightly rentals, it changes which asset types have a lawful operating route at all. The detail is here.

The cost difference compounds

A villa carries its own staff, pool and garden. An apartment usually shares building costs across units, which lowers the fixed base, and the fixed base is what sets break-even occupancy.

Lower fixed costs mean a lower break-even, which means more tolerance for a bad season. That resilience rarely appears in a comparison built on nightly rate.

Where villas still win

Rate and demand. A well-located, well-run villa with a pool commands a premium an apartment cannot, and Bali's guest expectation is villa-shaped.

For a buyer who will use the property personally, that matters beyond the numbers.

How to actually decide

Run both through the calculator with realistic figures for each. The property type selector adjusts the cost assumptions. Compare net yield after amortisation and break-even occupancy, not headline rates.

Then answer the question that comes first: on the specific plot or unit you are looking at, is nightly rental permitted, and does the operator hold a classification that covers it?

Neither asset type is a good investment if the answer is no.
