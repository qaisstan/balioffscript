Can British citizens buy property in Bali?

Yes, on the same terms as every foreigner: leasehold, Hak Pakai with residency, or HGB through a company, never freehold. What differs for UK buyers is the home tax side, and the assumption that a Bali leasehold works like an English one.

By Kai, Strategic Investment Adviser Updated 2026-10-08 4 min read

Yes, British citizens can buy property in Bali, on the same terms as every other foreigner. You cannot hold Hak Milik, Indonesian freehold. You can hold a leasehold, Hak Pakai if you hold an Indonesian residence permit, or HGB through your own foreign-owned company.

The trap for UK buyers is the word itself. In England, a long leasehold is a familiar, registered, mortgageable form of ownership. A Bali leasehold is none of those things, and the habits that protect you at home do not transfer.

A Bali leasehold is not an English leasehold

English long leaseholdBali leasehold (Hak Sewa)
RegisteredYes, at the Land RegistryNo, it is a contract and appears on no certificate
MortgageNormalNo bank will lend against it
Typical termOften 99 years or moreUsually 25 to 30 years
At the endStatutory rights to extend in many casesBuilding generally goes to the landowner unless the contract says otherwise
Your protectionLaw and registrationThe wording of the deed

That last row is the whole lesson. In Bali the drafting is the product: the extension clause, whether the lease binds the landowner's heirs, your right to sell or rent, and named remedies. See leasehold vs freehold in Bali and extension clauses explained.

Anything offered as freehold for foreigners is a nominee arrangement, which is void in Indonesian law and criminal in Bali since Perda 4/2026.

The UK tax side, in plain words

This is general information, not tax advice. Talk to a UK adviser who handles overseas property.

  • UK residents are taxed on overseas income and gains. Rental income from a Bali villa goes on your Self Assessment return.
  • Indonesian tax paid can usually be credited against UK tax on the same income, under the UK–Indonesia double taxation agreement. See the UK–Indonesia tax treaty.
  • Selling. A gain when you sell the lease can be taxable in the UK as well as in Indonesia, usually with credit for the Indonesian tax.
  • The rules for non-doms changed in April 2025. If you used the remittance basis before, check how the new regime treats a Bali property.
  • Inheritance. UK inheritance tax can reach overseas assets, and a Bali lease only passes cleanly if it is drafted to bind heirs. See wills and inheritance in Indonesia.

On the Indonesian side, see rental income tax and the tax when you sell.

Three UK habits that do not work here

1. Expecting a solicitor's searches to protect you. There is no conveyancing system doing the checks for you. Your own notary must verify the certificate at the land office, the seller's authority, the zoning and the permits before you pay. See the due diligence timeline.

2. Trusting the agent. The agent is almost always paid by the seller. See agents, managers and who works for you.

3. Assuming a mortgage. Bali is a cash market. UK buyers who borrow usually do it at home. See financing property in Bali.

Money and currency

Prices are often quoted in US dollars, contracts and running costs are in rupiah, and your return is in pounds. That is two currency moves, not one. Plan the transfer route before you agree a price. See paying for Bali property and currency risk.

Getting here and staying

There are no direct flights from the UK. Most routes connect once, through Singapore, the Gulf or elsewhere in Asia, so UK owners tend to visit less often and depend more on a good manager. Buying property does not give you a visa. See does buying property give you a visa.

What the numbers look like

Advertised gross yields of 8 to 15 percent usually land at 4 to 6 percent net on average stock and 7 to 9 percent on a well-bought, well-run villa, before counting the lease running down. See Bali villa rental yield and ROI and the Bali property investment guide.

Common questions

Can a British citizen buy property in Bali?

Not as freehold. A British citizen can hold a leasehold in their own name, Hak Pakai with an Indonesian residence permit, or HGB through their own foreign-owned company.

Is a Bali leasehold the same as a UK leasehold?

No. A Bali lease is a contract that is not registered, cannot be mortgaged, usually runs 25 to 30 years, and generally ends with the building going to the landowner unless the contract says otherwise.

Do I pay UK tax on Bali rental income?

If you are UK resident, yes. It goes on your Self Assessment return, and Indonesian tax paid can usually be credited under the UK–Indonesia double taxation agreement.

Can I get a UK mortgage for a Bali villa?

Not one secured on the Bali property. UK buyers who borrow usually raise the money at home, against UK property.

Are there direct flights from the UK to Bali?

No. Most routes connect once, through Singapore, the Gulf or elsewhere in Asia.
