Which areas of Bali are best to buy property and invest in?

The areas with the best rental demand and the areas with the best entry price are not the same, and the gap between them is the whole decision. Area by area: land prices, demand, the catch, and where I would look right now.

By Kai, Strategic Investment Adviser Updated 2026-10-08 9 min read

There is no single best area to buy property in Bali, because the question hides two different ones. The area with the best rental demand and the area with the best entry price are rarely the same place, and which one you want depends on your budget, your horizon and what the property has to do.

Land prices below are leasehold, per are (100 square metres), for the term. They move fast and vary street by street. Treat them as a starting point, not a valuation. See Bali land prices, area by area.

Canggu, Berawa and Batu Bolong: the strongest rental demand

The most liquid market on the island. Canggu alone accounted for roughly a third of transactions in Q3 2025. Berawa land runs around USD 82,500 per are.

Berawa has the highest and most consistent occupancy in Bali, a broad guest mix, walkable amenities, a deep repeat market and the deepest resale pool. The corridor is built out, so the return is largely priced in, and at a given budget what you give up is lease term.

Batu Bolong is the most walkable strip and the highest rate per bedroom for small properties. Digital nomads fill the quiet months. Small plots, short available terms, heavy traffic.

The catch: supply grew fast and generic stock now competes on price. Traffic is a real constraint on guest experience. See is Canggu oversupplied, Berawa vs Batu Bolong and Canggu and Berawa property.

Pererenan and Cemagi: where the value moved

The corridor that has already made the move from emerging to established. Rental demand is now genuinely good rather than promising, land is meaningfully cheaper than Berawa at around USD 55,000 to 75,000 per are, and lease terms at a given budget are about a decade longer. For most buyers under USD 400,000, this is where the arithmetic points. See Pererenan and Cemagi property.

Seminyak, Umalas and Kerobokan: mature and steady

Seminyak and Petitenget are mature, liquid and steady: older, higher-spending guests, shorter stays, a shallower low season and established infrastructure. Land is near its ceiling and appreciation is modest, but cash flow is the most stable and resale the easiest. Little land, ageing stock.

Umalas and Kerobokan are value plays, not growth plays: more house, land and term for the money, suited to long-term rental rather than nightly letting. See Seminyak property and Umalas property.

Uluwatu and the Bukit: the highest rates, the strictest checks

Land is roughly 40 percent cheaper than Canggu: the clifftop around USD 40,000 to 60,000 per are, inland USD 25,000 to 40,000. Nightly rates are the highest on the island, driven by view scarcity and a loyal surf market with high repeat and direct bookings.

Pecatu and Ungasan, the interior Bukit, hold the largest supply of developable land left in south Bali, with generous plots and long terms near the breaks.

The catch is water and zoning. On 21 July 2025, 48 buildings at Bingin were demolished: they stood on state land inside a green zone, and the Governor said none had permits. This is the area where zoning and permit checks matter most, not least, and the water question needs an answer in writing. See Uluwatu and Bukit property and Bukit vs Canggu.

Seseh, Kedungu and the Tabanan coast: the frontier

Seseh is one corridor north of Pererenan and correspondingly earlier: cheaper land, longer terms, thinner and more seasonal demand, and a regency boundary to check.

Kedungu, Nyanyi and the Tanah Lot corridor are 30 to 50 percent cheaper and the clearest appreciation frontier, with the Gilimanuk to Mengwi toll road targeted for 2027 to 2028. The rental market is several years behind, and Tabanan sits inside the construction moratorium, so zoning checks are not optional. See Seseh property, Kedungu and Nyanyi and the Tabanan west coast.

Sanur and Ubud: their own logic

Sanur has the steadiest year-round occupancy on the island at lower peak rates, with families and older travellers, calm water, and the best value per square metre in an established area. Unexciting and reliable. See Sanur property.

Ubud is a different market entirely: wellness, retreats, couples and Europeans on long stays, booked through programmes and direct rather than platforms. Occupancy is above 70 percent in the right pockets, rates are lower and less seasonal, and land is cheaper than the coast. See Ubud property and Canggu vs Seminyak vs Ubud.

North and East Bali: the longest horizon

The lowest entry and the thinnest rental demand. The northern case rests on the planned North Bali airport: a National Strategic Project under Perpres No. 12/2025, sited at Sumber Klampok in Buleleng, with planned capacity of up to 50 million passengers. As of March 2026, construction had not begun, and government targets run from a first phase around 2027 to 2028 to completion near 2030. Sellers in the north already price land as if it is coming. Pay for what is there today and treat the airport as a possible bonus. See North Bali and Lovina, Amed and Candidasa and East Bali and Sidemen.

The rule that decides most purchases

Under about USD 300,000, term beats postcode.

At USD 250,000 in Berawa you might get a three-bedroom with fifteen to eighteen years left. The same money in Pererenan gets a similar house with twenty-five to thirty. Berawa's higher occupancy and rate rarely recover a ten-year gap in term. Above roughly USD 500,000 you can buy both a long term and a prime position, and the depth of the resale market starts to matter more. See what each budget buys.

What drives returns more than the area

Walkability to something. A view. Plot size against KDB, and therefore pool and garden. The remaining term and the extension clause. Photography. Management. A licence that is in order.

Those seven decide more of your outcome than the choice between two neighbouring corridors. An excellent property in Pererenan beats a mediocre one in Berawa every time, and the gap between good and bad management on one street is wider than the gap between two areas. Once vacancy, management and maintenance come off, real yields are often closer between areas than the marketing suggests. See Bali villa rental yield and ROI.

The three questions that decide it

How long are you holding? Under five years, liquidity matters more than price, which points to Canggu and Seminyak. Over ten, entry price matters more, which points north and west.

Do you need income from year one? If yes, buy where guests already are. Buying ahead of demand is a capital strategy, not an income one.

Will you live in it? If yes, drive to the school, the hospital and the airport at the worst hour of the day before you decide. Bali distances are measured in minutes, not kilometres.

What does not decide it: the area's growth story. Every area has one, told with equal confidence about places that tripled and places that went nowhere. Verify the plot, the title, the zoning and the demand that exists today.

Where I would look right now

For income at a mid-range budget: Pererenan and Cemagi, on the longest term available.

For a build with room to do it properly: Pecatu, Ungasan or Seseh, with the water and zoning work done first.

For reliability and a liquid exit: Berawa or Seminyak, but only on a long lease.

For a view premium: the Bukit, with the water question answered in writing.

Common questions

What is the best area to buy property in Bali in 2026?

Pererenan and Cemagi for income at a mid-range budget, the interior Bukit for a build, Berawa or Seminyak for reliability and resale. The right answer depends on budget, horizon and whether you will live in it.

Where is the best place to invest in Bali?

For most investors under USD 400,000, Pererenan and Cemagi, because demand is established and the same money buys a much longer lease than in Berawa. Above USD 500,000, prime Berawa, Seminyak or clifftop Uluwatu on a long term.

Is Canggu still a good investment?

Berawa and Batu Bolong still have the strongest occupancy in Bali, but land prices mean the return is largely priced in and lease terms at a given budget are short. It works on a long lease and poorly on a short one.

Where is the cheapest place to buy property in Bali?

Tabanan and the west coast, followed by inland Gianyar, North Bali and the east. All carry the same trade-off: lower price, thinner rental demand and more zoning constraints.

Which area of Bali has the best rental yield?

Headline nightly rates are highest in Uluwatu and Canggu, and occupancy is steadiest in Sanur and walkable Canggu. After vacancy, management and maintenance, yields are often closer between areas than the marketing suggests.

Should you buy where you would want to live?

Only if you intend to live there. The area that suits a resident and the area that fills a booking calendar are often different places, and buying for both usually satisfies neither.

Does the area matter more than the property itself?

No. Walkability, view, plot size, remaining term, photography, management and licensing decide more of the outcome than the choice between two neighbouring areas.
