Where should you buy around Ubud?

Central Ubud is congested and built out. The value and the views are in the villages around it, and each one behaves differently.

By Kai, Bali property adviser Updated 2026-09-21 4 min read

Ubud is a market with a different guest, a different booking channel and a different calendar from coastal Bali. It is also, in its centre, congested and effectively built out. The opportunity is in the villages around it.

Penestanan

A ridge immediately west of central Ubud, reached by a famous set of steps and a road around. Rice fields, artists' studios, a long-established foreign community, and a walkable set of cafes and yoga studios.

Why it works. Close enough to Ubud centre to walk or ride in minutes, far enough to be quiet. The walkability is genuine, which in Bali is scarce. The community is established rather than transient.

What it costs. Land is among the more expensive around Ubud, and plots are often small and awkwardly shaped because of the terrain and the existing development pattern.

Guest profile. Long-stay Europeans, yoga and wellness visitors, creatives. Longer average stays than the coast and less price sensitivity at the top end.

Sayan and the Ayung valley

West of Penestanan, where the land falls away into the Ayung river gorge.

Why it works. The valley views are the best in the Ubud area and among the best in Bali. Several of the island's most prestigious resorts are here, which anchors the location's reputation and the price.

What it costs. The most expensive land around Ubud, and rightly so. Valley-edge parcels with a protected outlook are genuinely scarce.

What to check. Slope stability and the cost of building on it. Retaining and foundations on a gorge-edge plot are a large part of the budget. And river setback — the sempadan sungai applies and is strictly enforced.

Tegallalang

North of Ubud, known for the rice terraces and now a long strip of swings, cafes and viewpoints along the main road.

Why it works. Land is considerably cheaper than Penestanan or Sayan, plots are larger, and the rice terrace views are real. There is genuine development land.

What it costs you. Fifteen to thirty minutes to Ubud centre depending on traffic, which on that road can be bad. No walkability. And the main road strip is busy and increasingly commercial, which is not what a guest booking a rice-field retreat expects.

Where the value is. Off the main road, on the quieter lanes with terrace views. The difference between a parcel on the strip and one four hundred metres back is substantial in both price and guest experience.

The zoning problem, which is the big one here

The Ubud area contains extensive green and agricultural zoning, far more than coastal Badung.

Rice terraces are agricultural land. Much of what looks like ordinary villa land around Ubud sits in a zone where commercial accommodation is not permitted, and the fact that a neighbour is operating is not a licence.

This is the single most common problem in Ubud-area purchases and it is checkable in a week. Get the spatial plan for the exact parcel from Gianyar regency, and confirm your intended use is permitted before anything else.

The Ubud market, generally

Different guests. Wellness, retreats, yoga teacher trainings, couples and long-stay Europeans. Less Australian family demand than the coast.

Different channels. A substantial share of Ubud bookings come through retreat programmes and direct relationships rather than through Airbnb and Booking.com. An owner who only lists on platforms is missing part of the market.

Different calendar. Less driven by school holidays, more spread across the year, with a strong May-to-September European season.

Longer stays. Which reduces turnover costs and management burden meaningfully.

Lower land prices than the coastal corridor for equivalent quality, and generally longer available lease terms.

What to check here specifically

Zoning for the exact parcel, first and hardest. River setback on anything near a gorge or waterway. Slope stability and access on valley plots. Realistic drive times to central Ubud at peak, not off-peak. Water supply, which is better than the Bukit but should still be confirmed. And whether the view is over land somebody else owns and could build on.

Common questions

Where is the best place to buy near Ubud?

Penestanan for walkability and an established community, Sayan for valley views at the highest prices, Tegallalang for cheaper land and larger plots away from the main road strip.

What is the main risk buying around Ubud?

Zoning. The area has extensive green and agricultural classification, and commercial accommodation is not permitted in those zones regardless of what neighbours are doing.

Is Ubud good for rental income?

Yes, with a different profile: wellness and retreat driven, longer stays, a strong European season, and a booking channel mix that runs substantially through programmes and direct rather than platforms.

Is land cheaper in Ubud than Canggu?

Generally yes, for equivalent quality, with longer available lease terms. Sayan valley-edge parcels are the exception and are among the most expensive in Bali.

How far is Tegallalang from Ubud centre?

Fifteen to thirty minutes depending on traffic, which on that road can be significant. There is no walkability, which matters to the guest type Ubud attracts.
