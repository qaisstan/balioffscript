How do you check access rights to Bali land?

A road you drove down to view the land may belong to a neighbour. If your access is not documented, it can close.

By Kai, Bali property adviser Updated 2026-09-21 4 min read

You drive down a lane, view a beautiful parcel, and buy it. Two years later the neighbour whose land the lane crosses sells, and the new owner puts up a gate.

This happens in Bali, and the check that prevents it takes an afternoon.

Why it happens here so often

Much of Bali's rural and semi-rural land was accessed informally for generations. A family let a neighbour cross their land, the arrangement became habit, and nobody documented anything because within a village nobody needed to.

Then foreigners started buying, land started changing hands, and arrangements that ran on relationships began running on paperwork that did not exist.

The result: a great many Bali parcels are reached by a route that the owner has no documented right to use.

What proper access looks like

A public road frontage. The parcel adjoins a road that is genuinely public, maintained by the regency or the village as public infrastructure. This is the strongest position and the simplest.

A registered right of way. An easement over the neighbouring land, recorded and appearing on the relevant certificates. Enforceable against whoever owns that land in future.

A documented agreement with the landowner whose land you cross, binding on their heirs and successors, with a defined term at least as long as your own interest.

Land in common ownership forming an access strip, where a development has retained a road parcel.

What is not proper access

"Everyone uses it." Custom is not a right you can enforce against a new owner.

A verbal agreement with the current neighbour, however genuine.

A road that looks public and is not. This is the common one. Surfaced lanes in Bali are frequently on private land, built by whoever built there first.

An arrangement documented only between the previous owner and the neighbour, which may not transfer to you.

How to check it

Ask your notary specifically, as a separate instruction. Access is not always part of a standard title check and you should name it.

Have them establish:

The status of every metre of the route from the public road to the parcel boundary. Which land does it cross, who owns it, and what documents it.

Whether the parcel has any public road frontage at all.

Whether any right of way is recorded, and against which certificate.

The width of the documented access, because a right of way wide enough for a scooter is not wide enough for a concrete truck or a guest with a rental car.

Whether it is for all purposes, including construction traffic and commercial use, or limited.

Walk it, with the certificate

Take the certificate and the cadastral plan and walk from the public road to the boundary. Compare what is drawn with what is there.

Ask the neighbours whose land you cross. They will tell you what the arrangement is, and if they seem surprised that anyone is asking, that is information.

Ask the banjar. The customary village knows who has crossed whose land for how long, who has fallen out with whom, and whether there is a dispute nobody has mentioned. This is free and it is the check that surfaces what documents cannot.

If access is undocumented

It is fixable and it has to be fixed before you buy, not after.

Once you own the land, your negotiating position with the neighbour is transformed — they know you cannot reach your property without them. A right of way negotiated while you can still walk away costs a fraction of one negotiated afterwards.

The mechanisms: a registered easement, a long-term access agreement binding successors, or purchasing a strip of land forming the access.

Make completion conditional on it. Your own notary holds the funds, and they are released when documented access exists.

The splitting connection

If you may want to divide the parcel later, each resulting piece needs its own legal access. That usually means surrendering a strip for an access road, which on a deep narrow plot can take five to eight percent of your buildable land.

Have the geometry drawn before you assume a split is clean.

What it costs you if you get it wrong

A parcel with no documented access is close to unsaleable to any buyer doing proper due diligence, and it is not buildable in practice — a contractor cannot bring materials across land they have no right to cross.

That is a total loss of value on an asset that looked fine at the viewing.

Common questions

How do I check access rights to Bali land?

Instruct your notary specifically on access, establish the ownership of every metre of the route from the public road, check whether any right of way is recorded, and walk it with the certificate.

Is a road that looks public actually public in Bali?

Frequently not. Surfaced lanes are often on private land, built by whoever built there first, and use by others is custom rather than a right.

What happens if my access is not documented?

A future owner of the land you cross can close it. A parcel without documented access is effectively unbuildable and close to unsaleable.

How wide should a documented right of way be?

Wide enough for construction traffic and guest vehicles, not just a scooter. Check the stated width and whether it covers commercial use as well as residential.

Can I fix access after buying?

You can try, and it costs far more, because the neighbour knows you cannot reach your property without them. Make completion conditional on documented access instead.
