Living in Bali: the honest version

Whether Bali works as a place to live, and where the market goes next.

Bali is a genuinely good place to live for some people and a slow disappointment for others, and the difference is rarely what the brochures discuss. These pages cover what actually changes when you move here, the infrastructure and policy shifts pulling capital in, and where the market is heading next as Bali prices itself out of reach for some buyers. Written the same way as the rest of the site: specific, dated, and unwilling to sell you a version of the island that does not exist.

Every answer in this section

  • Is Bali actually a good place to live?

    For some people, genuinely. The honest answer depends on things nobody puts in the brochure.

  • Should you move to Bali?

    Ask what you will actually do here first. The visa follows the activity, and most disappointment starts with that order reversed.

  • Is Bali becoming the next Singapore or Dubai?

    The capital is arriving. The legal framework it usually requires is not, and that gap is the whole story.

  • What does it actually cost to live in Bali?

    What drives the number, what people underestimate, and why the figures you see online are usually somebody else's life.

  • Where should you actually live in Bali?

    Where to live and where to invest are different questions with different answers.

  • Bali, Thailand or Portugal for property?

    Only one of the three lets a foreigner own anything outright. That is the decision, not the yield.

  • How much does it cost to rent in Bali?

    One-bedroom villas start around IDR 17.5 million a month in Canggu. The bigger shock is that most landlords want a year up front.

  • Is Bali safe?

    Violent crime is not the risk. The roads are, by a wide margin, and almost nobody prepares for that.

  • What is healthcare in Bali actually like?

    Routine care is fine and affordable. Serious care is where people get financially destroyed, and the number is bigger than you think.

  • What do international schools in Bali cost?

    From around USD 3,000 to over USD 30,000 a year. And the headline tuition is not the number you actually pay.

  • What is it actually like to work remotely from Bali?

    The internet is better than the reputation. The legal position is worse than most people assume.

  • Do you need a licence to drive a scooter in Bali?

    Yes. And the reason it matters is not the police fine, it is what happens to your insurance claim.

  • What is Bali actually like in rainy season?

    Roughly November to March. It is not the constant downpour people fear, and it is not the postcard either.

  • What is it like moving to Bali with children?

    Families live in a completely different Bali to everyone else, and it costs roughly three times as much.

  • Can a foreigner open a bank account in Indonesia?

    With a KITAS it is straightforward. Without one it is mostly not possible, and the workarounds sold to tourists carry real risk.

  • Can you bring a pet to Bali?

    Bali is a rabies control zone, and the honest answer is that bringing a dog or cat in is restricted and difficult, not simply expensive.

  • Should you ship your belongings to Bali?

    Sea freight takes months and attracts duty. For most people the honest answer is to bring two suitcases and buy the rest here.

  • How much is electricity in Bali?

    Air conditioning and the pool pump are the bill. A three-bedroom villa running hard costs far more than owners budget from a residential baseline.

  • Is the internet good in Bali?

    Fibre in the main expat areas is genuinely fine for remote work. Outside them it is not, and the gap is sharper than people expect.

  • How do you get an Indonesian driving licence?

    An IDP endorsed for motorcycles covers a visit. If you live here, the local SIM removes the insurance argument entirely.

  • When is the best time to visit Bali?

    Dry season runs April to October and wet season November to March. Which is right depends on whether you are here to surf, dive, save money, avoid crowds or inspect a plot.

  • How bad is Bali traffic?

    Distances here are measured in minutes, not kilometres, and the two diverge badly. It should change where you buy.

  • How risky are earthquakes and volcanoes in Bali?

    Bali sits on an active margin. It rarely affects daily life and it should affect how you build, insure and choose a location.

  • Do you need to speak Indonesian in Bali?

    You can live here on English alone. You will pay more, understand less, and stay outside most of what is actually happening.

  • Where do you work from in Bali?

    Canggu has the density, Ubud the quiet, Sanur the people who actually want to work. All three beat a villa connection you cannot rely on.

  • What should you know about Balinese culture?

    Ceremony is not a tourist attraction here, it is the calendar. Understanding that changes how you build, hire and live.

  • How bad are Bali's water and waste problems?

    Groundwater depletion and waste management are the island's real constraints, and both are becoming regulatory risks for property owners.

  • What is moving to Bali actually like?

    The first three months are the holiday. Months four to twelve are the ones that decide whether you stay.

  • What health insurance do you need in Bali?

    Good private care exists in the south, and serious cases leave the island. Evacuation cover is the difference between an inconvenience and a catastrophe.

  • How do you send money out of Indonesia?

    Transfers out attract more scrutiny than transfers in. The constraint is documentation rather than permission, and planning it saves both time and spread.

  • What is it like having a baby in Bali?

    Good private maternity care exists in the south. The paperwork after the birth is where foreign parents get caught.

  • Can a foreigner buy a car in Indonesia?

    Registration needs a KITAS or a local arrangement, and the arrangement people use to avoid that is the same pattern that fails elsewhere.

  • Where do you buy furniture in Bali?

    Locally made solid timber costs a fraction of imported equivalents and suits the climate better. Knowing where to go is most of it.

  • How does childcare work in Bali?

    Domestic help is affordable in a way it is not at home, and employing someone carries the full Indonesian labour framework whether you realise it or not.

  • What do you need to do before leaving Bali?

    Obligations do not leave with you. A permit left open, a company still filing and a property with no one authorised to act all become expensive later.

  • How do phone and data plans work in Indonesia?

    Cheap, fast where there is coverage, and registration requires your passport. The catch is what happens when you leave.

  • Can you homeschool in Bali?

    A large community does it, the legal position for foreign children runs through immigration rather than education, and the practical question is accreditation.

  • How do you build a life in Bali?

    The foreign community turns over constantly, and the relationship that matters most commercially is the one with your neighbours.

  • What mental health support exists in Bali?

    Good support exists and is thinner than at home. Knowing where it is before you need it is the useful step.

  • Where do you shop for food in Bali?

    Local produce is cheap, imported goods are not, and the gap between the two is most of the difference in anyone's cost of living here.

  • What are gyms and fitness like in Bali?

    An unusually strong fitness scene for a place this size, and it is a genuine reason people stay and a genuine amenity for a villa.

  • What is there for children to do in Bali?

    More than people expect, concentrated in a few areas, and it is what decides where families with children actually live.
