How should you design outdoor space in a Bali villa?

Guests spend most of their time outside. The outdoor space is where the rate is set and where most villas underinvest.

By Kai, Bali property adviser Updated 2026-09-21 4 min read

A guest in a Bali villa spends most of their waking time outside. The indoor space is where they sleep. Designing as though the reverse were true is the most common error in Bali villa design and it shows up directly in the rate.

Why outdoor space sets the rate

It is the cover image. The pool with the villa behind it is what sells the booking, and everything around the pool is in that frame.

It is what guests photograph and post, which is free marketing.

It is what they remember, and what appears in reviews.

It is what differentiates you from fifty near-identical villas with the same bedroom count. Indoor space is broadly similar across the market; outdoor space is not.

The elements that matter

The pool, and its relationship to everything else. Not just its size. Where it sits relative to the living space, where the sun falls on it through the day, what you see from it, and what is in the frame behind it.

A generous pool with a poor relationship to the building photographs worse than a modest one placed well.

A covered outdoor living area. This is the most underprovided element in Bali villas and the most used. Somewhere to sit in shade, in rain, in the evening, with enough room to eat. Guests use it more than the interior.

An outdoor dining table that a group can actually sit around.

Sun loungers in the right number for the bedroom count, in a position where the sun is.

Shade, in more than one place, because a villa with no shade is unusable from eleven until three.

An outdoor shower, which photographs extremely well, costs very little, and is genuinely used near a beach.

Lighting. Uplighting on trees, path lighting, pool lighting. The cheapest transformation available and the thing most villas have none of. Evening photographs sell bookings that daytime ones do not.

Garden, which is a large part of what a Bali villa is selling and which appears in every frame.

The constraints

KDB, the building coverage ratio, caps the proportion of the plot the building footprint may occupy — commonly forty to sixty percent in residential zones.

That constraint is the reason outdoor space exists, and it should be treated as an opportunity rather than a limit. The open area it leaves is your pool, garden and terrace, which is what the property sells on.

Setbacks — road, boundary, river, coastal — come off before that, and they reduce usable area further. Have them drawn on a site plan before designing.

Plot size. On a small central Canggu plot, fitting four bedrooms and a pool that photographs well is genuinely difficult, and the common outcome is a compromised pool. A three-bedroom with a generous pool out-earns a cramped four-bedroom and costs less to build.

Design mistakes that cost money

Pool in the wrong place. Shaded for most of the day, or with a wall in the frame, or positioned so the best photograph is impossible.

No covered outdoor area, which means the space is unusable in rain and at midday.

Planting that drops into the pool, which is a permanent cleaning cost decided at landscaping.

Loungers with no sun, or no loungers.

Outdoor furniture that degrades in two years because the fabric had no UV or mould resistance.

Noisy plant. Aircon condensers and pool pumps sited where they are heard from the terrace or seen in photographs.

No drainage on terraces, which in Bali rain produces standing water and eventually damage.

Nowhere to dry anything. Towels, swimwear, wetsuits. A drying area out of sight is a small thing guests notice.

The surf detail

If you are near a break, board storage and an outdoor rinse and drying area are worth providing and almost no villa does.

Surf guests are loyal, repeat heavily and book direct at the highest rate of any segment. Providing for them costs almost nothing and it appears in reviews.

For families

Pool safety matters and is a genuine consideration for family bookings. Shallow areas, clear edges, and a view of the pool from the living space so a parent can see it.

Whether to fence is a judgement — it affects photographs and some families specifically look for it. Stating the position clearly in the listing is better than leaving it ambiguous.

Shade for children, and somewhere for them that is not the pool.

Common questions

What outdoor features matter most in a Bali villa?

The pool and its relationship to the living space, a covered outdoor living area, shade, lighting and the garden. Guests spend most of their waking time outside.

Is a bigger pool better than another bedroom?

In most Bali locations, yes. A three-bedroom with a generous pool out-earns a cramped four-bedroom, costs less to build and photographs considerably better.

What is the most underprovided element in Bali villas?

A covered outdoor living area with enough room to sit and eat. Guests use it more than the interior and most villas do not provide it properly.

Does outdoor lighting affect bookings?

Yes. Evening photographs perform disproportionately well and most listings have none, while uplighting and path lighting cost very little to install.

How does KDB affect outdoor space?

It caps the building footprint, commonly at forty to sixty percent of the plot in residential zones, and the open area it leaves is your pool, garden and terrace — which is what the property sells on.
