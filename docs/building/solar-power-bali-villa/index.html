Is solar worth it for a Bali villa?

The sunshine is excellent and the electricity bill is large, which makes the arithmetic better here than in most places owners come from.

By Kai, Bali property adviser Updated 2026-09-06 3 min read

On this page

  1. Why the load profile suits it
  2. Cost and sizing
  3. Metering and export
  4. Practical constraints
  5. When it does not make sense
  6. Common questions

Bali sits near the equator with strong, consistent irradiance year round. Combined with an electricity bill driven by air conditioning and pool plant, that makes solar genuinely worth modelling.

Why the load profile suits it

Solar generates during the day. A villa's daytime load is exactly what solar is good at:

  • Pool pump, which runs for hours every day
  • Air conditioning, heaviest in the afternoon heat
  • Water pumps and heating
  • General household load

That daytime alignment is the reason the economics work better here than for a house whose consumption is mostly in the evening.

Cost and sizing

System cost scales with capacity. A villa system sized to cover a meaningful share of daytime load is a five-figure investment in USD, with the exact number depending on panels, inverter, roof suitability and whether you add batteries.

Batteries roughly double the cost and are what let you ride through outages. Without them, a grid-tied system stops when the grid does, which surprises owners who bought solar partly for resilience.

Given that short outages are routine here, the resilience argument is worth weighing rather than dismissing.

Metering and export

Whether and how you are credited for exported power is set by regulation and has changed. Do not size a system on the assumption that generous export credit exists.

The safer design is one that covers your own daytime consumption well rather than one built to export surplus. Confirm the current rules before committing.

Practical constraints

Roof. Orientation, pitch, shading from trees and neighbouring buildings, and structural capacity. Traditional alang-alang thatch roofs are unsuitable, which affects a good number of villas here.

Salt air. Coastal installations need corrosion-resistant mounting and more frequent inspection.

Dust and ash. Panels need cleaning. Volcanic ash events are infrequent but they matter, and dry season dust reduces output more than owners expect.

Installer quality. This is the variable that decides whether the system performs for fifteen years or fails in three. Ask for installed references you can visit, and confirm warranty terms on panels, inverter and workmanship separately.

When it does not make sense

A villa with a low electricity bill, a heavily shaded or unsuitable roof, or a short intended holding period will struggle to justify it.

It also makes less sense on a property you intend to sell soon, because the local resale market does not reliably price solar into the sale.

Common questions

Is solar worth it in Bali?

For a villa with a high, steady daytime load such as a pool pump and air conditioning, the economics are generally favourable given the strong year-round irradiance.

How much does solar cost for a villa in Bali?

A meaningful villa system is a five-figure USD investment, roughly doubling if you add battery storage.

Does solar work during Bali's rainy season?

Output drops on heavily overcast days but does not stop. Annual generation still benefits from consistent equatorial sun.

Will solar keep my villa running during a power cut?

Only with batteries. A grid-tied system without storage shuts down when the grid does, for safety.

Can I sell excess solar power back to PLN in Indonesia?

Export arrangements are set by regulation and have changed. Size the system around your own consumption rather than relying on export credit, and confirm the current rules.
