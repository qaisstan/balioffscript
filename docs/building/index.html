Bali zoning, PBG permits and what you can build

Zone colours, permits, the moratorium, and what it costs to build.

Zone colour tells you whether you may build. KDB and KLB tell you how much — and a green-zone plot with a 10% footprint cap can be entirely legal and still useless as a rental business. PBG is the building approval and SLF confirms the finished building is fit for use; neither is a formality, and timelines run from 3 to 6 months in Denpasar to 10 to 12 months in Badung for an existing unpermitted structure. Setbacks come off before any of it.

Every answer in this section

  • What do Bali's zoning colours mean, and how do you check properly?

    Pink lets you rent. Yellow does not. Green gets demolished. Check it yourself before you pay anything.

  • What happens if you buy a villa built without a PBG?

    No licence, no legal rental, and exposure to sealing or demolition. It is more common than the market admits.

  • What are KDB and KLB, and why do they decide your project?

    Zoning tells you if you can build. KDB and KLB tell you how much. A plot can be perfectly legal and still economically pointless.

  • What are the real risks of buying off-plan in Bali?

    You are paying for something that does not exist yet, on permits that may not exist either. Here is what to verify before the first instalment.

  • How long does it take to get a PBG on empty land in Bali?

    Up to six months from bare land to building approval, and the regency you are in changes the answer by months.

  • Can you legalise a Bali villa that was built without permits?

    Often yes, through retroactive PBG and SLF. It takes eight months to a year and it is not guaranteed.
